I22

"Racehorses and sporting dogs live better than many people"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

expensive care is not the same as a good life for the animal. Racehorses and greyhounds are well fed and looked after while they earn, because they are investments; when they get injured or stop winning, the care ends. In Australia, the Coalition for the Protection of Racehorses recorded 168 horses killed on racetracks in the 2022/23 season, one every two days. And comparing them with human poverty justifies nothing: poverty is an injustice to fix, not a yardstick for what may be done to animals.

Short answer

The expensive care lasts while the animal earns: it's an investment. On the track, the risks are real: in Australia, a group that monitors the industry recorded 168 horses killed in races in a single season, one every two days. When they get injured or stop winning, many are discarded. And the comparison with human poverty justifies nothing: people in poverty suffer an injustice that should be corrected, not a measure of what may be done to animals.

For children

A racehorse gets good food and a doctor while it runs fast. But it didn't choose to race, it can get badly hurt on the track, and when it stops winning there's often no one left to look after it. Being treated well only while you're useful isn't the same as being loved.

Ask a question back

"If the care is out of love for the animal, what happens to it when it stops winning?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"A racehorse lives better than plenty of people: vets, good food, a clean stable. Nobody mistreats an animal worth millions."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Sporting horses and dogs get high-quality food, veterinary care and shelter.
  2. P2Many humans don't get that level of care.
  3. P3Whoever gets that care has a good life.
  4. CSo using animals in sport does them no harm; they live better than many people.

P1 is true while the animal earns. So is P2. P3 fails: the care serves performance, and the conclusion ignores the risks, the injuries and what happens after the career.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3, and the comparison in P2 is irrelevant to the conclusion.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument only looks at the period when the animal is useful:

    StageWhat the animal lives through
    BreedingSelective breeding for speed; many animals are born and few get to race
    CareerIntense training, transport, competition, risk of fractures and other injuries
    Serious injuryOften euthanasia on the track itself
    End of careerSome are adopted; others are sold, used for breeding or slaughtered
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Care is proportional to value.

    A valuable animal is well cared for for the same reason a racing car is well maintained: it earns. The care doesn't come from recognizing the animal's interests, and it ends when the earnings end.

    2. Racing kills.

    The Coalition for the Protection of Racehorses, which monitors Australian tracks in the absence of published official data, recorded 168 horse deaths in races in the 2022/23 season, the highest since the count began in 2014. The figures don't include deaths in training or the fate of retired horses.

    3. Governments recognize the problem.

    New Zealand passed, with support from several parties, the end of greyhound racing, citing animal welfare; the last commercial race was held in July 2026.

    4. The comparison with humans is irrelevant.

    That many people live in poverty is an injustice to be fought, not a standard for what may be done to others. "Others have it worse" has never justified a harm (J16).

    5. The animal didn't choose.

    Even a well-treated animal was bred, trained and put at risk for other people's profit and entertainment.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Coalition for the Protection of Racehorses168 horses killed on Australian racetracks in the 2022/23 season (August 1 to July 31), one every two daysAnimal advocacy group; the count exists because the industry doesn't publish official data

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: the industry has welfare rules and adoption programs

    The most serious version acknowledges the risks and points out that there are rules on whip use, veterinary checks and programs to rehabilitate and rehome retired animals, which improve every year.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Improving isn't making acceptable.

    The rules reduce part of the harm, but the activity still requires animals to take risks for entertainment and betting.

    The programs don't reach all of them.

    The number of animals bred to compete exceeds the capacity for adoption.

    The question remains.

    Why breed animals to run that risk, if the goal is entertainment and betting?

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question isn't whether the animal eats well while it races, but whether it's fair to breed it, risk its life and discard it for entertainment and profit. Good care during use doesn't change the fact that it is being used.

Logical

Irrelevant comparison

Epistemological

Contradictions

Says the animal is treated like royalty.

And accepts that it's put down on the track when injured.

Uses human poverty as an argument.

Without proposing anything to fix it.

Would reject the same with people.

A well-paid athlete discarded after an injury would be a victim of abuse.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that many sporting horses and dogs get high-quality care, and that many people who work with them like the animals. It's also true that some people live in worse conditions, and that this is unacceptable.

But

But the two facts don't lead to the conclusion: the care depends on earnings, the risk is real, and human poverty calls for justice for people, not a license to use animals.
