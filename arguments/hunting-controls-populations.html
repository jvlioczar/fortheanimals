H11

"Hunting controls populations"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

hunting often helps cause the imbalance it claims to correct: predators were eliminated, species were introduced, and populations are managed so there are animals to hunt. There are real cases of invasive species, such as wild boar in Brazil, where control is authorized by law. But that's managing a human-made problem, not a justification for hunting as recreation. And there are non-lethal methods, such as fertility control and barriers.

Short answer

The imbalance was often caused by humans: predators eliminated, species introduced, populations kept up for hunting. Wild boar in Brazil, for example, is an invasive alien species, and its control has been authorized by the federal environment agency since 2013. But that's managing a problem we created, not hunting for recreation. There are also non-lethal methods, such as contraception, barriers and reintroducing predators, which should be studied and preferred where possible.

For children

Sometimes people take animals to places where they didn't live before, or kill the animals that used to eat them. Then they multiply too much. That's a problem people created. To fix it, we can use ways that don't kill, like fences and medicines that prevent babies, and any control should be done carefully, not for fun.

Ask a question back

"If the problem was created by humans, does the solution have to be killing, or can it be fixing the cause?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Wild boars are a pest. Without hunting, deer starve."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1In some ecosystems, certain animal populations grow beyond what the environment can support.
  2. P2This causes starvation, disease and damage to vegetation and other species.
  3. P3Hunting reduces these populations.
  4. CSo hunting is necessary for ecological balance.

P1 and P2 are true in some cases. So is P3. The problem lies in the conclusion: the imbalance was often caused by humans, and there are alternatives.

The argument is valid for specific management cases. It fails as a general justification for hunting.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument mixes up different situations:

    SituationOrigin of the problemWhat to do
    An invasive species introduced by humans, such as wild boarHuman actionManagement with effective, less cruel methods; preventing new introductions
    Overpopulation after predators were eliminatedHuman actionReintroducing predators, contraception, barriers
    Sport or recreational huntingDoesn't solve a problemNot justified by population control
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The imbalance is often human-made.

    Eliminating predators, introducing species and artificial feeding create overpopulation. Hunting treats the symptom, and sometimes sustains it.

    2. Hunting can make the problem worse.

    In some species, killing individuals stimulates reproduction among those left or scatters groups, spreading the problem.

    3. The wild boar case is a management case.

    In Brazil, a 2013 regulation by the federal environment agency (Ibama) authorized population control of wild boar, considered an invasive alien species. That's different from hunting for sport, and the control itself should seek the least cruel methods.

    4. There are non-lethal methods.

    Contraception, barriers, habitat management and reintroducing predators are studied and used in various places.

    5. The argument doesn't justify eating meat.

    Almost all meat comes from farming, not hunting. Controlling an invasive species says nothing about cattle, pigs and chickens.

    6. Protecting what, exactly?

    Programs to exterminate invasive species usually aim to preserve a species composition considered "original", not to reduce animal suffering. If the aim were the animals' wellbeing, many of these programs would look different. Valuing being "native" more than the life of someone who feels is a criterion we would never accept among humans.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: non-lethal methods are expensive and slow

    The most serious version grants that non-lethal methods exist, but says they're expensive, slow and hard to apply over large areas, and that for invasive species, culling is often the only effective option.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    That justifies technical management, not recreational hunting.

    Controlling invasive species should be done by professionals, with the least cruel methods possible.

    The cost should be borne by those who caused the problem.

    And preventing new introductions is essential.

    It says nothing about farming animals.

    The argument doesn't apply to the meat people buy.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Managing invasive species is a real and difficult problem, caused by humans. But it doesn't justify hunting as recreation, or raising and slaughtering domestic animals. The moral question remains: when there's no need, what justifies killing?

Contradictions

Doesn't apply the criterion to humans.

No one defends reducing human populations by culling.

Ignores the alternatives.

Even when they exist and are workable.

Hunts species that aren't invasive.

The population-control argument doesn't apply to them.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Invasive species and overpopulation can cause real damage to ecosystems, to other species and to agriculture. The wild boar case in Brazil is serious, and ignoring it helps no one. Sometimes control is necessary.

But

But management isn't recreation, and human-made problems call for careful solutions, with less cruel methods and prevention. None of this justifies raising and slaughtering animals for food.
