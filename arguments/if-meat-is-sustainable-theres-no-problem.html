H13

"If meat is sustainable, there's no problem."

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about the environment and agriculture, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the argument reduces a moral question about victims to an environmental one. Sustainability is about resources, emissions and ecosystems; it isn't about who is raised and killed. Carbon-neutral meat would still come from an animal who wanted to live. The environment is an important reason to eat less meat, but it isn't the main one: the main one is the animal.

Short answer

Sustainability is about resources and emissions, not about who gets killed. Carbon-neutral meat would still come from an animal raised to die young. The environment is a good reason to eat less meat, but it isn't the only or the main one: the main one is the animal, who feels and wants to live. Solving the climate doesn't solve the moral question.

For children

Some people think the only problem with meat is that it pollutes the planet. But cows, pigs and chickens feel fear and pain, and they want to live. Even if meat didn't pollute at all, they'd still be killed. Caring for the planet is important, and so is caring for animals.

Ask a question back

"If meat were carbon neutral, would the animal suffer any less when it died?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"My only issue is deforestation. With carbon-neutral meat, there's no reason to be vegan."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1The main problems with meat are environmental: emissions, deforestation and water use.
  2. P2New technologies and practices can make meat sustainable.
  3. P3If meat causes no environmental harm, there's no reason to avoid it.
  4. CSo with sustainable meat, veganism loses its point.

P2 may be partly true in the future. The problem lies in P1 and P3: they leave out meat's most direct harm, which is to the animal itself.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P1, which ignores the moral question.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument separates meat's harms and considers only some of them:

    HarmDoes sustainability solve it?
    Greenhouse gas emissionsPartly, in the best case
    Deforestation and land usePartly
    Water use and pollutionPartly
    The animal's suffering and deathNo
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Sustainability doesn't include the animal.

    The concept is about resources and ecosystems for future generations, not about the animal's interest in living and not suffering.

    2. The moral question comes first.

    Even if meat had no environmental impact, the question would remain: is it fair to kill a sentient being when we don't need to? (A10, A16).

    3. Sustainable meat at scale doesn't exist.

    The data show that even the lowest-impact meat emits far more than plant foods (H8).

    4. The argument swaps the main problem.

    By reducing everything to the environment, it ignores that veganism began as a position about animals.

    5. Environment and animals often point the same way, but not always.

    Eating less meat benefits both. But insect farming, hunting and "sustainable" leather show the two criteria can diverge, and then it's the animal that should carry the weight (H14).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Our World in Data, less meat or sustainable meatEven the lowest-impact beef emits more than ten times as much as the highest-impact peasBased on Poore and Nemecek, 2018
    The Vegan Society, definition of veganismVeganism seeks to exclude exploitation of and cruelty to animalsShows the center is the animal, not the environment
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: cultivated meat

    The most serious version points to meat cultivated in a lab, which doesn't require raising and killing animals, and says that in that case there'd be neither an environmental nor a moral problem.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Cultivated meat is something else.

    If it doesn't involve raising and killing animals, it isn't what the argument was defending.

    The counter-reply agrees with the central point.

    It recognizes that the problem is the animal.

    Until it exists at scale, the question remains.

    The meat people buy today comes from killed animals.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    This is the chapter where the return to the moral question is most direct. Sustainability is important, but it doesn't touch the heart of the problem: the animal who feels, wants to live and is killed. Even in a world of sustainable meat, that question would still be waiting for an answer.

Contradictions

Wouldn't accept the logic for pets.

No one thinks killing a dog is acceptable if it's carbon neutral.

Knows animals feel.

But leaves that out of the argument.

Defends sustainable meat they don't buy.

Most meat consumed isn't sustainable.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Environmental concerns are legitimate and urgent, and they're among the strongest reasons to eat less meat. It's also true that technologies such as cultivated meat could change a lot.

But

But the environment isn't the only problem. Even perfectly sustainable meat would still come from someone who felt fear and pain, and didn't want to die.
