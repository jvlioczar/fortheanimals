G14

"My body can't adapt"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the complaints of people who tried a vegan diet are real, but they're rarely investigated. Gas, tiredness and hunger usually come from abrupt changes, eating too little or lack of planning, and tend to pass with adjustments. The blood type diet, often cited as proof that some bodies require meat, was tested and refuted in 2014.

Short answer

The symptoms you felt are real, but they usually have simple causes: an abrupt change, a lot more fiber at once, eating too little or too little variety. With adjustments and time, most of them pass. The blood type diet was tested in 2014, and the results didn't depend on people's blood type. Everybody is different, but no study has identified people who need meat by nature.

For children

When we suddenly change what we eat, the body sometimes finds it strange: it can cause gas or hunger. It's like getting up early on the first day of school, which is hard, but then the body gets used to it. Changing little by little and eating plenty helps the body adapt.

Ask a question back

"Was it possible to look into the cause, with blood tests and a dietitian, before deciding?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"I tried and felt sick. My blood type needs meat. Everybody is different."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1People vary in metabolism, digestion and preferences.
  2. P2Some people report symptoms when trying a vegan diet.
  3. P3If the body reacts badly, it's a sign the diet doesn't suit it.
  4. CSo the vegan diet doesn't suit everyone, and those who feel unwell should eat meat.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: symptoms of adapting to a dietary change don't indicate a permanent incompatibility, and they're almost never investigated.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The most reported symptoms have known causes:

    SymptomLikely causeSolution
    Gas and bloatingA sudden increase in fiber and legumesIncrease gradually; soak and cook well
    TirednessEating too little: plant foods have fewer calories per volumeEat more, include nuts, seeds and grains
    Frequent hungerToo little protein or fat in mealsInclude legumes, tofu, nuts
    Weakness after monthsPossible lack of B12 or ironBlood tests and supplementation
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Adaptation symptoms aren't incompatibility.

    The gut usually adapts to more fiber within a few weeks. Gradual changes reduce symptoms.

    2. The blood type diet was refuted.

    In 2014, researchers at the University of Toronto tested the hypothesis with about 1,400 people. Associations between the diets and health markers existed, but didn't depend on each person's blood type.

    3. Complaints are rarely investigated.

    Those who quit almost never get blood tests or see a dietitian. Without investigation, there's no way to know the cause.

    4. Individual variation calls for adjustments, not meat.

    People with specific conditions, such as bowel diseases, may need adaptations, which a professional can guide.

    5. "My body craves it" is often habit.

    The craving for meat is largely learned (C3).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Wang and colleagues, 2014, *PLoS ONE*The associations of "blood type diets" with health markers didn't depend on people's blood typeThe authors concluded the hypothesis is false
    Faunalytics, study of current and former vegetarians and vegans34% of those who quit kept the diet for three months or lessShows that many people quit during the adaptation period
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some people have real conditions

    The most serious version grants that most complaints are about adaptation, but points out that some people have real conditions, such as bowel diseases or multiple allergies, that make a vegan diet very hard.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    These conditions justify exceptions.

    Veganism already allows for what is "possible and practicable" (D7).

    They aren't the rule.

    Most people who use the argument don't have these conditions.

    Even in these cases, reducing is possible.

    A partial limitation doesn't require abandoning the effort.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    If adaptation symptoms pass and the blood type diet has no basis, most people's bodies adapt. For those with a real condition, exceptions apply. For everyone else, the question remains: does the discomfort of a transition justify the animals' deaths?

Absurd

Contradictions

Has adapted to other changes.

Such as schedules, travel and new foods.

Accepts that the body finds changes strange in other cases.

Such as when starting to exercise.

Doesn't follow the blood type diet in anything else.

Uses it only in this argument.

What the argument gets right

What is true

The experiences of people who tried and felt unwell are real and should be heard with respect. People vary, and some have health conditions that require special care. Animal advocates should offer support and information, not dismissal.

But

But most complaints are about adaptation, and can be resolved with time and adjustments. The human body is flexible, and that very flexibility is what makes it possible to live well without eating animals.
