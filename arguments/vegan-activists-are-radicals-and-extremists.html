L5

"Vegan activists are radicals and extremists"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about who defends veganism, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the question depended on who defends animals, vegans being pleasant and consistent would make the argument valid, and being annoying and inconsistent would make it invalid. But the animals suffer just the same, whoever the messenger is.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's reduction to the radical: judging a cause by the methods of a minority. Some activists use controversial tactics, as in any social movement. But most animal advocates act through legal and peaceful means, and the moral question doesn't depend on anyone's methods. Besides, many of the actions called "radical", such as filming on farms, revealed practices society didn't know about.

Short answer

Every social movement has people who use controversial tactics, and those can and should be discussed. But most animal advocates act legally and peacefully, and the moral question doesn't depend on anyone's methods. Many actions called radical, such as filming on farms, revealed practices society didn't know about (B10). And research shows that radical voices can even increase support for moderate ones.

For children

In every big group, some people do things that go too far. But that doesn't mean the whole group is the same, or that their idea is wrong. Most people who defend animals do it by talking, by choosing what they eat and by helping animals. What matters is whether animals feel and deserve to be cared for.

Ask a question back

"If the most radical activists disappeared, would the question about the animals stop existing?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Eco-terrorists. Vegans break into farms."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Some animal activists trespass, damage property or intimidate people.
  2. P2These tactics are illegal or unethical.
  3. P3A movement that uses these tactics doesn't deserve support.
  4. CSo veganism should be rejected.

P1 is true of a minority. P2 is true of some tactics. The problem lies in P3 and the conclusion: the methods of some don't determine the validity of the cause.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument judges the cause by the methods of a part of it:

    What the argument assessesWhat it should assess
    The tactics of a minorityThe moral question about animals
    The acts of some activistsThe reasons for veganism
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's reduction to the radical.

    A cause doesn't become wrong because some of its supporters use questionable methods.

    2. Most act peacefully.

    Consumer choices, education, legal and political action.

    3. Many movements had radical wings.

    Abolitionism, women's suffrage and civil rights all had activists who used controversial tactics, and the causes were just.

    4. "Radical" actions revealed facts.

    Investigations in farms and slaughterhouses showed practices society didn't know about, and some led to prosecutions and changes (B10).

    5. The radical flank can help moderates.

    Experiments from 2022, including one about the animal movement, found that the presence of a radical group increased support for moderate groups.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Simpson, Willer and Feinberg, 2022, *PNAS Nexus*The presence of a radical flank increased support for moderate groups in the same movementAlso used in K8
    Animal Legal and Historical Center, ag-gag lawsLaws criminalizing filming on farms have been struck down in several US statesShows that part of the "radicalism" is documentation
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: violence must be condemned

    The most serious version points out that some actions caused real harm to people and property, and that no cause justifies violence.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Condemning violence is compatible with veganism.

    And most vegans condemn it.

    The methods of some don't change the question.

    The question about the animals remains.

    Violence also happens to animals.

    Every day, legally and on an industrial scale.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Judging the cause by the methods of some is a way of not discussing the cause. The moral question remains, whoever defends it and however: is it fair to make animals suffer and die when we don't need to?

What the argument gets right

What is true

Some activists use tactics that may be illegal, dangerous or counterproductive, and these should be discussed openly. No cause justifies violence against people, and the animal movement should make that clear.

But

But the methods of some don't decide the cause. The question about the animals stays the same, and deserves its own answer.
