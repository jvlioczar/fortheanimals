J16

"My food beats yours"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about cost, practicality and the economy, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended for being cheap, practical or good for the economy, any situation in which exploitation were cheaper or created more jobs would justify it. No one accepts this reasoning when the victims are human: exploiting people was once profitable too.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's a taunt, not an argument. Even if the meat dish tasted better to the speaker, the question isn't which food wins a taste contest, but what happened to whoever became the food. Besides, the comparison is false: much of what people eat every day is already vegan, and much of the flavor credited to meat comes from salt, fat, the grill and seasoning.

Note: the Portuguese original is a cruder Brazilian taunt; the English phrasing keeps its spirit.

Short answer

Maybe you do like your food better. But the question isn't which dish tastes better; it's whether it's worth an animal's life. And honestly, the rice, beans, bread, pasta, fries and almost all the seasoning on your plate are already vegan.

For children

Imagine someone said: "My toy is much cooler than yours, so I can take your friend's without asking." Having a cool toy doesn't let anyone harm others. It's the same with food. What matters isn't which dish tastes better; it's whether someone had to suffer for it to exist.

Ask a question back

"If the best dish in the world were made of dog meat, would that settle the question?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"My food beats yours. While you eat lettuce, I'm eating steak."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Food exists to give pleasure, as well as nourishment.
  2. P2Dishes with meat give more pleasure than vegan dishes.
  3. P3Giving up a greater pleasure without need is irrational.
  4. CSo eating meat is the rational choice, and the vegan is the one missing out.

P1 is true. P2 is debatable and varies from person to person. The main problem lies in P3, which treats the eater's pleasure as the only thing at stake.

The argument is invalid as a reply to veganism: it compares menus, while the vegan objection is about harm to others.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The sentence mixes two different disputes:

    DisputeWhat's at stakeWho decides
    Which food tastes better?The eater's pleasureEach person's taste
    Is eating animals just?Another being's life and sufferingReasons, not taste buds

    Veganism doesn't claim vegan food always tastes better. It claims the eater's pleasure doesn't justify the harm to the one eaten.

  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Pleasure isn't a license to cause harm.

    In no other context do we accept "it tastes better" or "it's more fun" as a justification for harming someone (J11).

    2. The comparison is false.

    Much of what people eat every day is already vegan: rice, beans, bread, pasta, fruit, vegetables, potatoes, most sauces and seasonings. Vegan food isn't a separate cuisine; it's most of ordinary cuisine, without the animal.

    3. Much of the flavor doesn't come from the animal.

    Salt, fat, smoking, caramelization on the grill and seasoning account for much of what people associate with the taste of meat, and they can be reproduced with plant ingredients.

    4. Taste is habit.

    Food preferences form through repeated exposure and change with it. People who say vegan food is bad have often tried little of it, or only carelessly made versions.

    5. It's a taunt, and taunts reveal the lack of an argument.

    When a conversation moves from reason to mockery, it's almost always because the moral question went unanswered.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Piazza et al., 2015"Nice" is one of the four most common justifications for eating meatThe "4Ns": natural, normal, necessary and nice
    Brehm, A Theory of Psychological Reactance (1966)When people feel their freedom threatened, they react by reasserting the behaviorHelps explain the challenging tone of the phrase
    Minson and Monin, 2012Meat eaters tend to react against vegetarians because they expect to be judgedThe taunt as a preemptive defense
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: pleasure is also a legitimate interest

    The most serious version says: the pleasure of eating is an important part of a good life, tied to culture, memory and togetherness. Asking someone to give it up is asking a lot.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The pleasure doesn't have to disappear.

    The swap is of ingredients, not of the pleasure of eating. Culture, memory and togetherness remain (D8).

    Legitimate interests have limits too.

    The eater's pleasure is a real interest, but a small one next to another being's interest in not suffering and not dying.

    The disproportion decides.

    On one side, the difference between two dishes; on the other, a whole life.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if the taunt were true and the meat dish tasted better to the speaker, that would only answer a question of taste. The moral question remains open: does the pleasure of a dish justify what is done to the animal?

Generalizing from little experience

Contradictions

Eats vegan food every day.

Rice, beans and bread don't stop being tasty for having no animal in them.

Doesn't accept "it's tasty" for other harms.

No one would defend eating dog meat for being flavorful.

Uses seasoning to give meat flavor.

And the seasoning is plant-based.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Eating is a pleasure, and that pleasure matters. Anyone who wants to persuade someone to change their diet does well to show that vegan food can be delicious, not a sad sacrifice.

But

But flavor isn't the measure of justice. A food can be tasty and still cost the life of someone who didn't want to die.
