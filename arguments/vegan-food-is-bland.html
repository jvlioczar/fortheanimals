J6

"Vegan food is bland"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about cost, practicality and the economy, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended for being cheap, practical or good for the economy, any situation in which exploitation were cheaper or created more jobs would justify it. No one accepts this reasoning when the victims are human: exploiting people was once profitable too.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's a generalization born of unfamiliarity. The flavor of food comes mainly from seasonings, techniques and combinations of ingredients, and almost all seasonings are plants: garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices. Many traditional dishes from around the world are already vegan or nearly so: chana masala, falafel, dal, pasta arrabbiata, bean chili, Ethiopian lentil stews, Brazilian acarajé. Vegan food isn't "just salad".

Short answer

Almost all the flavor in food comes from seasonings and techniques, and seasonings are plants: garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spices. Many traditional dishes are already vegan or nearly so: well-seasoned beans, falafel, chana masala, dal, pasta arrabbiata, bean chili, garlic bread. Vegan food isn't "just salad". People who find it bland have often tried very little of it, or tried it badly made.

For children

What makes food tasty is the seasoning: garlic, onion, herbs, chili, oregano, cumin. All of that comes from plants! Well-seasoned beans, fries, popcorn, pasta with tomato sauce, bread, fruit: all of that is vegan and really tasty.

Ask a question back

"Are fries, seasoned beans and pasta with tomato sauce bland?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Just salad? Rabbit food. Am I supposed to eat grass?"

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Meat and dairy give flavor, texture and satiety to many dishes.
  2. P2Without them, many recipes are less tasty.
  3. P3The pleasure of eating is important for quality of life.
  4. CSo a vegan diet reduces quality of life.

P1 and P3 are true. P2 is true for recipes built around meat, but not for well-made plant cooking. The problem lies in the conclusion: it generalizes from bad examples or from unfamiliarity.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2, generalized.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument confuses the absence of meat with the absence of flavor:

    Source of flavorIs it a plant?
    Garlic, onion, chilies, herbs, spicesYes
    Techniques: sautéing, roasting, grilling, smoking, fermentingYes, applicable to plants
    Umami: tomato, mushrooms, soy sauce, misoYes
    Fat: olive oil, oils, nuts, avocadoYes
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Seasonings are plants.

    Much of what makes food tasty comes from plants: garlic, onion, herbs, chilies and spices.

    2. Many traditional dishes are already vegan.

    Around the world, there's an enormous variety of dishes without animal products or easily adapted.

    3. It's a generalization.

    Often, the person saying it has tried few options, or badly prepared ones.

    4. Much of the flavor we associate with meat comes from technique and seasoning.

    Smoked, grilled, browned, sautéed: all of this can be applied to vegetables and plant proteins. The result isn't identical to meat, but it isn't "bland".

    5. Many chefs and restaurants show otherwise.

    Plant cooking has gained ground in award-winning restaurants and kitchens around the world.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Brazil's Ministry of Health, *Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population* (2014, in Portuguese)Values traditional cooking based on plant foods such as rice, beans, cassava, vegetables and fruitAn example of how rich a plant-based cuisine can be
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some flavors are irreplaceable

    The most serious version grants that vegan food can be tasty, but says certain flavors, such as a good barbecue or an aged cheese, have no plant equivalent.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It changes the argument.

    It stops being "bland" and becomes "doesn't have exactly that flavor" (J5).

    There are new flavors to discover.

    The swap isn't only a loss; it's also a discovery.

    The pleasure of a flavor doesn't justify a death.

    The central argument returns to J5.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if some specific flavor were irreplaceable, the question would be whether the pleasure of a dish justifies an animal's life. And in practice, plant cooking offers so much pleasure that the question rarely needs to be asked.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that there is bland vegan food, just as there is bland food with meat. For people who grew up with meat-centered dishes, early experiences can disappoint, and learning to cook with plants takes some time.

But

But plant cooking is vast, creative and full of flavor. Most of what makes food tasty already comes from plants.
