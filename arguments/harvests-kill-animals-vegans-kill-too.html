H2

"Harvests kill animals; vegans kill too"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's true that agriculture kills animals, such as rodents, insects and birds, during planting and harvest. But livestock farming uses far more cropland, because the animals eat feed, as well as using enormous pastures. About 80% of the world's farmland serves the production of meat, dairy and farmed fish. Eating plants directly tends to cause fewer harvest deaths than eating animals fed on plants.

Short answer

It's true: agriculture kills animals. But livestock farming needs much more agriculture, because the animals eat feed, and it occupies immense pastures. About 80% of the world's farmland serves the production of meat, dairy and farmed fish. Eating plants directly uses less land and tends to cause fewer harvest deaths. The question isn't which diet is perfect, but which causes fewer deaths.

For children

When machines harvest the fields, some little animals can die. That's sad. But farm animals eat a lot of crops. So to make meat, even more has to be planted, and more little animals die in the harvest. Eating the plants directly means fewer little animals die.

Ask a question back

"If harvests kill animals, doesn't eating animals fed on harvests kill even more?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"How many mice died for your salad? Vegans have blood on their hands too."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Agriculture kills animals during soil preparation, planting, pesticide application and harvest.
  2. P2Vegans eat agricultural products.
  3. P3So vegans also cause animals' deaths.
  4. CSo the vegan diet isn't morally superior.

P1, P2 and P3 are true. The problem lies in the conclusion: it ignores that eating animals requires even more agriculture, and therefore even more harvest deaths, on top of the deaths of the farmed animals themselves.

The argument is valid to show that no diet is entirely innocent. Its soundness fails when it concludes the diets are equivalent.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument compares the vegan diet with a cost-free diet, not with the real diet with meat:

    DietHarvest deathsDeaths of farmed animals
    VeganFrom crops for human foodNone
    With meat from feed-fed animalsFrom crops for human food and from crops for feed, which is largerBillions of animals a year
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Livestock farming uses most of the farmland.

    According to Our World in Data, about 80% of the world's farmland, between pasture and feed crops, serves the production of meat, dairy and farmed fish, which provide only 17% of calories.

    2. The animals eat feed.

    To get one calorie of meat, the animal must be given several calories of feed. This multiplies the area planted and, with it, tends to multiply harvest deaths. Estimates of how many animals die per hectare are very uncertain, but the difference in area is large enough to matter.

    3. A vegan diet would greatly reduce farmland.

    According to Poore and Nemecek, a global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75%.

    4. There are also the deaths of the farmed animals themselves.

    On top of harvest deaths, livestock farming directly kills tens of billions of land animals a year.

    5. The argument confuses accidental and intentional deaths.

    Even where there are harvest deaths, they're side effects, while slaughter is the goal (F4).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Our World in Data, land for agricultureLivestock uses 80% of farmland and provides 17% of calories and 38% of proteinGlobal data
    Our World in Data, land use and dietsA global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75%Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018
    Matheny, 2003, *Least Harm*, record in the FAO's AGRIS databaseReplies to Davis (2003): per-hectare counts ignore how many people each area feeds; corrected, they favor vegetarianismChecked from the abstract
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: pasture-raised meat may kill less

    The most serious version, defended by the philosopher Steven Davis in 2003, says cattle raised only on pasture, without feed, would cause fewer wild animal deaths per calorie than crops, because pastures aren't harvested.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The numbers were disputed.

    Matheny showed that Davis compared deaths per hectare, not per calorie produced. Per calorie, crops for direct human consumption come out better.

    Pasture requires far more land.

    Pasture-raised meat uses an enormous area, often won through deforestation (H1).

    Almost no one eats only pasture-raised meat.

    Most meat comes from animals fed on feed.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The argument recognizes that the death of animals matters, and that's the central point of veganism. If it matters, the consistent choice is the one that causes fewer deaths. The moral question returns: if eating plants kills fewer, why choose what kills more?

Contradictions

Says they care about the mice in the harvest.

But eats animals raised and slaughtered.

Ignores the cropland for feed.

Which is larger than that for human food.

Wouldn't reduce their own contribution.

Even knowing that eating plants would cause fewer deaths.

What the argument gets right

What is true

The argument gets one important thing right: agriculture kills animals, and no diet is entirely innocent. Vegans should recognize this and support farming practices that reduce those deaths. The philosophical debate on the subject is serious.

But

But comparing real diets, eating plants directly uses less land and causes fewer deaths. If harvest deaths matter, they're one more reason to stop eating animals.
