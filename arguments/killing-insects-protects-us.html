F6

"Killing insects protects us"

The answer in one sentence

protecting yourself from a real threat is legitimate, and some insects and arachnids transmit diseases or have dangerous venom. But the phrase generalizes from a few species: most insects killed at home threaten no one. And killing often isn't the best protection. Brazil's Ministry of Health doesn't recommend insecticides against scorpions, which can drive them out of hiding and increase stings, and the Wolbachia method, which doesn't kill mosquitoes, reduced dengue cases by 69% in Niterói. Prevention protects more and causes less suffering.

Short answer

Protecting yourself from a real threat is legitimate. But most insects threaten no one, and killing often isn't the best protection. Brazil's Ministry of Health doesn't recommend insecticides against scorpions: they have no proven effectiveness and can drive scorpions out of hiding, increasing stings. Against dengue, removing standing water and releasing mosquitoes carrying the Wolbachia bacterium, which doesn't kill mosquitoes, reduced cases by 69% in Niterói. Prevention protects more and causes less suffering.

For children

Some little creatures, like certain mosquitoes and scorpions, can harm us, and it's right to protect ourselves. But the best protection is often to stop them from coming: getting rid of standing water, using window screens, cleaning the yard. That way we stay protected and don't need to kill the butterfly, the ladybug and so many other creatures that harm no one.

Ask a question back

"If prevention protects more, why start by killing?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Mosquitoes carry disease and a scorpion can kill a child. Killing these creatures is about protection."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Some small animals are dangerous: mosquitoes transmit dengue, Zika and chikungunya, and the yellow scorpion can cause serious stings.
  2. P2We have the right to protect ourselves from real threats.
  3. P3Killing these animals protects us.
  4. CSo killing insects and other small animals is justified.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 is only partly true: in several cases, killing isn't the most effective form of protection. And the conclusion goes beyond the premises, moving from "some species, in some cases" to "insects" in general.

The argument is valid in its narrow form. Its soundness fails at P3 and in the generalized conclusion.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    SituationIs there a real threat?What protects best
    Aedes aegypti mosquitoYes, in areas with dengue, Zika and chikungunyaRemoving breeding sites, screens, repellent, the Wolbachia method
    Yellow scorpionYes, especially for childrenCleaning the yard, sealing drains and cracks, no insecticide
    CockroachCan contaminate foodSealing, cleaning, storing food
    Butterfly, moth, ladybug, bee, most spidersNoNothing: just leave them alone or put them outside
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The threat comes from few species.

    Among the millions of insect species, only a small fraction transmit diseases to humans or cause serious harm. The phrase "killing insects protects us" uses the danger of a few to justify killing all.

    2. Against scorpions, poison can make things worse.

    Brazil's Ministry of Health doesn't recommend chemical control of scorpions in urban areas: the products have no proven effectiveness and can make the animals leave their hiding places, which increases the risk of stings. The guidance is to manage the environment: clean yards, no rubble, sealed drains and cracks. Scorpions, by the way, aren't insects but arachnids.

    3. Against dengue, prevention works better.

    Fogging only kills the adult mosquitoes it reaches and is reserved for outbreaks. The main measure is removing breeding sites. The Wolbachia method releases mosquitoes carrying a bacterium that hinders virus transmission, without killing the population: in Niterói it was associated with a 69% reduction in dengue cases, and in a randomized trial in Indonesia, 77%.

    4. The method matters.

    Even when killing seems necessary, some methods cause more suffering than others. Glue traps, for example, hold the animal until they die of exhaustion or hunger. And broad-spectrum insecticides also kill the animals that eat mosquitoes, such as spiders, geckos and toads.

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: in a moment of danger, there's no time to prevent

    The most serious version says that when there's a scorpion in a child's bedroom or a dengue epidemic in the neighborhood, there's no time for prevention, and killing is the immediate response.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Immediate danger is the exception.

    Self-defense against a concrete threat is recognized, as in F5. That doesn't justify killing all insects, or spraying the house routinely.

    Even in an emergency there are options.

    The scorpion can be covered with a jar and taken to the zoonosis control center, as health authorities advise.

    The emergency shows why prevention matters.

    The more prevention, the fewer dangerous situations and the fewer deaths, of humans and of other animals.

Contradictions

Says it's for protection.

And kills the butterfly and the spider that threaten no one.

Wants to get rid of mosquitoes.

And kills the geckos and spiders that eat them.

Wants to avoid scorpion stings.

And uses the poison that can drive them out of hiding.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that mosquitoes transmit serious diseases, that scorpions can kill children, and that protecting yourself from real threats is legitimate.

But

But the threat comes from few species, and the best protection, according to health authorities themselves, is almost always prevention. Protecting yourself doesn't require killing everything that flies or crawls.
