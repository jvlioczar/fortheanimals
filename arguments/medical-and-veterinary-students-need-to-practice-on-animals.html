I4

"Medical and veterinary students need to practice on animals"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

learning anatomy, physiology and surgery is essential, but research shows that methods that don't harm animals teach just as well. A 2021 systematic review found 50 studies comparing the two kinds of teaching: in 90% of them, humane methods were as or more effective than harmful animal use. Simulators, models, ethically sourced cadavers and real supervised surgeries on animals who need them teach without killing.

Short answer

Practice is essential, but research shows that methods without harm teach just as well: a 2021 review of 50 studies found humane methods as or more effective in 90% of them. Simulators, models, ethically sourced cadavers and supervised surgeries on animals who need them teach without killing. No one needs to kill a healthy animal to learn.

For children

In the past, in class, people cut open frogs and other animals to see what they looked like inside. Today you can learn with models, computer programs and dummies that imitate the body. Studies show that students learn just as well, without any animal having to die.

Ask a question back

"If students learn just as well with simulators, what does the animal's death add to the teaching?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Would you want to be operated on by a surgeon who never practiced on an animal? Students need to practice."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Health professionals need practice to work safely.
  2. P2Practicing on animals, living or killed for the purpose, is the most effective way to learn.
  3. P3Future patients' safety is worth more than these animals' lives.
  4. CSo using animals in teaching is necessary.

P1 is true. P2 is contradicted by research on teaching. P3 would only apply if there were no effective alternative.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    Use in teachingHarm-free alternatives
    Dissection in biology classesAnatomical models, interactive software, videos
    Surgical practice on healthy animalsSimulators, synthetic models, cadavers donated after natural death
    Physiology on live animalsComputer simulations, experiments with the students themselves
    Veterinary clinical trainingReal, supervised surgeries on animals who need them, such as spays at shelters
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The evidence on teaching favors alternatives.

    Patronek and Rauch reviewed controlled studies in 2007 and found equal or better results with alternative methods. Zemanova and Knight expanded the review in 2021: in 90% of 50 studies, humane methods were as or more effective.

    2. Killing isn't necessary to practice.

    Cadavers of animals who died naturally, donated by their guardians, and surgeries the animals actually need provide real practice.

    3. Many schools have already changed.

    Medical schools in several countries have dropped the use of live animals in teaching, and Brazil regulates this use under the Arouca Law (Law 11,794/2008), with ethics committees that assess each project.

    4. Teaching also teaches values.

    Using animals as disposable material conveys to future professionals an idea about these animals' worth.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Patronek and Rauch, 2007, *JAVMA*Alternative methods had results equal to or better than animal useSystematic review of controlled studies
    Zemanova and Knight, 2021, *Animals*In 90% of 50 studies, humane methods were as or more effectiveSystematic review
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some skills require living tissue

    The most serious version points out that the feel of operating on living tissue, with bleeding and physiological reactions, is hard to reproduce in simulators.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Real surgeries on animals who need them provide this.

    Shelter spays and supervised treatments give practice on living tissue without killing healthy animals.

    Human medicine already teaches this way.

    Doctors learn surgery with simulators, donated cadavers and supervised practice on patients, not by killing people.

    The evidence shows the result.

    The reviews measure exactly learning, and the alternatives do well.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to kill a healthy animal to teach what can be taught without that. When the alternative works, the harm stops being necessary.

Contradictions

Demands real practice.

And accepts that doctors learn without killing people.

Teaches how to care for animals.

By killing healthy animals.

Values science.

And ignores the reviews of its own teaching.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that practice is essential and that future patients' safety matters a great deal. It's also true that some skills are hard to reproduce in simulators.

But

But research on teaching shows that alternatives work, and that it's possible to practice on living tissue without killing healthy animals.
