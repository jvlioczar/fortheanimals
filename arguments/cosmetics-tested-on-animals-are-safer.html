I3

"Cosmetics tested on animals are safer"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

cosmetics aren't medicine: there's no disease to cure that would justify the testing. And safety doesn't depend on it. The European Union has banned the sale of cosmetics tested on animals since 2013, and Brazil banned in 2023 the use of vertebrates in testing cosmetics with ingredients of known effect. Companies use already-assessed ingredients and animal-free methods, such as skin models reconstructed in the lab.

Short answer

Cosmetics aren't medicine: there's no disease to cure that would justify the testing. And safety doesn't depend on it: the European Union has banned the sale of cosmetics tested on animals since 2013, and Brazil banned in 2023 the use of vertebrates in testing cosmetics with ingredients of known effect. Companies use already-assessed ingredients and animal-free methods, such as lab-reconstructed skin.

For children

In the past, to find out whether a shampoo stung the eyes, people put shampoo in rabbits' eyes. Today scientists can test on little pieces of skin made in the lab. And many countries have banned testing makeup and shampoo on animals.

Ask a question back

"If cosmetics sold in Europe are safe without animal testing, what does the test add?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"I prefer cosmetics tested on animals. At least I know they won't give me an allergy."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Cosmetics can cause irritation and allergies.
  2. P2Animal tests identify these risks before sale.
  3. P3Consumer safety is worth more than the use of some animals.
  4. CSo testing cosmetics on animals is justifiable.

P1 is true. P2 is partial: animal models predict many human reactions poorly, and there are validated animal-free methods. P3 ignores that there are ways of ensuring safety without tests.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2 and P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    QuestionMedicines (I1)Cosmetics
    Is there a medical need?Yes, in many casesNo
    Are there animal-free methods?PartlyYes, for most assessments
    What does the law require?Still requires testing in many casesEU bans; Brazil restricts
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. There's no medical need.

    The medicines argument, debatable as it is, appeals to lives saved (I1). With cosmetics, the benefit is aesthetic.

    2. Safety is ensured in other ways.

    The European Union has banned animal testing of cosmetics since 2009 and the sale of cosmetics tested on animals since 2013, under Regulation 1223/2009. Products sold there are still assessed, using ingredients of known safety and alternative methods.

    3. Brazil has changed too.

    Normative Resolution 58 of Brazil's National Council for the Control of Animal Experimentation (CONCEA), in force since March 2023, bans the use of vertebrates in research and quality control for cosmetics, personal hygiene products and perfumes with ingredients of known effect. Animal protection organizations point out that the resolution doesn't ban selling products tested abroad.

    4. Animals are poor models for many human reactions.

    Differences between species mean that animal tests predict some reactions in people poorly, which drove the development of methods using human cells and tissues.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: new ingredients still need to be assessed

    The most serious version points out that new ingredients, with no history of use, need safety assessment, and that not all effects can be predicted without animals.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    No one needs a new cosmetic ingredient.

    There are thousands of ingredients already assessed.

    Alternative methods keep advancing.

    The European rule has worked for more than a decade without animal testing.

    The benefit doesn't justify the harm.

    Even with some uncertainty, an aesthetic advantage doesn't outweigh animals' suffering.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to make animals suffer for a beauty product. When not even safety depends on it, only habit is left.

Contradictions

Buys European cosmetics.

Which can't be tested on animals.

Loves their own pet.

And accepts that rabbits and guinea pigs suffer for a lipstick.

Doubts safety without testing.

Without knowing that most ingredients are already known.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that cosmetics can cause reactions and that consumer safety matters. It's also true that genuinely new ingredients require careful assessment.

But

But European experience and the Brazilian rule show that safety can be ensured without animals. For a product with no medical need, the harm isn't justified.
