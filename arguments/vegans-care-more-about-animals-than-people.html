L6

"Vegans care more about animals than people"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about who defends veganism, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the question depended on who defends animals, vegans being pleasant and consistent would make the argument valid, and being annoying and inconsistent would make it invalid. But the animals suffer just the same, whoever the messenger is.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's a false dilemma and a generalization. Empathy isn't a zero-sum resource: caring about animals doesn't reduce caring about people. A small 2010 neuroimaging study found that vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of empathy-related areas in response to human suffering too; it's a hint, not proof. And many animal advocates also work for human rights.

Short answer

Empathy isn't zero-sum: caring about animals doesn't reduce caring about people. A small 2010 study found that vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of brain areas linked to empathy in response to human suffering too. Many animal advocates work for human rights, against hunger and for social justice. And no longer eating animals takes nothing away from anyone.

For children

The heart has no limit on how many people or animals we can love. Caring for your dog doesn't make you love your family less. It's the same with vegans: caring for animals doesn't make anyone care less about people.

Ask a question back

"What would you stop doing for people if you stopped eating animals?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"You cry for the cow but not for the homeless. Vegans hate people."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Some vegans show more indignation at animal suffering than at human suffering.
  2. P2Humans should have moral priority.
  3. P3Whoever reverses that priority has distorted values.
  4. CSo veganism reflects distorted values.

P1 may be true of some. The problem lies in the conclusion: it generalizes from a few and assumes that caring about animals takes care away from humans.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails because it generalizes and assumes a competition that doesn't exist.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument assumes empathy is limited and competitive:

    AssumptionStatus
    Those who care about animals care less about humansNo evidence; there are hints of the opposite
    Vegans in general despise humansA baseless generalization
    No longer eating animals takes something from humansFalse (J6)
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's a false dilemma.

    No one has to choose between animals and humans.

    2. Empathy isn't zero-sum.

    Filippi and colleagues found in 2010 that vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of empathy-related regions in response to scenes of human suffering, not less. The study is small, with 60 people, and measures brain activation, not behavior; but it points in the opposite direction from the stereotype.

    3. It's a generalization.

    Judging all vegans by a few is unfair, as it would be for any group.

    4. Many animal advocates work for humans.

    The animal movement has strong ties to social justice movements.

    5. Livestock farming also affects humans.

    Slaughterhouse workers, communities affected by deforestation and public health (J6).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Filippi et al., 2010, *PLoS ONE*Vegetarians and vegans showed greater activation of the anterior cingulate cortex and inferior frontal gyrus in response to scenes of human sufferingA neuroimaging study with 60 participants
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some vegans really do despise people

    The most serious version points to real cases of vegans who make disdainful remarks about humans or compare people to animals offensively.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Some don't represent all.

    There are unpleasant people in every group.

    Such behavior should be criticized.

    Including by vegans themselves.

    It doesn't change the question about animals.

    The question is still about them.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Caring about animals doesn't require despising anyone. The moral question stays the same: do animals deserve not to suffer and die without need? Answering yes takes nothing away from people.

Contradictions

The argument serves to accuse, not to help.

Admires people who care for both animals and people.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Some vegans express themselves badly about humans, and disrespectful comparisons can hurt. Animal advocates should take care that their cause doesn't seem indifferent to human suffering.

But

But empathy isn't zero-sum, and the little evidence there is points away from the stereotype rather than toward it. There's no need to choose.
