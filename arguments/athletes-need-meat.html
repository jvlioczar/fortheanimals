G9

"Athletes need meat"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

what matters for performance and muscle gain is the amount of protein and energy, not its source. A 2021 study compared young vegans and omnivores eating the same amount of protein over 12 weeks of resistance training and found no difference in muscle mass or strength. There are elite vegan athletes in many sports.

Short answer

What matters for muscle is the amount of protein and energy, not its source. In 2021, a study compared young vegans and omnivores eating the same amount of protein over 12 weeks of resistance training and found no difference in muscle mass or strength. There are elite vegan athletes in weightlifting, running, football and other sports. With planning, a vegan diet meets athletes' needs.

For children

To get strong, the body needs protein and plenty of food. That protein can come from beans, lentils, tofu and other plants. There are champion athletes who don't eat any meat at all!

Ask a question back

"If vegans and omnivores gain the same muscle with the same protein, what does meat add?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Nobody builds muscle without meat. There's no such thing as a vegan bodybuilder."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Athletes need more protein than the average person.
  2. P2Animal proteins are high quality and rich in leucine, which is important for muscle synthesis.
  3. P3With plant protein, it's harder to meet an athlete's needs.
  4. CSo athletes need to eat meat.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 has a point: better planning may be needed. The problem lies in the conclusion: the evidence shows that, with enough protein, the source makes no difference.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails because P3 describes a planning difficulty, not an impossibility.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument confuses the need for more protein with a need for meat:

    What the athlete needsCan it come from plants?
    More proteinYes: legumes, soy, tofu, seitan, plant protein powders
    Enough leucineYes, with an adequate amount of protein
    Enough energyYes
    Iron, B12, creatineYes, with planning and supplementation when needed
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The source of protein doesn't change the result.

    In 2021, Hevia-Larraín and colleagues followed young vegans and omnivores through 12 weeks of resistance training, with protein intake matched at 1.6 g per kilogram per day. There was no difference in muscle mass, fiber area or strength. To reach that amount, the vegans used soy protein powder.

    2. With enough protein, the difference is small or nil.

    A 2018 meta-analysis found no difference between supplementing soy protein or animal protein in gains in mass and strength from resistance training. Another, from 2021, also found no difference in strength or absolute lean mass, but found a small advantage for animal protein in the lean mass of younger adults. The difference appears in some studies, is small, and can be offset with more plant protein.

    3. There are elite vegan athletes.

    In many sports, vegan athletes compete and win at a high level. This shows the diet doesn't prevent performance.

    4. Nutrition bodies include athletes.

    The US Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics considers well-planned vegan diets adequate for athletes too.

    5. "Nobody builds muscle without meat" is false.

    There are vegan bodybuilders, and the phrase describes a prejudice, not a fact.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Hevia-Larraín and colleagues, 2021, *Sports Medicine*With the same protein intake, vegans and omnivores had the same gains in mass and strength over 12 weeksA small study (38 young men), conducted at the University of São Paulo
    Messina and colleagues, 2018, *IJSNEM*No difference between soy protein and animal protein in gains in mass and strengthMeta-analysis of supplementation trials
    Lim and colleagues, 2021, *Nutrients*No difference in strength or absolute lean mass; a small advantage for animal protein in the lean mass of adults under 50Meta-analysis; the result in younger people comes from a subgroup analysis
    Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016Well-planned vegan diets are adequate for athletes tooOfficial position
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: it's harder to reach the protein

    The most serious version grants that vegans can build muscle, but says it's harder to reach high protein intakes from plant foods alone, and that many need supplements.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Omnivorous athletes also use supplements.

    Whey is a supplement; there are plant equivalents.

    Harder isn't impossible.

    It's a matter of planning, as with any athlete's diet.

    The difficulty doesn't justify the harm.

    The effort of planning weighs less than the animals' lives.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    If the source of protein doesn't change the result, meat isn't necessary even for athletes. What remains is a choice: does the convenience of a more concentrated protein source justify raising and killing animals?

What the argument gets right

What is true

Athletes have greater needs, and it's true that reaching high protein intakes takes more planning on a vegan diet. It's also true that studies on the subject are still relatively small.

But

But the available evidence and high-level vegan athletes show that meat isn't necessary. The protein muscles need can come from plants.
