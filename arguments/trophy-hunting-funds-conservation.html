I12

"Trophy hunting funds conservation."

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment and companion animals, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

little trophy hunting money reaches conservation or local communities. A 2013 report on Africa, citing research by pro-hunting bodies, found that hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to communities. And even if the money did arrive, the argument instrumentalizes individuals: it kills an animal to protect the species, as if they were just a specimen.

Short answer

Little money reaches conservation: a 2013 report on Africa, citing research by pro-hunting bodies, found that hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to local communities. Wildlife-watching tourism generates far more money and jobs without killing. And even if the money did arrive, killing an animal to protect the species treats the individual as a mere specimen.

For children

Some people pay lots of money to hunt lions or elephants and take the head home. They say the money protects the other animals. But little of that money reaches the people and parks that look after them. And animals can be protected through wildlife-watching tourism, without killing any.

Ask a question back

"If wildlife-watching tourism generates more income without killing, why would conservation need hunting?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Hunters are the biggest conservationists. The license pays the park rangers."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Wildlife conservation needs money.
  2. P2Trophy hunters pay a lot for licenses.
  3. P3That money funds habitat protection and the fight against poaching.
  4. CSo trophy hunting benefits conservation.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: the evidence suggests that a small share of the money reaches conservation and communities, and there are ways of funding that don't involve killing.

The argument is valid if P3 is true. Its soundness fails because P3 is largely false.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument promises benefits the data don't confirm:

    PromiseWhat the data show
    The revenue funds conservationA small share reaches protected areas and communities
    Hunting is important for the economyThe revenue is a tiny fraction of the GDP of the countries studied
    Without hunting, animals lose their valueWildlife-watching tourism generates more revenue
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Little money reaches communities.

    The 2013 report The $200 million question cited research according to which hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to local communities.

    2. The revenue is small for the economies.

    The same report estimated trophy hunting revenue as a very small fraction of the GDP of the African countries studied.

    3. Hunting removes important individuals.

    Hunters seek the biggest males, with the largest horns or manes, which can affect the social and genetic structure of populations.

    4. Wildlife-watching tourism is an alternative.

    It generates ongoing revenue from the same living animals. Not every area attracts tourists, and that's the strongest point for those who defend hunting; the answer is to fund conservation by other means, not to kill animals to pay for it.

    5. The argument instrumentalizes individuals.

    Killing an animal to "save the species" treats them as a specimen, not as someone (A18).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Economists at Large, *The $200 million question* (2013)Hunting companies passed on about 3% of their revenue to local communities; the revenue is a tiny fraction of GDPCommissioned by animal protection organizations; critics note that some sources aren't public
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: in some areas, hunting is the only income

    The most serious version points out that in remote areas unattractive to tourists, hunting may be the only source of income that gives wildlife value and prevents the habitat being turned into pasture.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    That calls for other forms of funding.

    International funds and payments for conservation can fill that role.

    The case is limited.

    It doesn't justify trophy hunting in general.

    It's still killing for fun.

    The hunter pays for the pleasure of killing, not for conservation.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if trophy hunting funded conservation, it would still be killing an animal for pleasure. The moral question is whether it's fair to take a life for fun, and no spreadsheet answers that.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Conservation needs money, and communities living alongside wildlife need real benefits to support it. In some places, regulated hunting has generated resources and incentives to maintain habitats.

But

But most of the money doesn't reach where it should, there are alternatives that don't involve killing, and the argument forgets the dead animal. Conservation can be funded without trophies.
