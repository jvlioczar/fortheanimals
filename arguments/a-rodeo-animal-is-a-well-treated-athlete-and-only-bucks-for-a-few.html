I17

"A rodeo animal is a well-treated athlete and only bucks for a few seconds"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

rodeo bulls and horses buck because they are provoked: the flank strap, tightened around the groin, and spurs are part of the event, and the animal doesn't choose to compete. Brazil's own rodeo law, from 2002, bans pointed spurs and electric shocks, which shows the risk of cruelty is recognized. Veterinary bodies, such as the Australian Veterinary Association, and welfare organizations, such as the RSPCA, criticize the use of flank straps and spurs. The eight seconds in the arena are the end of a life of transport, confinement and training for it.

Short answer

The animal bucks because it is provoked: the flank strap, tightened around the groin, and spurs are part of the event. They didn't choose to compete, so calling them an athlete is misleading. Brazil's own rodeo law bans pointed spurs and electric shocks, recognizing the risk of cruelty, and veterinary bodies criticize flank straps and spurs. Being well fed doesn't change what happens in the arena, or the years of transport and confinement.

For children

The rodeo bull doesn't buck because he likes to play. He bucks because a tight strap is tied around his belly and because someone is on top of him that he wants to throw off. He didn't choose to be there. You can have a party with music, food and horse rides without making the bull buck in discomfort.

Ask a question back

"If the bull liked bucking, why would the flank strap be needed?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"A rodeo bull is an athlete, worth a fortune, treated better than a lot of people. And he only bucks for eight seconds."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Rodeo animals are valuable, and their owners have an interest in keeping them healthy.
  2. P2The ride lasts a few seconds, and there are welfare rules and inspection.
  3. P3Rodeo supports jobs and is part of rural culture.
  4. CSo rodeo isn't cruelty.

P1 is partly true: a valuable animal tends to receive veterinary care. P2 is true about the duration, but ignores how the bucking is provoked and what happens outside the arena. P3 is true, but says nothing about the animal.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2 and in the step from "well cared for" to "doesn't suffer".

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    ClaimWhat's trueWhat's left out
    "He's an athlete"He gets food and careHe doesn't choose to compete and can't quit
    "It's only eight seconds"The ride is shortFlank strap, spurs, transport, confinement, roping events
    "There are rules"Law 10,519/2002 requires a vet and bans some instrumentsThe law allows flank straps and blunt spurs
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The bucking is provoked.

    The flank strap is tied around the animal's groin and pulled tight as it leaves the chute, and spurs are used by the rider. The industry says the flank strap doesn't injure; critics, including vets, say it causes discomfort and is used precisely for that. Either way, the animal bucks to get rid of something that bothers it.

    2. "Athlete" is a misleading metaphor.

    A human athlete chooses to compete, knows the risks and can stop. The animal is transported, confined and ridden without a choice.

    3. The law recognizes the risk.

    Law 10,519/2002 bans spurs with pointed rowels and any instrument that causes injuries, including electric shock devices. A law only bans what was happening or could happen.

    4. Veterinary and welfare bodies criticize the practice.

    RSPCA Australia opposes rodeos and cites injuries and stress in bulls and bucking horses, and the Australian Veterinary Association is concerned about the use of flank straps and spurs and doesn't support events such as calf rope-and-tie.

    5. There are events besides riding.

    At many rodeos, calves are roped at a run and thrown to the ground, which can injure their necks and legs.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Brazil, Law 10,519/2002Bans spurs with pointed rowels and instruments that cause injuries, including electric shockFederal rodeo law
    RSPCA Australia, rodeosThe Australian Veterinary Association doesn't support steer wrestling or calf rope-and-tie and is concerned about flank straps and spursThe RSPCA opposes rodeos

    RSPCA Australia, bucking horsesDescribes stress, transport and injuries in bucking horsesSame organization
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: without rodeo, these animals wouldn't even exist

    The most serious version says bucking bulls are bred for this, live better than beef cattle and, without rodeo, would never have been born.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Existing isn't a favor that justifies any use.

    The same argument would justify breeding any animal for entertainment.

    Living better than beef cattle isn't living well.

    Comparing with something worse doesn't prove rodeo is acceptable.

    The culture can continue without the suffering.

    Music, food, horse rides and gatherings don't depend on provoking the animal.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to provoke an animal into trying to throw someone off, for an audience's entertainment. Caring for the animal well outside the arena doesn't answer that.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that many rodeo animals receive veterinary care, that the ride lasts a few seconds, and that rodeo matters to many communities and jobs.

But

But the animal bucks because it is provoked, doesn't choose to be there, and the law itself recognizes the risk of cruelty. The festival can go on without it.
