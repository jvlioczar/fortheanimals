I19

"Circus animals are well treated and part of the family"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

many circus performers have a real bond with the animals, but traveling life doesn't meet the needs of animals such as elephants, lions and bears: constant transport, small cages and training for acts they wouldn't do on their own. That's why more than 40 countries restrict the use of wild animals in circuses, and in Brazil several states and cities have banned it. Circuses without animals, such as Cirque du Soleil, show that the show doesn't depend on them.

Short answer

Many performers have a real bond with the animals, but traveling life doesn't meet the needs of elephants, lions and bears: constant transport, small cages and training for acts they wouldn't do alone. That's why more than 40 countries restrict wild animals in circuses, and in Brazil several states and cities have banned them. Circuses without animals, such as Cirque du Soleil, show the show doesn't depend on them.

For children

In the wild, an elephant walks many kilometers a day with its family. At the circus, it travels from city to city in a truck and spends a lot of time locked up, to do tricks it wouldn't do on its own. Today there are beautiful circuses, with acrobats and clowns, and no animals at all.

Ask a question back

"If an elephant walks kilometers a day in the wild, which family can give it that while traveling in a truck?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Circus animals are part of the family. The performers have looked after them since they were little, they travel together and they're well treated."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Circus animals are cared for by the same families for generations.
  2. P2They get food and veterinary care.
  3. P3The bond with their trainers shows they don't suffer.
  4. CSo using animals in circuses is acceptable.

P1 and P2 may be true. P3 confuses bond with welfare: an animal can be attached to those who keep it and still live badly.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The animal needsThe traveling circus offers
    Large areas to walk and exploreCages and temporary enclosures
    Company of its own speciesIsolation or artificial groups
    A stable routineConstant travel and change
    Natural behaviorTraining for artificial acts
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. A bond isn't welfare.

    Animals can become attached to those who keep them and still suffer from confinement and transport. The bond doesn't change the living conditions.

    2. These animals' needs don't fit in a circus.

    Elephants, big cats and bears need space, movement and company that traveling life can't provide.

    3. Many countries and cities have decided to ban it.

    More than 40 countries restrict the use of wild animals in circuses, and Bolivia was the first to ban all animals, in 2009. Brazil has no federal law, but several states and cities ban it.

    4. The show doesn't depend on animals.

    Hugely successful contemporary circuses, such as Cirque du Soleil, have never used animals.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Four Paws, worldwide circus bansMore than 40 countries restrict the use of wild animals in circusesAnimal protection organization
    Bans on circus animals, on WikipediaOverview of bans by country, including Bolivia's in 2009Overview
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: circuses with animals are a tradition and a livelihood

    The most serious version points out that circus families have lived from this work for generations, and that bans threaten a cultural tradition and these families' livelihood.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The tradition can continue without animals.

    Circus art is much bigger than animal acts (D1).

    The livelihood can be kept.

    Many circuses have adapted and keep running without animals.

    The question is about the animals.

    A valuable tradition doesn't license keeping animals in conditions that don't meet their needs.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to keep animals caged and on the road to entertain the public, when the same show can exist without them.

Contradictions

Says the animal is family.

And keeps it in cages and trucks.

Values circus tradition.

And ignores that it carries on without animals.

Wants children to know animals.

And shows animals doing what they'd never do in the wild.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that many performers look after the animals with affection, that there are real bonds, and that the circus is an important cultural tradition and many families' livelihood.

But

But wild animals' needs don't fit a traveling life, and circus art lives on without them.
