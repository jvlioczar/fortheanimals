I4

"Sheep need to be shorn"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

wool sheep need shearing because they were bred to produce excess wool; their wild ancestors shed their coats naturally. The need was created by the industry itself. And wool production includes practices such as mulesing, cutting away skin from lambs without anesthesia, still legal in Australia, the largest producer of merino wool, and slaughtering sheep when production drops.

Short answer

Wool sheep need shearing because they were bred to produce excess wool; their ancestors shed naturally. It's the same pattern as dairy cows (C11). Besides, wool production includes practices such as mulesing, cutting skin from lambs' hindquarters, still legal and often done without anesthesia in Australia, and slaughtering sheep when they produce less.

For children

Long ago, sheep shed their coats on their own, like dogs in spring. People chose sheep with more and more wool, until they couldn't shed it anymore. Now they need to be shorn. That isn't a favor: it's a problem people created.

Ask a question back

"If sheep were bred not to shed their wool, is shearing a favor or a fix for a problem we created?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Without shearing, sheep die of heat. Wool is natural and sustainable."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Wool sheep don't shed their fleece on their own.
  2. P2Without shearing, they suffer from heat, dirt and infestations.
  3. P3Shearing relieves that suffering.
  4. CSo using wool is making use of necessary care for sheep.

P1, P2 and P3 are true for today's wool sheep. The problem lies in the conclusion: the need was created by breeding for wool, and the industry includes practices that cause suffering.

The argument is valid for existing sheep. Its soundness fails because it leaves out where the need came from and what happens in production.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument starts in the middle of the story:

    What the argument showsWhat comes before
    The sheep needs shearingIt was bred not to shed its wool
    Shearing relieves the heatExcess wool causes the heat
    The wool is put to useIt's sold, and that's the point of raising them
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The need was created.

    Primitive and wild sheep shed their coats naturally. Continuously growing wool is the result of human breeding.

    2. Mulesing still exists.

    Mulesing removes wrinkled skin from lambs' hindquarters to reduce flystrike. In Australia it's still legal, and pain relief is only mandatory in some states. The RSPCA in Australia itself calls for it to end.

    3. Commercial shearing can be violent.

    Shearing is done at speed, often paid per animal, and investigations have recorded injuries and abuse.

    4. The sheep are slaughtered.

    When wool production drops, sheep are sold for slaughter. Wool and meat are part of the same system.

    5. There are alternatives.

    Cotton, linen, hemp, recycled fibers and other materials keep people warm and clothed without involving animals.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    RSPCA Australia, mulesingMulesing is still legal in Australia; the RSPCA advocates a transition to flystrike-resistant sheep and an end to the practiceAn animal welfare organization
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: what about the sheep that already exist?

    The most serious version grants that the need was created, but says existing sheep need shearing, and using their wool harms no one.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Caring for existing sheep doesn't require buying wool.

    Sanctuaries shear sheep and don't sell the wool to fund new breeding.

    Buying wool funds new sheep.

    Demand sustains breeding, selection and slaughter.

    Commercial wool comes from the industry.

    Not from rescued sheep.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Shearing is necessary because we created sheep who depend on it. The moral question isn't whether we should care for the sheep that exist, but whether we should keep breeding and killing animals to clothe ourselves, when there are alternatives.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that today's wool sheep need shearing, and that not shearing them would cause suffering. Some farmers treat their sheep well, and careful shearing isn't cruel in itself.

But

But the need was created by breeding for profit, and the wool industry includes mulesing and slaughter. Caring for the sheep that exist is different from continuing to breed new ones to clothe ourselves.
