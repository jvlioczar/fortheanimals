L21

"Comparing animal farming to slavery or the Holocaust is offensive"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about who defends veganism, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the question depended on who defends animals, vegans being pleasant and consistent would make the argument valid, and being annoying and inconsistent would make it invalid. But the animals suffer just the same, whoever the messenger is.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the objection is substantially right: these comparisons can hurt people and communities who suffered atrocities, and they should be made with great care, or avoided. But structural analogy must be distinguished from moral equivalence. Pointing out similarities of mechanism, such as treating beings as things, isn't saying the victims are worth the same or that the suffering is equal. And the case for animals doesn't depend on these comparisons.

Short answer

These comparisons can hurt, and it's fair to ask for care with them, or to avoid them. But two things need separating: pointing out similarities of mechanism, such as treating sentient beings as things, isn't saying the victims are worth the same. Some people close to these atrocities have made such comparisons themselves. In any case, the defense of animals doesn't depend on these analogies: it stands on its own.

For children

Some very sad things happened to people in history, and it's important to remember them with respect. Comparing those tragedies with other things can hurt those who suffered. That's why it's better to talk about animals without using those comparisons. Animals deserve care for their own sake.

Ask a question back

"Setting the comparisons aside, what do you think of what happens to the animals?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"You trivialize the Holocaust. That comparison is racist."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Slavery and the Holocaust were atrocities against human beings, whose memory must be respected.
  2. P2Comparing these atrocities with the treatment of animals can suggest the human victims are worth no more than animals.
  3. P3That suggestion offends descendants and survivors, and reproduces the historical dehumanization they suffered.
  4. CSo these comparisons are offensive and should be rejected.

P1 is true. P2 and P3 point to a real risk: in many situations, these comparisons hurt. The problem is concluding from this that the question of animals can be dismissed.

The argument is valid as a criticism of a way of communicating. It fails when used to reject the moral question about animals.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument mixes a legitimate criticism of form with a rejection of content:

    QuestionAnswer
    Can these comparisons hurt?Yes, and they should be used with care or avoided
    Is comparing mechanisms equating victims?Not necessarily: analogy isn't equivalence
    Does the question of animals depend on these comparisons?No
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Analogy isn't equivalence.

    Comparing structures, such as objectification, property and the denial of interests, isn't claiming the victims are worth the same.

    2. Criticizing the form doesn't refute the content.

    If the comparison is inappropriate, the answer is not to use it, not to dismiss the question of animals.

    3. Some people close to these atrocities made the comparison.

    Writers and people who lived through atrocities have drawn comparisons of this kind. That doesn't oblige anyone to make them, but it shows they aren't necessarily disrespectful.

    4. The case for animals stands without analogies.

    Sentience, the needlessness of the harm and moral consistency are enough (A8).

    5. Care is a duty for those who defend animals.

    Especially toward communities still living with the consequences of these atrocities.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Spiegel, *The Dreaded Comparison* (1988)Discusses the comparison between human slavery and animal exploitationMirror Books; written by an author who defends the comparison
    Patterson, *Eternal Treblinka* (2002)Discusses historical parallels between the treatment of animals and the HolocaustLantern Books; a controversial work
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: the harm of the comparison outweighs the benefit

    The most serious version says that even if the analogy is structurally valid, it hurts so much and alienates so many people that it shouldn't be used.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It may be right about strategy.

    Many animal advocates agree and avoid these comparisons.

    It's about communication, not about the animals.

    The moral question remains.

    There are other ways to speak.

    Animal suffering can be described on its own terms.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even without any historical comparison, the moral question remains: animals feel, and we make them suffer and die without need. Is that fair?

What the argument gets right

What is true

This objection is largely right. Comparisons with slavery and the Holocaust can deeply hurt people and communities still living with the consequences of these atrocities, and animal advocates have used them disrespectfully. Care here is a duty.

But

But criticizing the form doesn't erase the content. The defense of animals can and should be made without instrumentalizing human suffering, and it still stands.
