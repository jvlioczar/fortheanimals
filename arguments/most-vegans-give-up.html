L10

"Most vegans give up"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about who defends veganism, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the question depended on who defends animals, vegans being pleasant and consistent would make the argument valid, and being annoying and inconsistent would make it invalid. But the animals suffer just the same, whoever the messenger is.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

difficulty sticking with something says nothing about whether it's morally right. A 2014 Faunalytics survey found that about 84% of vegetarians and vegans abandon the diet, many in the first months. That shows change is hard and support is lacking, not that the change is wrong. Many people also give up trying to quit smoking, and no one concludes smoking is good.

Short answer

It's true that many people give up: a 2014 Faunalytics survey found that about 84% of vegetarians and vegans abandon the diet, many in the first months. The reasons most cited were dissatisfaction with the food, health and social issues, which shows a lack of support and information. But that says change is hard, not that change is wrong. Many people give up trying to quit smoking, and no one concludes smoking is good.

For children

Lots of people try to learn a musical instrument and give up. Does that mean playing music is bad? No! It means it's hard and some people need more help. Veganism is similar: some people give up, but that doesn't change the fact that animals feel.

Ask a question back

"If many people give up something hard, does that make it wrong to try?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Almost everyone goes back to eating meat. Not even you can stick with it."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Most people who adopt a vegetarian or vegan diet abandon it.
  2. P2If so many give up, the diet must be hard, unsatisfying or harmful.
  3. CSo veganism isn't a realistic or adequate option.

P1 is supported by surveys. P2 has a point: the difficulty is real. The problem lies in the conclusion: how hard it is to keep up a practice doesn't say whether it's right.

The argument is valid as an observation about difficulty. It fails as a moral argument.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument confuses difficulty with wrongness:

    FactWhat it showsWhat it doesn't show
    Many give upThat keeping up the change is hardThat the change is wrong
    Many give up earlyThat the initial phase is the hardestThat the diet is inadequate
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Difficulty isn't wrongness.

    Many good practices are hard to keep up, such as quitting smoking or exercising.

    2. The reasons point to a lack of support.

    In the Faunalytics survey, the main reasons were dissatisfaction with the food (32%), health (26%) and social issues (13%). Many problems could be solved with recipes, planning and community.

    3. Many give up early.

    About a third of those who gave up kept the diet for three months or less, the hardest phase.

    4. Giving up calls for support, not abandoning the question.

    If people give up for lack of recipes, planning and company, the answer is to offer those.

    5. The moral question doesn't depend on adherence.

    Animals still feel, however many people manage to change.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Faunalytics, study of current and former vegetarians and vegans84% of vegetarians and vegans give up; 34% within three months; the reasons most cited are dissatisfaction with the food, health and social issuesA survey of more than 11,000 adults in the US
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: giving up signals health problems

    The most serious version points out that 26% of those who gave up cited health, and that this may indicate the diet doesn't suit many people.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Health problems usually come from lack of planning.

    Such as missing B12 or too little energy (G10).

    Many had no supervision.

    Which calls for more information, not abandonment.

    The moral question remains.

    Even where there are real difficulties, they justify exceptions, not a general rule (D3).

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The number of people who give up shows that change is hard, and that those who try deserve support. But it doesn't answer the question about the animals. Those who give up still have the same moral reason that led them to try.

Contradictions

Doesn't use the argument for other hard habits.

Such as quitting smoking.

Values persistence in other areas.

Study, sport, work.

Recognizes the diet needs support.

Which calls for help, not abandonment.

What the argument gets right

What is true

The high dropout rate is real, and it says something important: change is hard, and many people don't get the support they need. Animal advocates should take this seriously, offering recipes, nutrition information and a welcoming community.

But

But the difficulty doesn't change the moral question. Animals still feel, and everyone who tries, even if they stumble, is already answering that question.
