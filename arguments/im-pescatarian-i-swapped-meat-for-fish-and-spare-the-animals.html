J11

"I'm pescatarian; I swapped meat for fish and spare the animals"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about cost, practicality and the economy, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended for being cheap, practical or good for the economy, any situation in which exploitation were cheaper or created more jobs would justify it. No one accepts this reasoning when the victims are human: exploiting people was once profitable too.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

swapping meat for fish usually increases the number of animals killed, rather than reducing it. Fish are sentient, and they are much smaller than cattle and pigs: the same amount of food involves many more individuals. According to a 2024 study published in the journal Animal Welfare, between 1.1 and 2.2 trillion wild fish are caught each year, not counting discards; and shrimp farming kills, according to Rethink Priorities, about 440 billion animals a year. In fishing, most die by suffocation or crushing, and many farmed fish are fed with feed made from other fish.

Reply

Fish are animals too, and they feel pain. And they're much smaller than a cow or a pig: for the same amount of food, you eat many more individuals. Between 1.1 and 2.2 trillion wild fish are caught each year, and shrimp farming alone kills about 440 billion a year. Most fish die by suffocation or crushing. So if you swap meat for fish, the number of animals killed on your account will probably go up. If the idea is to do less harm to animals, why swap one death for many?

For children

Fish feel fear and pain too. And they're small: to make a plate of fish, many more animals die than for a plate of beef. To spare the animals, the best thing is to swap meat for beans, lentils and other plant foods.

Ask a question back

"How many fish or shrimp does it take to make the same amount of food as one cow?"

How it can show up

The strongest version

How it is said

"I stopped eating red meat and chicken; now I only eat fish and seafood. That way I spare the animals and look after my health."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Cattle, pigs and poultry have rich emotional lives and suffer a great deal in animal farming.
  2. P2Fish and seafood feel less, or nothing.
  3. P3Someone who swaps meat for fish stops funding the suffering of farmed animals.
  4. CSo being pescatarian spares the animals.

P1 is true. P2 is false for fish, whose capacity to feel pain has strong evidence, and uncertain for shrimp and other crustaceans, which are increasingly included in animal protection laws. P3 ignores that the swap involves many more animals.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2 and in the conclusion.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    SwapWhat changes for the animals
    Beef for fishMany more individuals per kilo of food
    Meat for shrimpHundreds or thousands of animals per kilo
    Farmed fishMany are fed with feed made from wild fish
    FishingAnimals caught unintentionally and discarded dead
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Fish are sentient.

    There is strong evidence that fish feel pain, and the 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness recognizes a realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates and in decapod crustaceans such as shrimp and crabs (see A2).

    2. The swap multiplies victims.

    A fish or a shrimp provides much less meat than a cow or a pig. So the same amount of food involves many more animals.

    3. The scale is enormous.

    According to Mood and Brooke (2024), between 1.1 and 2.2 trillion wild fish were caught each year on average from 2000 to 2019, not counting fish caught illegally or discarded. Rethink Priorities estimates that about 440 billion farmed shrimp are killed each year.

    4. The deaths are long.

    In fishing, most fish die by suffocating out of water, crushed in nets or on ice. In farming, slaughter without stunning is still common.

    5. Fish farming also consumes fish.

    Part of the feed for farmed fish and shrimp is made from the meal and oil of wild fish.

  3. c

    Data and sources

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: fish is good for your health

    The most serious version says fish is recommended by health bodies, especially for omega-3, and that swapping red meat for it is an improvement for the eater.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The question was about the animals.

    The argument claimed to spare animals, and the swap increases the number of victims.

    The omega-3 in fish comes from algae.

    There are algae-based omega-3 supplements, with no fish (see G7).

    There is a swap that spares animals.

    Beans, lentils, chickpeas and tofu replace meat without multiplying victims.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    People who swap meat for fish usually want to spare animals. The question is whether the swap achieves that goal, and, kilo for kilo, it usually does the opposite.

Contradictions

And the swap increases the number of animals killed.

Cares about the cow's suffering.

And fish feel pain.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that animal farming causes great suffering to cattle, pigs and poultry, and that wanting to stop funding it is a good reason.

But

But fish and shrimp are sentient animals too, and swapping meat for them usually multiplies the victims. The path that spares animals is swapping meat for plants. Eating fewer animals, without swapping some for smaller ones, is better than doing nothing, and can be a step toward veganism; but for the animals who are still killed, the harm is still terrible.
