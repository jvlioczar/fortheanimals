I18

"The fighting bull lives free in the field and dies fighting"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the fighting bull lives a few years in the field, but a good life doesn't justify the death that follows: in the Spanish bullfight he is wounded by the picador's lance, the banderillas and the sword, in front of an audience, over about 20 minutes. It isn't a fair fight, because the bull is deliberately weakened before the final confrontation. And the idea that the breed would disappear without bullfighting doesn't help any particular bull. Colombia passed a law in 2024 banning bullfights from 2027, upheld by its Constitutional Court, and Spain abolished its National Bullfighting Prize in 2024.

Short answer

It's true that the fighting bull lives a few years in the field. But a good life doesn't justify the death: in the Spanish bullfight he is wounded by the picador's lance, the banderillas and the sword, for about 20 minutes, for an audience. It isn't a fair fight, because he is deliberately weakened. And the breed disappearing harms no bull that already exists. Colombia has banned bullfights from 2027, and Spain abolished its national bullfighting prize in 2024.

For children

In a bullfight, the bull is taken into an arena full of people, stabbed several times and then killed with a sword. He doesn't understand what's happening and didn't choose to be there. Having lived well before doesn't make his pain hurt any less.

Ask a question back

"If the fight were fair, why would the bull need to be weakened first?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"The fighting bull lives four or five years loose in the pasture, like a king. He dies fighting, on his feet, not in a slaughterhouse. And without bullfighting, the breed dies out."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1The fighting bull lives for years in freedom, on extensive pastures, better than most beef cattle.
  2. P2In the arena he fights and can injure or kill the bullfighter, who also risks his life.
  3. P3Without bullfighting, the breed and the pastures where it lives would disappear.
  4. CSo bullfighting is more dignified for the bull than ordinary slaughter, and should be preserved.

P1 is largely true. P2 is true about the bullfighter's risk, but the bull doesn't choose to fight and is weakened beforehand. P3 is debatable and, even if true, benefits no individual bull.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2 and P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    In the Spanish bullfight, the bull's death follows stages:

    StageWhat happens to the bull
    Tercio de varasThe mounted picador drives a lance into the neck muscles, weakening him and making him lower his head
    Tercio de banderillasPairs of banderillas, sticks with barbed points, are driven into his back
    Tercio de muerteThe bullfighter tries to kill him with the sword; if he fails, he uses other blows until death
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. A good life doesn't justify a painful death.

    The argument compares bullfighting with the slaughterhouse, not with life. A death less bad than another is still an imposed death, and this one is carried out with prolonged pain and fear, for entertainment.

    2. It isn't a fair fight.

    The bull doesn't choose to enter the arena, and the first stages exist to weaken him before the final confrontation. It is almost always he who dies.

    3. Preserving the breed doesn't help the bull.

    A breed feels nothing; each bull does. Keeping a breed so that its members die in the arena isn't a benefit to them. And pastures and breeds can be preserved by other means, if society wants.

    4. Society is changing.

    Colombia passed Law 2385 in 2024, banning bullfights from July 2027, and the Constitutional Court declared it constitutional in 2025. In Spain, the Ministry of Culture abolished the National Bullfighting Prize in 2024, citing rejection by broad sectors of society; there was a strong backlash, and the Senate and an industry foundation created an alternative prize. In Portugal, the bull isn't killed in the arena, with a legal exception in Barrancos.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: bullfighting is art and heritage

    The most serious version says bullfighting is an art with centuries of history, recognized by law as cultural heritage in Spain, and that judging it from outside means not understanding its meaning.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Being heritage doesn't make a practice fair.

    Many old practices were abandoned once the harm they caused was recognized.

    The artistic meaning is for the audience, not for the bull.

    What the bull experiences is pain and fear, whatever it means to those watching.

    Criticism also comes from within.

    A large part of Spanish society and of other countries with a bullfighting tradition rejects bullfights, and it was those countries' parliaments and courts that approved restrictions.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to wound and kill an animal, slowly and in front of an audience, so that death becomes a spectacle. The years in the pasture don't change the answer.

Contradictions

And wounds him several times before killing him.

And deliberately weakens the opponent.

Wants to preserve the fighting bull.

And breeds him to die in the arena.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that the fighting bull usually lives for years on extensive pastures, in better conditions than many beef cattle, and that bullfighting has centuries of history and meaning for many people.

But

But a good life doesn't justify a slow, painful death turned into a spectacle, and preserving a breed is no benefit to any bull. Several countries are already leaving this tradition behind.
