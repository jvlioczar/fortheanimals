I3

"Leather is just a byproduct of meat."

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment and companion animals, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

profitable byproducts help pay for slaughter. In the United States, hides traditionally made up about 6% to 8% of the total value of a steer, and were the most valuable byproduct; with the recent fall in prices, the share reached about 1% in 2020. Even small, it's revenue: buying leather helps pay for slaughter and sustains the system. Besides, some leather comes from animals raised partly for their skin, and from highly polluting tanning processes.

Short answer

Leather isn't waste: it's a product that gets sold. In the United States, it traditionally made up about 6% to 8% of the total value of a steer, and was the most valuable byproduct; with falling prices, it reached about 1% in 2020. Even so, it's revenue that helps pay for slaughter. Buying leather funds part of livestock farming. And tanning is one of the most polluting industrial processes, using chromium and other chemicals.

For children

Leather is a cow's skin. People who sell meat also sell the skin, and make money from it. That money helps pay for raising and killing more cows. So buying leather also helps keep going the system that kills the animals.

Ask a question back

"If leather had no value, why would slaughterhouses sell it?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"The leather would be thrown away. Better to use the whole animal. Synthetic leather is plastic."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Cattle are slaughtered mainly for meat.
  2. P2If the hide weren't used, it would be thrown away.
  3. P3Using what would be thrown away is better than wasting it.
  4. CSo using leather causes no additional harm and is even a form of respect.

P1 is true for most cattle. P2 is partly true: there are times when hides are worth little. The problem lies in the conclusion: leather has economic value, and that value helps fund slaughter.

The argument is valid if leather had no value. Its soundness fails because it does.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument treats as disposable something that has a price:

    Source of livestock revenueWeight
    MeatMost of it
    LeatherTraditionally the most valuable byproduct; about 6% to 8% of a steer's value in the US, with large price swings
    Other byproducts (fat, bones, offal)The rest
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Leather has economic value.

    It's sold to tanneries. When the price of hides falls, the price paid for cattle falls too, which shows it's part of the calculation.

    2. Byproducts subsidize the main product.

    Byproduct revenue lowers the cost of meat and makes livestock farming more profitable. Buying leather funds the system.

    3. Not all leather comes from beef cattle.

    Some leathers come from animals raised partly or mainly for their skin, such as crocodiles, ostriches and some goats.

    4. Tanning pollutes heavily.

    Chrome tanning produces toxic waste that pollutes rivers and affects workers in many countries.

    5. There are alternatives.

    Plant-based, recycled and better-quality synthetic materials are available, and choosing among them can take environmental impact into account.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Hide Staff Paper, AgEcon SearchTraditionally, hides contributed about 6% to 8% of the total value of fed cattle in the US; in 2020, the share fell to about 1%Checked from the summary; the value varies widely with the market
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: leather is worth little today

    The most serious version points out that in some recent years the value of hides fell so much that many were discarded, and that under those conditions using leather funds nothing.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The fall proves leather counts.

    When hides are worth less, cattle are worth less: the revenue is part of the calculation.

    Buying leather sustains demand.

    It's precisely demand that keeps its value up.

    The waste argument applies to meat too.

    And it doesn't justify raising and killing more animals.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Leather comes from the skin of an animal who was killed, and selling it helps pay for that death. If there are alternatives for clothing and shoes, the moral question remains: what justifies funding the slaughter?

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that most cattle are slaughtered for meat, and that the value of hides is sometimes low. It's also true that some synthetic materials have an environmental impact, and that choosing among alternatives deserves attention.

But

But leather is part of livestock revenue, and buying it helps pay for slaughter. There are ways to dress and wear shoes without funding the death of animals.
