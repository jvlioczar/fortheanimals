J10

"I'm vegetarian for the animals; I already do my part"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about cost, practicality and the economy, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended for being cheap, practical or good for the economy, any situation in which exploitation were cheaper or created more jobs would justify it. No one accepts this reasoning when the victims are human: exploiting people was once profitable too.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

giving up meat spares many animals, and it's a real step. But egg and milk production also kills: in the egg industry, male chicks are killed at birth and hens are slaughtered when laying declines; in dairy, the cow must give birth to produce, and so she is made pregnant again and again, usually by artificial insemination; calves are separated from their mothers, almost always within the first hours or the first day of life, which causes signs of distress in both; and cows are slaughtered after a few years. Someone who replaces meat calories with eggs may even increase the number of animals killed. Vegetarianism for the animals is a beginning, not a destination.

Reply

Giving up meat spares many animals, and that counts. But do you know what happens in egg and milk production? Male chicks are killed at birth, hens and cows are slaughtered when production falls, and, in dairy, the cow is made pregnant again and again, usually by artificial insemination, and the calf is taken from her shortly after birth, which makes both of them suffer. If you swap meat for lots of eggs, you may even increase the number of animals killed. Vegetarianism is a good start. But if you gave up meat so that animals wouldn't suffer and die because of you, the same reason applies to eggs and milk.

For children

Not eating meat already helps many animals. But to have eggs and milk, sad things also happen: male chicks don't lay eggs and are killed, and, to have milk, cows are made pregnant one time after another, and calves are taken from their mothers right after birth so the milk can go to people. Mother and calf both suffer from the separation. You can go further and help these animals too.

Ask a question back

"What happens to the male chicks and the calves in producing the eggs and milk you eat?"

How it can show up

The strongest version

How it is said

"I've been vegetarian for years, for the animals. I don't eat any meat. I already do my part; veganism is excessive."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Eating meat requires killing the animal.
  2. P2Eggs and milk can be obtained without killing the animal who produces them.
  3. P3Someone who doesn't eat meat already avoids most of the deaths caused by food.
  4. CSo being vegetarian already fulfills the essence of our duty to animals.

P1 is true. P2 is true in theory but false in commercial production, which kills male chicks, calves and the hens and cows themselves when they stop producing. P3 depends on what replaces the meat.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2 and P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    ProductAnimal who diesWhen
    EggsMale chicks, who don't lay eggsAt birth, in the hatchery
    EggsLaying hensWhen laying declines, usually at about one to two years old
    MilkCalves, especially malesShortly after birth, for veal or beef (see B6)
    MilkDairy cowsWhen production falls, after a few years

    And before death, milk exacts another price: cows are made pregnant for each production cycle, and their calves are taken from them.

  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Eggs and milk don't come without deaths.

    In commercial production, male chicks of laying breeds have no value and are killed at birth. The practice is so common that countries such as Germany and France have banned it in recent years, requiring the industry to determine sex while still in the egg. The data on deaths in egg and milk production are in B5.

    2. Replacing meat with eggs can increase deaths.

    Over her life, a laying hen provides more calories than a broiler chicken, but far fewer than a cow. Someone who swaps beef calories for eggs may end up involved in the deaths of more animals than before.

    3. Suffering in life counts too.

    Caged hens and cows separated from their calves live in conditions that are among the worst in animal farming. Not killing directly doesn't mean not causing suffering.

    4. In dairy, pregnancy and separation repeat.

    A cow only produces milk after giving birth, and to keep production going, dairy farms usually aim for one calf per year: the cow is inseminated again, usually artificially, a few months after each calving. On most commercial farms, the calf is separated from the mother within the first hours or the first day of life. Separation causes stress responses, such as calling and searching, which are stronger the longer mother and calf have been together, and that's why the industry tends to separate early. Separating early reduces the immediate reaction, but doesn't remove the harm: in one experiment, calves showed signs of negative mood after being separated from their mothers, and a systematic review concluded that prolonged contact has positive effects for calves, such as more normal social behavior and fewer abnormal behaviors, although the authors note that studies are still few.

    5. Milk is a debated case.

    Some calculations say milk causes few deaths per calorie; others, that it causes more cattle deaths per calorie than meat. The comparison depends on the assumptions, and the guide doesn't treat it as settled.

    6. "I already do my part" is a form of moral licensing.

    Having taken one step doesn't excuse the next, when it's possible.

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: vegetarians spare far more animals than omnivores

    The most serious version says that, on average, vegetarians cause far fewer deaths than meat eaters, and that demanding more can push people away from the step they've already taken.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's true on average, but not automatically.

    It depends on what replaces the meat.

    Sparing more isn't sparing all.

    The animals of egg and milk production are still exploited and killed.

    Recognizing the step doesn't require stopping there.

    You can value vegetarianism and, at the same time, show what's still missing.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The reasons that lead someone to stop eating meat for the animals are the same reasons that apply to male chicks, calves, hens and cows. The question isn't whether vegetarianism helps, because it does, but whether it fulfills what those very reasons ask.

Contradictions

Stopped eating meat for the animals.

And ignores the animals killed for eggs and milk.

Says eggs don't kill.

And the industry kills male chicks.

Drinks milk without thinking of the cow.

Who is made pregnant again for each cycle and loses her calf right after birth.

Already does their part.

And the part that's missing rests on the same reason.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that giving up meat spares many animals, that it's a hard and real step, and that vegetarians, on average, cause far fewer deaths than meat eaters. Cutting down is better than doing nothing, especially as a step toward veganism.

But

But for the animals who are still exploited, the harm is still terrible: egg and milk production also kills and causes suffering, and swapping meat for eggs can even increase deaths. The same reasons that led to vegetarianism lead to the next step.
