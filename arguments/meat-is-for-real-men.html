L12

"Meat is for real men"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about who defends veganism, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the question depended on who defends animals, vegans being pleasant and consistent would make the argument valid, and being annoying and inconsistent would make it invalid. But the animals suffer just the same, whoever the messenger is.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's a gender stereotype. The link between meat and masculinity is cultural: a 2012 study showed, across six experiments, that many people associate meat, especially red meat, with masculinity, and that this shows up even in the grammar of several languages. But masculinity is neither a moral nor a nutritional criterion. Vegan strength athletes, fighters and bodybuilders show that strength doesn't depend on meat.

Short answer

The link between meat and masculinity is cultural, and it's been documented: a 2012 study showed, across six experiments, that many people associate red meat with men. But masculinity is neither a moral nor a nutritional criterion. There are vegan strength athletes, fighters and bodybuilders. Being a man doesn't depend on what's on the plate, and courage can also mean changing when you realize something is wrong.

For children

There's no such thing as boys' food or girls' food. Boys and girls, men and women can eat anything. There are very strong male athletes who don't eat any meat at all. Being strong and brave has nothing to do with what you eat.

Ask a question back

"What's masculine about paying for an animal to be killed?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Real men eat meat. Salad is for women."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1In many cultures, meat is associated with strength, virility and the male role.
  2. P2These associations are part of many men's identity.
  3. P3Giving up meat threatens that identity.
  4. CSo it's understandable and legitimate for men to reject veganism.

P1 is true and documented. So is P2. P3 has a real psychological point. The problem lies in the conclusion: a cultural association isn't a moral reason, and the idea of masculinity can change.

The argument is valid as a psychological explanation. It fails as a justification.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument uses a cultural association as a justification:

    ClaimStatus
    Meat is associated with masculinityTrue, in many cultures
    So men should eat meatDoesn't follow: a cultural association isn't a norm
    Men who don't eat meat are weakFalse: there are high-level vegan athletes
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's a stereotype.

    The link between meat and masculinity is cultural, not biological or moral.

    2. The association is documented, and that explains it.

    Rozin and colleagues showed in 2012 that many people associate meat, especially red meat, with masculinity, and that this shows up in several languages with grammatical gender.

    3. Strength doesn't depend on meat.

    With enough protein, vegans gain mass and strength equally or very similarly to omnivores (G9).

    4. Masculinity isn't a moral criterion.

    What's right or wrong doesn't depend on whether it's associated with men or women.

    5. Ideas of masculinity change.

    Many behaviors once seen as "unmanly" are now common among men.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Rozin et al., 2012, *Journal of Consumer Research*Six studies show a metaphorical association between mammal meat and masculinityIncludes an analysis of 23 languages with grammatical gender
    Hevia-Larraín and colleagues, 2021, *Sports Medicine*With the same protein intake, vegans and omnivores had the same gains in mass and strengthAlso used in G9
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: masculine identity matters to many men

    The most serious version points out that for many men masculine identity is central, and that proposals threatening it provoke rejection, which harms the cause.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's a question of communication.

    And it can be addressed with examples of strong, respected vegan men.

    Identity can include other values.

    Such as protecting the weaker.

    The moral question remains.

    No identity answers it.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The stereotype explains part of the resistance, but doesn't justify the animals' deaths. The moral question is the same for men and women: is it fair to make animals suffer and die when we don't need to?

Contradictions

Admires strong athletes.

What the argument gets right

What is true

The link between meat and masculinity is real, deep and documented, and it explains part of the resistance to veganism among men. Understanding this matters for communicating better and showing examples of vegan men.

But

But a cultural association isn't a moral reason. Masculinity can include strength, courage and also the choice not to cause unnecessary suffering.
