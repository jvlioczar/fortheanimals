G10

"I know someone who got sick being vegan"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

one case doesn't refute the reviews and positions of nutrition bodies, and what really happened is rarely known. Often the diet was poorly planned, lacked B12 or energy, or there was another health problem. A poorly planned vegan diet can do harm, just like a poorly planned diet with meat. The problem is planning, not the absence of meat.

Short answer

I'm sorry about your cousin. But one case doesn't show the cause: often the diet was poorly planned, lacked B12 or energy, or there was another problem. A Faunalytics survey of former vegetarians and vegans showed that most gave up within the first year, many without supervision. People also get sick eating meat, and no one concludes from that that meat is dangerous. Scientific reviews and nutrition bodies show that a well-planned vegan diet is adequate.

For children

Sometimes someone gets sick while eating in a new way and thinks the new way is to blame. But maybe they were missing a vitamin or eating too little. A person who eats only bread also gets sick, but no one says the problem is not eating meat. The important thing is to eat well and eat a variety of foods.

Ask a question back

"Was she supervised by a dietitian, and was she taking B12?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"My cousin got sick. An influencer quit veganism for health reasons. I saw an ex-vegan's story."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1I know people who got sick or felt unwell being vegan.
  2. P2They got better when they went back to eating meat.
  3. P3This indicates the vegan diet doesn't work for everyone.
  4. CSo the vegan diet is risky.

P1 and P2 may be true. The problem lies in P3: individual cases don't show the cause, and improvement may come from any change in diet, such as more energy, more variety or correcting a deficiency.

The argument is valid as a testimony. It fails as proof about the vegan diet in general.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The personal case has many possible explanations:

    Possible causeIs it the lack of meat?
    Lack of B12No: it's lack of a supplement
    Insufficient energy intakeNo: it's eating too little
    A diet based on few foodsNo: it's lack of variety
    Another, unrelated illnessNo
    The effect of expectations or social stressNo
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's anecdotal evidence.

    Individual cases don't show the cause of anything. Reviews and the positions of nutrition bodies are based on many studies (D9).

    2. The case is rarely investigated.

    Whoever tells the story almost never knows whether there was supervision, blood tests or supplementation.

    3. People usually quit quickly.

    The Faunalytics survey showed that most former vegetarians and vegans gave up within the first year, and many within three months. The main reasons included dissatisfaction with the food, health and social issues.

    4. Getting better after a change doesn't prove the cause.

    Whoever goes back to eating meat usually also starts eating more and more varied food. The improvement may come from that.

    5. Public stories aren't a sample.

    "Ex-vegan" stories attract attention and sometimes come with sales of products or diets. That doesn't make them false, but it shows that the cases that circulate most don't represent everyone.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Faunalytics, study of current and former vegetarians and vegans84% of vegetarians and vegans give up; 34% within three months; the main reasons are dissatisfaction with the food (32%), health (26%) and social issues (13%)A survey of more than 11,000 adults in the US
    Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016Well-planned vegan diets are adequateOfficial position
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some people find it harder

    The most serious version grants that individual cases prove nothing in general, but says some people, because of health conditions or individual factors, may find a vegan diet harder.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    That justifies exceptions, not the rule.

    Veganism already allows for what is "possible and practicable" (D3).

    Difficulty calls for supervision.

    Many difficulties are solved with professional guidance.

    Most people aren't in that situation.

    Often, whoever repeats someone else's story has never tried.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    A sad case doesn't change what the evidence shows, and even real cases of difficulty justify exceptions at most. For most people, the moral question remains: if we can live well without killing animals, why carry on?

And doesn't conclude meat is dangerous.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Cases of people who had problems being vegan exist, and should be taken seriously. They show that information and supervision are essential, and that animal advocates should speak clearly about B12, energy and variety.

But

But one case isn't a rule. What these stories show, most of the time, is the importance of planning, not the need to eat animals.
