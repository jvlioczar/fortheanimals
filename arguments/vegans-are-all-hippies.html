L14

"Vegans are all hippies"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about who defends veganism, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the question depended on who defends animals, vegans being pleasant and consistent would make the argument valid, and being annoying and inconsistent would make it invalid. But the animals suffer just the same, whoever the messenger is.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's a stereotype, and even if it were true it wouldn't answer anything. What happens to animals doesn't depend on the lifestyle, clothes or politics of those who defend them. Vegans are a varied group, and research shows that prejudice against them is real and strong. Labeling someone a hippie is a way of not discussing the argument.

Short answer

There are vegans of every kind: athletes, doctors, business owners, people on the right and on the left. But even if they were all hippies, the pig would still feel pain just the same. The style of the speaker doesn't change whether the argument is right. Labeling someone is a way of not discussing what they said.

For children

Imagine someone said: "I won't listen to you because you wear a colorful T-shirt." Does that make sense? The T-shirt doesn't change whether what you said is right or wrong. It's the same with vegans: how the people who defend animals dress or live doesn't change the fact that animals feel pain.

Ask a question back

"If the same idea were said by someone in a suit, would it become more correct?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Vegans are all hippies, living on incense and peace and love. You can't take them seriously."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Veganism is associated with a counterculture holding unrealistic ideas about society.
  2. P2Ideas coming from unrealistic groups tend to be unreliable.
  3. CSo vegans' conclusions about animals don't deserve to be taken seriously.

P1 has a historical origin: natural food movements and the counterculture of the 1960s and 1970s helped spread vegetarianism. But animal ethics has other roots, and veganism today is far more varied. The bigger problem lies in P2, which judges the idea by the image of those who hold it.

The argument is invalid: it's an attack on the person (ad hominem), not on the argument.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument swaps the question about the idea for a question about who holds it:

    QuestionAbout whatRelevant to the animals?
    What are vegans like?Lifestyle, appearance, politicsNo
    Do animals suffer from exploitation?Facts about the animalsYes
    Is it just to cause that suffering for a taste?EthicsYes
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's ad hominem.

    Judging an idea by the image of those who hold it is a classic error. An animal's pain doesn't depend on anyone's haircut.

    2. It's a stereotype.

    Vegans are found in every profession, income bracket and political position. The image of the "hippie" describes, at most, a part of a part.

    3. The prejudice is real and documented.

    Research shows that vegans and vegetarians are the target of negative attitudes equal to or greater than those directed at several other groups. The hippie label is one way of expressing that prejudice.

    4. Animal ethics has academic foundations.

    The central argument of veganism comes from moral philosophy, discussed in universities by authors from different schools, not from a countercultural aesthetic.

    5. Even hippies can be right.

    That a group has wrong ideas on some subjects doesn't show it's wrong on all of them. Each claim has to be assessed on what it says.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    MacInnis and Hodson, 2017Omnivores evaluated vegetarians and vegans equally or more negatively than several common prejudice target groups; vegans were evaluated more negatively than vegetariansGroup Processes & Intergroup Relations, 20(6)
    Minson and Monin, 2012Meat eaters tend to disparage vegetarians because they expect to be judged by themSocial Psychological and Personality Science, 3(2)
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: the lifestyle puts people off

    The most serious version says: even if the label is unfair, the hippie image makes veganism less attractive, and that harms the cause. It would be better for vegans to look more "normal".

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's a question of communication, not of truth.

    It can be useful to think about how the message lands, but that doesn't change whether the message is right (K6).

    The image no longer matches reality.

    There are vegans of every profile, and showing that works better than asking everyone to fit one mold.

    Whoever uses the label isn't assessing the argument.

    The objection serves to end the conversation, not to improve communication.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if every vegan were a hippie, the question would remain the same: is it just to make animals suffer and die for the pleasure of taste? The answer doesn't depend on who asks it.

Contradictions

Doesn't judge other ideas by the looks of their advocates.

No one rejects medicine because a doctor has long hair.

Knows vegans who aren't hippies.

The stereotype doesn't describe even the people the person knows.

Recognizes that other groups are victims of stereotypes.

But uses one against vegans.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's right that veganism has historical roots intersecting with the counterculture, and that a movement's public image affects how its ideas are received. Thinking about communication is legitimate.

But

But the image of those who hold an idea doesn't decide whether it's true. Animals feel pain whoever says so.
