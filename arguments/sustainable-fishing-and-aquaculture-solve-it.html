H10

"Sustainable fishing and aquaculture solve it"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

fishing and aquaculture have problems no label solves. According to the FAO, more than a third of marine fish stocks are overfished, and the share has been rising. Bycatch kills dolphins, turtles and birds. Aquaculture uses meal and oil from wild fish as feed. And above all, fish are sentient beings, killed by the billions through suffocation or crushing.

Short answer

According to the FAO, more than a third of marine fish stocks are overfished, and the share keeps growing. Even well-managed fishing kills dolphins, turtles and birds as bycatch. Aquaculture uses meal and oil from wild fish in feed. And fish are sentient beings, killed by the billions, often by suffocation. No label changes that.

For children

Many fish are disappearing from the sea because people catch too many. Dolphins and turtles also get caught in the nets by accident. And fish raised in tanks eat feed made from other fish from the sea. Besides, fish feel pain and fear, like other animals.

Ask a question back

"Does the sustainability label say anything about how the fish died?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"I buy certified fish. Farmed fish don't affect the ocean."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Some fish stocks are well managed and certified as sustainable.
  2. P2Aquaculture can reduce pressure on wild fish.
  3. P3So it's possible to eat fish without depleting the oceans.
  4. CSo eating sustainable fish isn't a problem.

P1 and P2 are true in some cases. The problem lies in the conclusion: even well-managed fishing has bycatch, aquaculture depends on wild fish, and the argument ignores the suffering of the fish themselves.

The argument is valid as far as fish stocks go. Its soundness fails when it concludes there's no problem.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument talks about the stock, not the fish:

    ProblemDoes "sustainable" fishing solve it?
    OverfishingPartly, in well-managed stocks
    BycatchNot entirely
    Aquaculture feed made from wild fishNo
    The fish's suffering and deathNo
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Overfishing is large and growing.

    According to the FAO's 2024 report, more than a third of marine fish stocks are overfished, an increase on the previous report.

    2. Bycatch continues.

    Nets and hooks catch non-target species, including dolphins, turtles, sharks and seabirds.

    3. Aquaculture depends on the sea.

    Many farmed fish, such as salmon, are carnivores and still eat feed with meal and oil from wild fish, although the share of plant ingredients has been growing.

    4. Fish are sentient.

    The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness recognizes a realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fish (A6).

    5. The number of fish killed is enormous.

    Estimates suggest that between about 1 and 2.7 trillion wild fish are caught each year, many killed by suffocation or crushing.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    MSC, on the FAO's SOFIA 2024 reportThe share of overfished stocks increased compared with the previous report; more than a third of monitored stocks aren't fished sustainablyChecked from the summary; the MSC is a fisheries certifier
    Fishcount, estimate of the number of fish caughtEstimated about 1 to 2.7 trillion wild fish caught each yearA 2010 study; approximate figures
    The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024)A realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fishSigned by hundreds of researchers
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: herbivorous farmed fish

    The most serious version grants the problems with fishing, but says herbivorous farmed fish, such as tilapia, don't depend on wild fish and have a low environmental impact.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Environmental impact is only part of it.

    Farmed fish suffer from high stocking density, disease and slaughter often without stunning.

    There's pollution and disease.

    Aquaculture can pollute rivers and spread disease to wild fish.

    There are plant alternatives.

    Protein and omega-3 can come from plants and algae (G6).

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if fishing were perfectly sustainable, every fish caught would be a sentient individual killed. Sustainability talks about the species and the stock, not the fish. The moral question remains: what justifies its death when there are alternatives?

What the argument gets right

What is true

Concern for the oceans is legitimate, and there are real differences between well-managed and predatory fishing. Labels and management policies can reduce environmental harm, and some forms of aquaculture have a lower impact.

But

But the sustainability of the stock isn't the sustainability of the fish. Each fish is someone who feels, and no label changes the fact that they were killed.
