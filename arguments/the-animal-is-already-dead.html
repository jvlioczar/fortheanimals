K2

"The animal is already dead"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about effectiveness and individual responsibility, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended only by each person's impact, anyone who proved their purchase changes nothing would be let off. But no one accepts this for other harms: taking part in an injustice is still wrong even when each person's share is small.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the animal on the shelf is already dead, but the purchase doesn't end with them: it signals to the market that stock needs replacing, and the replacement is the next death. A single purchase rarely decides on its own whether one more animal will be raised, but the average effect of each purchase is real (K1). No one buys the past; the purchase counts toward the next order.

Short answer

The animal on the shelf has already died, but the purchase signals to the market that stock needs replacing. The replacement is the next death. The store doesn't ask whether the animal was already dead; it counts how many products were sold and orders more. Each purchase counts toward the next order, not toward the animal who already died.

For children

When you buy a packet of cookies, the store sees it was sold and orders another from the factory. Meat is the same: when someone buys it, the store orders more, and another animal is raised and killed to replace it. So the purchase isn't about the animal who already died, but about the next one.

Ask a question back

"If no one bought the meat on the shelf, would the store order the same amount next week?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"It's already in the store; it'll go to waste. I wasn't the one who killed it."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1The animal whose meat is on the shelf has already died.
  2. P2Nothing I do now can bring them back.
  3. P3If I don't buy it, the meat will be wasted.
  4. CSo buying that meat causes no harm.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3 and the conclusion: the market responds to purchases, and today's purchase influences tomorrow's production.

The argument is valid if the purchase didn't affect production. Its soundness fails because it does.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument looks only at the product in front of it, not at the cycle:

    StageWhat happens
    PurchaseThe product leaves the shelf
    StockThe store records the sale
    OrderThe store orders more from the supplier
    ProductionThe slaughterhouse kills more animals
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The purchase funds the replacement.

    The money and the record of the sale move the production chain.

    2. The market responds to demand.

    Although each individual purchase has a small and uncertain effect, the expected effect is real (K1).

    3. The argument would apply to any product.

    Anything on the shelf "has already been made", and buying it still influences future production.

    4. "I wasn't the one who killed it" is outsourced morality.

    Paying for the act is taking part in it (K3).

    5. The leftovers case is different.

    Eating something that would be thrown away, without creating demand, is a rare and distinct case (F10).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Kagan, *Do I Make a Difference?* (2011)Each purchase has a real expected effect on production, even in chains with thresholds; the central example is buying chickenAlso used in K1
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: the supply chain absorbs small variations

    The most serious version points out that stocks, waste and long-term contracts can absorb small variations in demand, and that a single purchase may affect nothing.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The expected effect is still positive.

    Even with absorption, there's a chance the purchase affects production (K1).

    The sum of purchases matters.

    The market responds to the total, which is made of individual purchases.

    Responsibility doesn't depend only on effect.

    Taking part in a harmful practice counts too.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The animal on the shelf can't be saved, but the next one can. The moral question isn't about the past; it's about what today's purchase brings about tomorrow.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that the animal on the shelf can't be saved, and that a single purchase has a small and uncertain effect. In rare cases, such as leftovers that would be thrown away, the situation is different.

But

But the market works by replacement. Today's purchase is tomorrow's order, and the moral question is about the next animal.
