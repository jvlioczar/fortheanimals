A11

"Species is biology, race is a social construct"

The answer in one sentence

the argument tries to show that speciesism isn't like racism, because species would be a real biological category and race a social construct. But being biological doesn't make a criterion fair. A slavery based on height would use a biological trait and be as unjust as any other. Racism is unjust because it gives someone's interests biased treatment, not because of where the concept of race came from. The same holds for speciesism.

Short answer

Being biological doesn't make a criterion fair. Imagine enslaving people by height: height is biological, and that slavery would be as unjust as any other. Racism is wrong because it disregards someone's interests because of a trait that doesn't change how much they can suffer. Species does the same. And if it were ever shown that races exist biologically, racism would still be wrong.

For children

Imagine a school where the shortest children had to carry the tallest children's backpacks. Height is something about the body; it's real. Even so, the rule would be unfair, wouldn't it? What makes a rule unfair isn't whether the difference is real or made up. It's someone suffering just because they were born different.

Ask a question back

"If height is biological, would a slavery based on height be just?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"You can't compare speciesism with racism. Race doesn't exist, it's a social invention. Species really exists, it's biology."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Racism and sexism are unjust because they use categories that are social constructs, with no real biological basis.
  2. P2Species is a real biological category, with genetic, reproductive and evolutionary differences.
  3. CSo using species as a moral criterion isn't unjust the way racism is.

Premise P2 has support: species is a scientific concept, even though there's debate about how to define it. And P1 echoes current criticism of the biological concept of race.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P1: what makes racism unjust isn't the origin of the concept.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    QuestionThe argument's answerThe correct answer
    Is species real?YesYes, in general
    Is race real?NoScientific debate aside, it's irrelevant here
    What makes discrimination unjust?Using an invented categoryGiving less weight to someone's interests because of an irrelevant trait

    The argument gets the facts right and the theory of injustice wrong.

  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It doesn't explain why the criterion's origin matters.

    The argument assumes, without justifying it, that a biological criterion would be fair and a social one unfair.

    2. There are clearly unjust biological criteria.

    Height, eye color, blood type and sex are biological. Using them to decide who may be enslaved or killed would be unjust.

    3. Racism doesn't depend on the social construction thesis.

    If races were shown to have some biological basis, racism would be just as unjust as before. The injustice lies in giving people's interests biased treatment.

    4. Species doesn't measure what matters.

    It doesn't tell us whether someone can suffer, or how much. So it's as irrelevant to moral consideration as race (A8).

    5. The comparison is of structure, not of victims.

    Saying speciesism and racism have the same logical structure doesn't equate the victims or minimize racism (L21).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    *A Journey Through Animal Ethics*, vol. I, ch. 7Answers the appeal to the distinction between the biological and the socially constructedThe main source for this chapter
    *A Journey Through Animal Ethics*, vol. I, ch. 2Shows that species, like race and gender, is an arbitrary criterionBasis for point 4
    Ryder, *Speciesism* (1970)Coins the term "speciesism" by analogy with racismThe original leaflet, distributed in Oxford
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: species carries real capacities with it

    The most serious version says species doesn't matter in itself, but because it comes with real differences in capacity: language, reason, culture. Race brings none of that; species does.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Then the criterion is capacity, not species.

    The argument becomes A15 or A18, and runs into species overlap: there are humans without these capacities.

    The capacities cited don't measure suffering.

    Language and culture don't say how much someone suffers when confined or killed.

    The analogy with racism holds.

    Racists also claimed real differences in capacity between races. The error wasn't only in the facts, but in using irrelevant capacities to deny interests.

Contradictions

Would reject discrimination by height.

Which is also biological.

Condemns sexism.

Which rests on real biological differences between the sexes.

Says species is just biology.

And gives it a moral weight biology doesn't give.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Species are biological categories, and there are real differences between humans and other animals. Those differences justify different treatment in many cases: a pig doesn't need to vote or go to school.

But

But different treatment isn't disregard. A difference of species doesn't change the fact that a pig can suffer, and that's what matters when we decide whether it's fair to make him suffer.
