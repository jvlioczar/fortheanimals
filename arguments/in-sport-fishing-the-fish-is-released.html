I10

"In sport fishing the fish is released."

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment and companion animals, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

releasing the fish doesn't undo what happened before. The hook wounds the mouth or throat, the fight causes exhaustion, the time out of water causes suffocation. A review of 274 estimates found an average mortality of 18% after release, with some cases as high as 95%. And even when the fish survives, the pain and stress were caused for fun.

Short answer

Releasing the fish doesn't undo the hook in its mouth, the fight to exhaustion and the time without breathing out of water. A review of 274 estimates found an average mortality of 18% after release, with some cases as high as 95%, depending on where the hook wounds and how the fish is handled. And even when it survives, the fish felt pain and stress for someone's fun.

For children

Imagine a hook stuck in your mouth, pulling you to a place where you can't breathe. Even if they let you go afterwards, you'd be hurt and very scared. It's similar for fish: they feel pain, and some die after being released.

Ask a question back

"If the fish feels pain, does releasing it afterwards undo what it felt?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"I catch and release. It's just a little hook. Fishing is my therapy."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1In sport fishing, the fish is returned to the water.
  2. P2Most released fish survive.
  3. P3If the fish survives, there's no significant harm.
  4. CSo sport fishing is harmless.

P1 is true. So is P2, on average. The problem lies in P3: surviving isn't the same as not having been hurt, and a significant share die afterwards.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument assesses only the end of the story:

    StageWhat happens to the fish
    HookingThe hook pierces the mouth, throat or other tissue
    The fightExhaustion and lactic acid build-up
    Out of waterSuffocation, damage to the gills and the mucus layer
    ReleaseSome die afterwards; survivors are injured and stressed
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. There's mortality after release.

    Bartholomew and Bohnsack reviewed 274 estimates and found a median mortality of 11% and a mean of 18%, ranging from 0% to 95%.

    2. Where the hook lands matters a lot.

    Fish hooked in the throat or gills die far more often. Natural bait, deep hooking and prolonged handling increase mortality.

    3. Fish feel pain.

    The New York Declaration recognizes a realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fish (A6).

    4. The harm is caused for fun.

    There's no necessity: sport fishing is recreation.

    5. "Therapy" can be found in other ways.

    Being in nature, by the water, doesn't require hurting an animal.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Bartholomew and Bohnsack, 2005, *Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries*Post-release mortality with a median of 11% and a mean of 18%, ranging from 0% to 95%, across 274 estimatesA review of 53 studies
    The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024)A realistic possibility of consciousness in all vertebrates, including fishSigned by hundreds of researchers
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: good practice reduces mortality

    The most serious version points out that circle hooks, barbless hooks, artificial lures and quick handling greatly reduce mortality.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Reducing mortality doesn't eliminate pain.

    The fish is still hooked and pulled from the water.

    Not everyone follows good practice.

    The average reflects real practice.

    The question remains.

    Why hurt an animal for fun, even carefully?

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Sport fishing is perhaps the clearest case of harm caused without necessity: it isn't for food, it's for fun. The moral question becomes sharp: is it fair to cause an animal pain and fear for recreation?

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that releasing a fish is better than killing it, and that good practice greatly reduces mortality. Fishing also has real value for those who do it: contact with nature, calm and companionship.

But

But those values don't depend on hurting an animal. And the harm caused before the release is still caused for fun.
