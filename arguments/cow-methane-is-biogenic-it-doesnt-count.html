H7

"Cow methane is biogenic; it doesn't count"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

cattle methane lasts about 12 years in the atmosphere, but in that time it warms far more than CO₂. That's why cutting methane is one of the fastest ways to slow warming, and the UN estimates that agriculture, mainly livestock, accounts for about 40% of the methane emitted by human activity. The claim that a stable herd causes no further warming only holds if the herd doesn't grow, and it does. And the methane from a stable herd still keeps the planet warmer than it would be without it.

Short answer

Methane lasts about 12 years in the air, but during that time it warms much more than CO₂. That's why cutting methane is one of the fastest ways to slow warming. The UN estimates that agriculture, mainly livestock, accounts for about 40% of methane from human activity. A stable herd wouldn't cause further warming, but herds are growing. And even if stable, a herd keeps the planet warmer than it would be without it.

For children

Methane is a gas cattle release when they burp. It doesn't stay in the air very long, but while it does, it warms the planet much more than other gases. Since there are billions of cattle, that makes a difference. If there were fewer cattle, the planet would cool a little quite quickly.

Ask a question back

"If cutting methane cools the planet quickly, why not shrink the herd?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Methane disappears in 12 years. Cows have always burped."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1The methane emitted by cattle is part of a biological cycle: the carbon comes from plants and returns to them.
  2. P2Methane breaks down in about 12 years, unlike fossil CO₂, which accumulates for centuries.
  3. P3A herd of stable size keeps methane concentration constant, with no additional warming.
  4. CSo cattle methane shouldn't be counted like fossil fuels.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 is technically correct for a stable herd. The problem lies in the conclusion: herds are growing, the methane already emitted keeps the planet warmer, and cutting it would bring rapid cooling.

The argument is valid for stable herds. Its soundness fails because herds aren't stable, and because "not warming further" isn't the same as "not warming".

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument has a correct technical part and a wrong conclusion:

    ClaimStatus
    Methane lasts about 12 yearsTrue
    The carbon in cattle methane comes from plantsTrue
    A stable herd causes no additional warmingTechnically true
    So cattle methane doesn't matterFalse: herds grow, and methane already emitted keeps the planet warmer
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Methane is a very potent greenhouse gas.

    Over 20 years, it warms dozens of times more than the same amount of CO₂. Its short life makes cutting it especially effective.

    2. Agriculture is the largest human source of methane.

    The UN's 2021 methane report estimated that agriculture accounts for about 40% of human-caused methane, with livestock as the largest source within it.

    3. Herds are growing.

    The world's cattle population has increased in recent decades and tends to grow with demand. More animals mean more methane and more warming.

    4. "Not warming further" isn't "not warming".

    A stable herd maintains a level of methane that leaves the planet warmer than it would be without it. Shrinking the herd would bring real cooling.

    5. The comparison with bison and other wild animals doesn't hold.

    Today, the mass of farmed animals is about fourteen times that of all wild mammals, and those animals occupy land that could store carbon (H9).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    UNEP, *Global Methane Assessment* (2021)Agriculture accounts for about 40% of human-caused methane, and livestock is the largest agricultural sourceReport by the United Nations Environment Programme
    Agri-Pulse, on the FAO report *Tackling Climate Change through Livestock* (2013)Livestock supply chains account for about 14.5% of human greenhouse gas emissionsIn 2023, the FAO revised the estimate to about 12%, a figure disputed by researchers who consider it an underestimate
    Bar-On, Phillips and Milo, 2018, *PNAS*Farmed animals total about 0.1 gigatonnes of carbon; wild mammals, about 0.007The distribution of biomass on Earth
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: the GWP* metric shows cattle warm less

    The most serious version cites the GWP* metric, which treats short-lived gases differently and shows that stable or declining herds contribute little additional warming.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    GWP* itself shows that shrinking the herd cools.

    Used correctly, the metric indicates that reducing the herd would bring rapid climate benefit.

    The metric doesn't justify keeping the current level.

    It measures additional warming, not the warming already caused.

    Livestock has other impacts.

    Deforestation, land use and nitrous oxide remain (H1, H9).

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if cattle methane were harmless to the climate, the cattle would still be raised and killed. The climate argument, however important, doesn't answer the central question: is it fair to kill animals when we don't need to?

Logical

Contradictions

Accepts cutting other gases.

But not cattle methane.

Ignores herd growth.

Which contradicts the premise of stability.

Uses the argument to maintain consumption, not reduce it.

The metric itself would point the other way.

What the argument gets right

What is true

There is a real difference between methane and CO₂, and the GWP* metric captures something important about short-lived gases. It's also true that the carbon in cattle methane comes from plants, and that fossil fuels are the biggest climate problem.

But

But these truths lead to another conclusion: since methane is short-lived, shrinking the herd would bring a rapid climate benefit. Cattle methane counts, and a lot.
