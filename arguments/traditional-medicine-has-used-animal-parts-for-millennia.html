I6

"Traditional medicine has used animal parts for millennia"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

being old doesn't prove that something works, nor that it's fair. Bear bile is extracted from bears kept in cages for years; more than 12,000 bears are on farms of this kind in China and Vietnam. Rhino horn is made of keratin, the same protein as human fingernails. For the few animal compounds with a real effect, such as ursodeoxycholic acid from bile, synthetic or plant-based versions already exist.

Short answer

Being old doesn't prove that something works, nor that it's fair. Bear bile is extracted from bears caged for years; more than 12,000 are on farms of this kind in China and Vietnam. Rhino horn is made of keratin, the same protein as fingernails. And for the few compounds with a real effect, such as ursodeoxycholic acid, synthetic versions already exist and are used in mainstream medicine.

For children

In some places, bears are kept in small cages for many years so people can take a liquid from their bodies to use as medicine. But the medicine from that liquid that really works is already made in labs, without any bear. And a rhino's horn is made of the same material as our fingernails.

Ask a question back

"If the useful compound in bile is already made without bears, why keep bears caged to extract it?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Traditional Chinese medicine has used bear bile for thousands of years. If it didn't work, nobody would use it."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Traditional medicine has used animal parts for centuries.
  2. P2A practice that lasts so long must have some effect.
  3. P3Banning these practices disrespects non-Western cultures.
  4. CSo using animal parts in traditional medicine is acceptable.

P1 is true. P2 is an appeal to tradition: many practices lasted centuries without working. P3 raises a real concern, but respect for cultures doesn't license any harm (D6).

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    ProductWhere it comes fromAlternative
    Bear bileCaged bears, with repeated extractionSynthetic ursodeoxycholic acid and herbs used in traditional medicine itself
    Rhino hornIllegal huntingNo demonstrated effect; it's keratin
    Pangolin scalesHunting and traffickingNo demonstrated effect
    Civet coffeeCaged civets fed coffee cherriesOrdinary coffee
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Tradition isn't proof of efficacy.

    Bloodletting and mercury were also used for centuries. Efficacy is checked with studies, not with time in use.

    2. The cost to animals is enormous.

    According to Animals Asia, more than 12,000 bears are held on bile farms in China and Vietnam, many for years, in cages, with repeated extractions.

    3. What works already exists without animals.

    Ursodeoxycholic acid, the active ingredient in bear bile, is produced synthetically and used in mainstream medicine. Traditional Chinese medicine itself has herbs that practitioners recommend as substitutes.

    4. Respecting a culture doesn't require cruelty.

    There are traditional medicine practitioners who support ending the use of animal parts. Criticizing a practice isn't attacking a whole culture (D6).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Animals Asia, what is bear bile farmingMore than 12,000 bears held on bile farms in China and VietnamAnimal protection organization working in the region
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: farming protects wild animals

    The most serious version says that, without farms, demand would lead to hunting wild bears, and that farming would be a lesser evil.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Farming keeps demand alive.

    Researchers debate whether farms reduce or stimulate hunting, since they normalize the product.

    A lesser evil is still an evil.

    Bears caged for years suffer, even if others are spared.

    There's a better way.

    Synthetic and plant substitutes meet the need without any bear.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to keep animals suffering for remedies that don't work or that already exist without them. The age of a custom doesn't answer that question.

Contradictions

Trusts tradition.

And rejects other old practices that fell out of use.

Values what's natural.

And accepts animals caged for years.

Respects the culture.

Without listening to the practitioners who call for ending these practices.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that traditional medicine is part of important cultures, that some of its compounds have real effects, and that criticism from outside can sound disrespectful.

But

But the useful part already exists without animals, and the part with animals causes enormous suffering. Respecting a culture includes recognizing those within it who want to change these practices.
