G12

"A carnivore, paleo or keto diet is healthiest"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the evidence for the carnivore diet is weak, short term and based mostly on personal accounts. Large long-term studies point the other way: in a study of more than 15,000 adults followed for about 25 years, those who replaced carbohydrates with animal protein and fat had higher mortality, and those who replaced them with plant sources had lower mortality. Losing weight in the short term isn't the same as living longer.

Short answer

The evidence for the carnivore diet is weak and short term: it comes mostly from personal accounts and surveys of enthusiastic volunteers. Large long-term studies point the opposite way. In a study of more than 15,000 adults followed for about 25 years, published in The Lancet Public Health in 2018, replacing carbohydrates with animal protein and fat was linked to higher mortality, and replacing them with plant sources, to lower mortality.

For children

Some people think eating only meat is good for you. But when scientists follow thousands of people for many years, they see that people who eat more fruit, vegetables, beans and grains tend to live longer. Varied food, full of plants, is what helps the body most.

Ask a question back

"Is there any long-term study, with thousands of people, showing that meat-based diets make people live longer?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"The carnivore diet cured my illness. Our ancestors ate only meat. Carbs are the villain."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Low-carbohydrate diets can lead to weight loss and better blood sugar control in the short term.
  2. P2Many people report health improvements when adopting carnivore or ketogenic diets.
  3. P3Our hunter-gatherer ancestors ate a lot of meat.
  4. CSo a meat-based diet is the healthiest.

P1 is supported by short-term studies. P2 describes personal accounts, not controlled evidence. P3 is debatable and, even if true, wouldn't tell us what's best today (C5). The problem lies in the conclusion: long-term studies don't support it.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails because P2 and P3 don't support a conclusion about long-term health.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument mixes very different kinds of evidence:

    Kind of evidenceWhat it showsStrength
    Personal accountsPeople felt betterWeak: no controls, no follow-up
    Short-term studies of low-carb dietsWeight loss and better blood sugarModerate, but only in the short term
    Large long-term cohort studiesDiets with more meat and fewer plants linked to higher mortalityStrong for association
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Personal accounts aren't controlled evidence.

    People who feel good on a diet tend to talk about it; those who feel unwell usually quit quietly. Changes such as cutting ultra-processed food and sugar also explain some of the improvements.

    2. Long-term studies point the other way.

    In the ARIC study, with 15,428 adults followed for about 25 years, the lowest mortality was linked to a moderate carbohydrate intake. Replacing carbohydrates with animal protein and fat was linked to higher mortality; replacing them with plant sources, to lower mortality.

    3. Processed and red meat have known risks.

    The WHO's cancer agency classifies processed meat as carcinogenic and red meat as probably carcinogenic (G13).

    4. What works is quality, not meat.

    Many of the benefits attributed to low-carb diets come from cutting sugar and ultra-processed food, which can be done on a plant diet.

    5. Our ancestors didn't eat only meat.

    Hunter-gatherers had very varied diets, with plenty of plants, roots and fruit, depending on the environment.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Seidelmann et al., 2018, *Lancet Public Health*Replacing carbohydrates with animal protein and fat was linked to higher mortality; with plant sources, to lower mortality15,428 adults followed for about 25 years, plus a meta-analysis of other studies; an observational study
    IARC, press release no. 240 (2015)Processed meat is carcinogenic; red meat is probably carcinogenicAlso used in D9
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: observational studies don't prove causation

    The most serious version points out that studies like ARIC are observational, based on food questionnaires, and don't prove that meat causes mortality.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The evidence for the carnivore diet is even weaker.

    If observational studies aren't enough, personal accounts are even less so.

    Several lines of evidence converge.

    Cohort studies, biological mechanisms and trials on saturated fat point in the same direction.

    The body of evidence favors plants.

    Plant-rich diets have the largest body of favorable evidence, from several kinds of study.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if a meat-based diet brought some short-term personal benefit, that wouldn't justify the animals' deaths. And since long-term studies don't support that benefit, the moral question returns to the center.

Contradictions

Uses ancestors as a model only for diet.

In nothing else.

The model itself doesn't confirm the diet.

What the argument gets right

What is true

There are truths here: low-carb diets can help with weight loss and blood sugar control in the short term, and cutting sugar and ultra-processed food is good for you. Many people really do feel better when they change their diet, and those experiences deserve attention.

But

But what long-term studies show is that the healthiest path runs through more plants, not more meat. And no personal benefit answers the question about the animals.
