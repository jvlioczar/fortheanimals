I5

"Pig organs will save thousands of lives"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the organ shortage is a real and serious problem, and xenotransplantation is a serious attempt to solve it. But it requires breeding genetically modified pigs, in sterile facilities, to remove their organs. The first patients, in 2022 and 2024, lived about two months. Before creating a new form of exploitation at scale, there are paths that don't require killing animals: increasing human organ donation, artificial organs and organs grown from cells.

Short answer

The organ shortage is real and serious, but xenotransplantation requires breeding genetically modified pigs, in sterile facilities, just to remove their organs. The first patients, in 2022 and 2024, lived about two months. Before creating a new exploitation at scale, there are paths that don't kill animals: increasing human organ donation, artificial organs and organs grown from cells.

For children

Some people need a new heart or kidney, and sometimes there's no donor. Scientists are trying to use organs from modified pigs. But for that they need to breed pigs just to take their organs. Other scientists are trying to make artificial organs, or organs from the person's own cells, without needing animals.

Ask a question back

"If human organ donation increased, would it still be necessary to breed pigs for this?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"People are dying on the transplant list. If a pig heart saves a life, what's the harm?"

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Thousands of people die waiting for an organ.
  2. P2Organs from genetically modified pigs can fill that gap.
  3. P3Saving human lives is worth more than these pigs' lives.
  4. CSo xenotransplantation is justified.

P1 is true. P2 is a promise still being tested. P3 depends on there being no alternatives, and there are.

The argument is valid. Its soundness depends on P2 and P3, both debatable.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    Path to the organ shortageDoes it require breeding and killing animals?
    Xenotransplantation with modified pigsYes
    Increasing human organ donationNo
    Artificial organs and mechanical devicesNo
    Organs grown from human cellsNo, or far less
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's still experimental.

    David Bennett received a genetically modified pig heart in January 2022, at the University of Maryland, and died two months later. Richard Slayman received a pig kidney in March 2024, at Massachusetts General Hospital, and died about two months later; the hospital said there was no indication that his death resulted from the transplant.

    2. The pigs are bred for this.

    The organs come from pigs with multiple gene edits, kept in isolated facilities to avoid infections, and killed to remove the organs. In Bennett's case, a pig virus was found in the organ.

    3. There are alternatives that don't require animals.

    Policies that increase human organ donation, such as the presumed consent adopted by several countries, artificial organs and organs grown from cells reduce the shortage without creating a new form of exploitation.

    4. The comparison with the plate is revealing.

    "We already eat pigs" shows that pigs are already used without need. That doesn't make one more use acceptable; it shows how much of the current use is dispensable.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    NPR, 2022First transplant of a genetically modified pig heart into a humanUniversity of Maryland
    Science NewsHistory of pig organ transplants, including the 2024 kidneyScience journalism
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: human lives are at stake now

    The most serious version says patients are dying on the list today, and that animal-free alternatives are still far from solving the problem.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Xenotransplantation is also far off.

    The first cases show there's still much to solve.

    Human donation can grow now.

    Donation policies can save lives without breeding animals for it.

    A tragic case doesn't justify an industry.

    Even if a single transplant were defensible, breeding pigs at scale to harvest organs is a new form of exploitation.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to breed animals just to take their organs when there are paths that don't require it. Patients' urgency is real; it calls for investing in those paths.

Contradictions

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that the organ shortage kills many people, that xenotransplantation is serious research, and that in the face of imminent death the choice is tragic.

But

But the solution doesn't need to be a new industry of animals bred to have their organs removed. Human donation, artificial and grown organs also aim to save these lives, without new victims.
