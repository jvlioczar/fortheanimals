B6

"Veal makes use of the calves born in dairy farming"

The answer in one sentence

veal doesn't make use of a calf who would be born anyway: it is part of the system that causes these calves to be born. To give milk, a cow has to give birth every year, and the males are of no use for milk production. So they are killed at a few days or weeks old, or raised for a few months to become veal. In New Zealand, about 1.8 million of these calves were slaughtered in a single year, many at 4 days old. "Making use" of the calf just gives a destination to a victim that milk itself creates.

Short answer

Veal doesn't make use of a calf who would be born anyway: it's part of the system that causes that calf to be born. A cow only gives milk if she gives birth every year, and the males are no use for milk; so they are killed young, some as veal. In New Zealand, about 1.8 million calves were slaughtered in a single year, many at 4 days old. What decides how many are born is how much milk is bought.

For children

A cow only gives milk after having a baby, just like human mothers. To keep giving milk, she has to have a calf every year. The male calves won't give milk, so many are killed while still babies, some to become a meat called veal.

Ask a question back

"If the calf is only born because the cow needs to give milk, is he born 'anyway', or because of the milk people buy?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"The male calf is going to be born anyway in dairy farming. At least veal makes use of him instead of wasting him."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Dairy production generates male calves who are no use for milk.
  2. P2If nothing is done with them, they'll be killed and wasted.
  3. P3Using what would be wasted is better than wasting it.
  4. CSo eating veal is a responsible way to make use of these calves.

P1 is true. So is P2, within the current system. The problem is that the argument treats as fixed something that results from a choice: the calves only exist because people buy milk.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails because it treats as inevitable what depends on demand.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument starts in the middle of the story:

    StageWhat happens
    InseminationThe cow is made pregnant so she starts giving milk
    BirthA calf is born; half are males
    SeparationThe calf is taken from his mother so the milk can be sold
    The male's fateSlaughter at a few days old, or a few months of rearing as veal
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The calf isn't born "anyway".

    He's born because dairy production requires births. Buying milk funds those births, and buying veal funds one of the ways of disposing of the males.

    2. The scale is large.

    In New Zealand, where dairy is central, about 1.8 million such calves were slaughtered in the year ending September 2019, according to Ministry for Primary Industries data; slaughter is allowed from 4 days of age.

    3. Veal production has had very cruel versions.

    Individual crates, in which calves were kept unable to turn around, were banned in the European Union for calves over 8 weeks by Directive 2008/119/EC. Improvements like this reduce suffering, but they don't change the animal's fate (B4).

    4. "Making use" isn't the same as not causing.

    Giving a profitable destination to a victim of the system makes the system more profitable, not less.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    1NewsNew Zealand's dairy industry kills about two million calves a yearReport using Ministry for Primary Industries data
    Directive 2008/119/ECMinimum standards for the protection of calves in the EU, including the end of individual pens after 8 weeksEU legislation
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: what if the veal is raised with good welfare?

    The most serious version says there is veal from calves raised outdoors, with their mother or in groups, for a few months, and that this is better than killing them at a few days old.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's better for the calf, compared with slaughter at 4 days.

    But it's still a young animal killed to be eaten.

    It doesn't change the cause.

    The calf still exists because of milk, and veal still makes milk more profitable.

    The question goes back to milk.

    The argument shows that milk and meat are part of the same system (B5).

Reasoning errors

Logical

Is the form valid?

False inevitability
The calf's birth is treated as a natural fact, not as an effect of demand

Epistemological

Does the evidence support it?

Not knowing how milk is produced
Ignores that the cow must give birth to give milk

Moral

Is the criterion coherent?

Use as justification
Giving a profitable destination to a victim is treated as a virtue

Biases

Status quo bias

The current system is treated as given, not as a choice

Samuelson and Zeckhauser, 1988

Functional categorization

The calf is seen as a byproduct, not as an individual

Bratanova, Loughnan and Bastian, 2011

Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if a system creates victims, making use of them is responsible".

Contradictions

Talks about not wasting.

And supports a system that generates millions of calves it has no use for.

Considers milk harmless.

And knows the males must have some fate.

Wouldn't accept the same with puppies.

Breeding dogs to sell their milk and killing the puppies would be cruelty.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that, within the current system, male calves will be born, and that raising them carefully for a few months may be better than killing them at a few days old. It's also true that wasting an animal's life is even worse than using it.

But

But the argument only works if the system is treated as inevitable. It isn't: the number of calves depends on how much milk is bought.
