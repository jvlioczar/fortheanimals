I6

"Zoos conserve species and educate"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

most species kept in zoos aren't threatened. A 2013 study using data from zoos around the world found that about 23% of the land vertebrate species in their collections are threatened, and few take part in reintroduction programs. The most effective conservation happens through habitat protection. And the "education" of seeing a confined animal may teach that confining it is normal.

Short answer

Most zoo species aren't threatened: a 2013 study found that about 23% of the land vertebrate species in zoo collections are threatened, and few take part in reintroduction. The most effective conservation is protecting habitats. Wide-ranging animals, such as elephants and big cats, suffer greatly in confinement. And seeing an animal locked up can teach that locking it up is normal.

For children

In zoos, many animals live in small spaces, far from where they were born. Most of them aren't in danger of disappearing from the wild. To really protect animals, the most important thing is to look after the forests, rivers and seas where they live. And you can learn a lot about them from documentaries, books and visits to sanctuaries.

Ask a question back

"How many of the zoo animals you know will ever go back to the wild?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Without zoos, children wouldn't know animals. They live longer in the zoo."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Many species are threatened with extinction.
  2. P2Zoos maintain reserve populations and take part in breeding programs.
  3. P3Zoos educate the public about conservation.
  4. CSo zoos are important for conservation, and keeping animals in them is justified.

P1 is true. So is P2, for some species. P3 has mixed evidence. The problem lies in the conclusion: it justifies the whole zoo with a small part of what it does.

The argument is valid for conservation programs. It fails as a justification for confinement in general.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument uses a small part to justify the whole:

    Claimed functionStatus
    Conserving threatened speciesAbout 23% of species in collections; few reintroduced
    EducationMixed evidence on learning and change of attitude
    EntertainmentThe main reason for visits
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Most species aren't threatened.

    A 2013 study found that about 23% of the land vertebrate species in zoos are threatened, and that zoos hold about one seventh of the world's threatened species.

    2. Few species are reintroduced.

    Successful reintroduction programs exist, but they involve a small fraction of the animals kept.

    3. Conservation depends on habitat.

    Without protected forests, rivers and grasslands, there's nowhere to reintroduce animals to. Most effective conservation happens in situ.

    4. Wide-ranging animals suffer.

    Elephants, big cats and other animals that travel long distances develop stereotypic behaviors in captivity, signs of suffering.

    5. The "education" may teach the opposite.

    Seeing confined animals as entertainment can normalize confinement.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Conde et al., 2013, *PLoS ONE*About 23% of land vertebrate species in zoos are threatened; zoos hold about one seventh of threatened speciesBased on data from zoos worldwide
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some programs have saved species

    The most serious version cites real cases, such as the California condor and the golden lion tamarin, where captive breeding helped prevent extinction.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    These cases are exceptions.

    They justify conservation centers, not display zoos.

    The golden lion tamarin depends on Brazil's Atlantic Forest.

    The success came together with protecting the habitat.

    Sanctuaries and rehabilitation centers can do this work.

    Without displaying animals as entertainment.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Conserving species is important, but the animal living in the zoo is an individual, not a species. The moral question remains: what justifies confining someone for their whole life so that others can look at them?

What the argument gets right

What is true

Some zoos do serious conservation, research and rehabilitation work, and there are real cases of species saved with the help of captive breeding. Concern about extinction is legitimate and urgent.

But

But conservation can be done without displaying animals, and most zoo animals aren't part of these programs. Protecting species starts with protecting habitats and individuals.
