H10

"Cattle prevent wildfires; they're firefighter cattle"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the idea has a real basis: in native grasslands of Brazil's Pantanal wetland, cattle eat the grass and reduce the material that burns, as research by Brazil's agricultural research agency Embrapa has shown since the 1980s. But the effect is limited and there's no consensus that more cattle means fewer fires: a study from the Federal University of Minas Gerais found more fire hotspots precisely in the areas with more cattle in 2020. And cattle ranching is among the biggest causes of deforestation, and fire is widely used to clear and renew pastures. "Firefighter cattle" justify a small part of the system that burns the most.

Short answer

The idea has a basis: in native grasslands of Brazil's Pantanal, cattle eat grass and reduce the material that burns, according to Embrapa research. But the effect is limited and there's no consensus: a university study found more fire hotspots in the areas with more cattle in 2020. And cattle ranching is among the biggest causes of deforestation, and fire is widely used to clear and renew pastures. "Firefighter cattle" justify a small part of the system that burns the most.

For children

Dry grass catches fire easily, and cows eat grass. That's why some people say cows help prevent fires. But many fires start precisely when people burn forest to make pasture for cattle. And there are other ways of looking after grass without having to raise and kill cows.

Ask a question back

"If cattle prevent fires, why do so many fires start precisely to clear or renew pastures?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Without cattle, the Pantanal burns. Cattle eat the dry grass and prevent fires; they're firefighter cattle."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Accumulated grass is fuel for fires.
  2. P2Cattle eat that grass and reduce the fuel.
  3. P3Less fuel means fewer severe fires.
  4. CSo cattle ranching protects grasslands and savannas against fire.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 holds in part, in specific contexts. The conclusion generalizes a local, limited effect to cattle ranching as a whole.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails in the generalization.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    What the argument highlightsWhat's left out
    Cattle reduce grass in native grasslandsRanching clears land to create pastures
    Less grass, less fuelFire is used to manage pastures
    An effect in specific areasGeneralization to all cattle ranching
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The effect is real but limited.

    Embrapa itself, in a 2024 bulletin, explains that "firefighter cattle" don't put out fires: they provide a prevention service by reducing fuel in native grasslands, based on experiments in the Pantanal.

    2. There's no consensus.

    A study from the Federal University of Minas Gerais (UFMG), coordinated by professor Ubirajara Oliveira, found more fire hotspots in the areas with more cattle in 2020, suggesting that the presence of cattle alone doesn't guarantee fire control.

    3. Ranching also causes fire.

    Clearing for pasture and using fire to renew pastures are among the main sources of burning in Brazil's Cerrado and Amazon.

    4. There are other ways to manage fuel.

    Integrated fire management, with prescribed burns by fire brigades and management of native vegetation, reduces fuel without depending on raising animals for slaughter.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Embrapa, CiCarne Bulletin 67 (2024)"Firefighter cattle" don't put out fires, but reduce fuel in the Pantanal's native grasslandsAgricultural research institution (in Portuguese)
    Press coverage of Mato Grosso's "firefighter cattle" law (UFMG/CSR)The 2024 state law was controversial; a UFMG study found more fire hotspots in areas with more cattlePress compilation by UFMG's Remote Sensing Center (in Portuguese)
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: in the Pantanal, removing cattle from some areas was followed by large fires

    The most serious version says areas without cattle built up grass and burned more intensely, and that extensive ranching has coexisted with the Pantanal for centuries.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    That's about one kind of management, not ranching in general.

    Most meat doesn't come from grazing on the Pantanal's native grasslands.

    There are other ways to manage fuel.

    Brigades and prescribed burns do it without raising animals for slaughter.

    The environmental argument doesn't settle the moral one.

    Even if cattle reduced fires, they would still be raised to be killed.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The environmental question has answers that don't require killing animals. The moral question remains: is it fair to raise and kill animals for an effect that can be achieved in other ways?

Reasoning errors

Logical

Is the form valid?

Undue generalization
A local effect becomes a justification for all ranching

Epistemological

Does the evidence support it?

Cherry-picking evidence
Cites prevention and omits the fire used to clear pastures

Moral

Is the criterion coherent?

The environment as the only criterion
An environmental service justifies raising animals for slaughter

Contradictions

Says cattle protect against fire.

And ignores the fire used to create pastures.

Defends the Pantanal.

And accepts the deforestation caused by ranching in other biomes.

Accepts brigades and prescribed burns in parks.

And says only cattle protect.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that accumulated grass is fuel, that grazing can reduce it in native grasslands, and that fire in the Pantanal is a serious problem that calls for solutions.

But

But the effect is local and disputed, ranching also causes deforestation and burning, and there are ways to manage fuel without raising animals for slaughter.
