B13

"Foie gras is a traditional delicacy, and ducks naturally eat a lot"

The answer in one sentence

foie gras is the diseased liver of ducks and geese force-fed through a tube pushed down the throat, two or three times a day, in the last weeks of their lives, until the organ grows several times its size. It's true that these birds store fat before migrating, but none of them eats like this of its own accord. The European Union's scientific committee concluded in 1998 that force-feeding harms the birds' welfare, and several countries have banned the practice.

Short answer

Foie gras is the diseased liver of force-fed ducks and geese: a tube is pushed down the throat, two or three times a day, in the last weeks of life, until the liver grows several times its size. These birds store fat before migrating, but none eats like this of its own accord. The European Union's scientific committee concluded in 1998 that force-feeding harms the birds' welfare, and several countries have banned it.

For children

To make foie gras, people put a tube down a duck's throat and push in lots of food, several times a day, until its liver becomes huge and sick. The duck doesn't choose to eat that much. It would be like someone stuffing food into you when you're already full.

Ask a question back

"If the duck fattened up on its own, why would a tube down its throat be needed?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Foie gras is a French culinary tradition. And ducks naturally eat a lot; they fatten up on their own before migrating."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Wild ducks and geese store fat in their livers before migrating.
  2. P2Foie gras production only enhances this natural capacity.
  3. P3Foie gras is an old gastronomic tradition.
  4. CSo producing and eating foie gras is acceptable.

P1 is true. P2 fails: production doesn't let the bird eat more, it forces food through a tube in amounts the bird wouldn't eat. P3 is true, but tradition doesn't justify harm (D1).

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    What the argument saysWhat happens in production
    "The duck fattens up on its own"Food is pushed through a tube, in amounts far above the natural
    "It just enhances a natural capacity"The liver grows several times its size and becomes diseased (steatosis)
    "It's tradition"The current industrial technique, with individual cages, is recent
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The bird doesn't eat; it's force-fed.

    In production, ducks and geese are fed through a tube two or three times a day, for about two weeks in the case of ducks and a little longer for geese.

    2. European science assessed the problem.

    The EU Scientific Committee on Animal Health and Animal Welfare (SCAHAW) concluded in 1998 that force-feeding, as practiced, harms the birds' welfare. The report noted that the birds avoided the person who force-fed them, unlike what happens with ordinary hand-feeding, and that 80% of ducks were kept in individual cages.

    3. Several countries have banned it.

    Israel, once a major producer, banned force-feeding in 2005, and the practice is banned in several European countries, though many still import the product.

    4. Tradition doesn't justify harm.

    Many old practices were abandoned once the suffering they caused was recognized (D1).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    SCAHAW, "Welfare Aspects of the Production of Foie Gras in Ducks and Geese" (1998)Force-feeding harms the birds' welfare; 80% of ducks in individual cages; mortality of 2% to 4% during the periodEuropean Commission scientific committee
    Foie gras controversy, on WikipediaCountries that have banned production, including Israel in 2005Overview of bans
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some producers use neither cages nor tubes

    The most serious version points out that some producers make fatty liver by letting birds eat freely before migration, without force-feeding.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's a tiny exception.

    Almost all foie gras sold comes from force-feeding.

    The product is different.

    Liver obtained this way is smaller and less fatty, and isn't usually sold as traditional foie gras.

    The killing remains.

    Even without force-feeding, the bird is killed for a liver no one needs to eat.

Contradictions

Says it's natural.

And accepts that a tube is needed to obtain it.

Values tradition.

And accepts the recent industrial technique.

Wouldn't accept the same with a dog.

Force-feeding a dog until its liver became diseased would be cruelty.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that foie gras has a long history and that wild ducks and geese store fat before migrating. It's also true that there are attempts to produce it without force-feeding.

But

But what's sold as foie gras comes almost entirely from force-feeding, which European science itself assessed as harmful to the birds. Tradition explains the recipe; it doesn't justify the method.
