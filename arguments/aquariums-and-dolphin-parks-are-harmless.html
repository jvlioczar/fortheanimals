I7

"Aquariums and dolphin parks are harmless"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

dolphins and orcas travel dozens of kilometers a day in the ocean and live in complex social groups. No tank reproduces that. In captivity, these animals develop repetitive behaviors and health problems. That's why countries such as Canada banned keeping and breeding cetaceans in captivity in 2019. Aquariums with fish also involve capture, confinement and death.

Short answer

Dolphins and orcas swim dozens of kilometers a day and live in complex social groups. No tank reproduces that, and in captivity they develop repetitive behaviors and health problems. The dolphin's "smile" is just the shape of its mouth. That's why Canada banned keeping and breeding cetaceans in captivity in 2019. To learn about the ocean, documentaries and responsible wildlife watching don't require locking anyone up.

For children

The dolphin seems to smile, but that's just the shape of its mouth. In the sea, it swims long distances with its family every day. In a tank, it just swims in circles in the same place. That's why some countries decided not to let dolphins and whales be kept in tanks anymore.

Ask a question back

"Can a tank, however big, compare with the ocean the dolphin would travel?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Dolphins enjoy doing shows. Aquariums teach about the ocean."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Aquariums and dolphin parks bring the public closer to marine life.
  2. P2This generates interest in and support for ocean conservation.
  3. P3The animals receive food, veterinary care and protection.
  4. CSo aquariums and dolphin parks are beneficial and don't harm the animals.

P1 is true. P2 has mixed evidence. P3 is true but incomplete: basic care doesn't make up for the lack of space and social life.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails because P3 ignores what the animals lose.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument considers what the animals receive, not what they lose:

    What the animal receivesWhat the animal loses
    Regular foodHunting and exploring
    Veterinary careSpace to swim dozens of kilometers
    Protection from predatorsSocial life with its family and group
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The space is incompatible.

    Dolphins and orcas travel great distances in the ocean. A tank is a tiny fraction of that space.

    2. Social life is lost.

    Cetaceans live in stable family groups, with complex communication. In captivity, they're separated and regrouped for convenience.

    3. Captivity causes suffering.

    Repetitive behaviors, aggression and health problems are frequently reported in captive cetaceans.

    4. The "smile" is anatomy.

    The shape of a dolphin's mouth doesn't express emotion.

    5. The law is changing.

    In 2019, Canada passed a law banning the keeping and breeding of whales, dolphins and porpoises in captivity, except for rescue and rehabilitation.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Parliament of Canada, Bill S-203Bans keeping and breeding cetaceans in captivity in CanadaPassed in 2019
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: rescued animals need care

    The most serious version points out that some animals are rescued injured and can't return to the sea, and that aquariums care for them.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    That justifies rehabilitation centers and sanctuaries.

    Not shows or captive breeding.

    Most of the animals weren't rescued.

    Many were captured or born in captivity.

    There are marine sanctuaries.

    Protected coastal areas offer much more space.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The argument talks about education and care, but what's at stake is the entire life of animals confined so that others can see them. The moral question remains: what justifies locking up someone who would live freely in the ocean?

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that aquariums can spark interest in the oceans, and that some do rescue and rehabilitation of marine animals. Keepers are often sincerely devoted to the animals.

But

But for cetaceans and other wide-ranging animals, no tank is enough. Interest in the ocean can be sparked without locking up those who live in it.
