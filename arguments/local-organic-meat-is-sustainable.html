H8

"Local, organic meat is sustainable."

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about the environment and agriculture, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

"organic" and "local" say little about meat's emissions and land use. The lowest-impact beef, from the 10% most efficient producers, emits about 9 kilos of CO₂ equivalent per 100 grams of protein; the highest-impact peas, about 0.8. Even the best beef emits more than ten times as much as the highest-impact peas. And pasture-raised meat usually requires even more land.

Short answer

Organic and local help in some ways, but they don't change the essentials: meat emits a lot and uses a lot of land. According to Poore and Nemecek's data, the most efficient beef emits about 9 kilos of CO₂ equivalent per 100 grams of protein; the least efficient peas, about 0.8. Even the best beef emits more than ten times as much as the highest-impact peas. And pasture-raised meat usually requires even more land.

For children

Organic meat doesn't use certain medicines and poisons, and that's good. But the cow still releases gases and needs a lot of space to live. Even the most carefully produced meat pollutes much more than growing beans or peas.

Ask a question back

"If the lowest-impact meat emits ten times more than the highest-impact plant, sustainable compared with what?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"I buy organic. Grass-fed beef is sustainable. Eat local."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Intensive livestock farming causes serious environmental harm.
  2. P2Organic, local, pasture-raised meat avoids some of that harm, such as pesticides, antibiotics and transport.
  3. P3So that meat is sustainable.
  4. CSo those who eat it don't contribute to the environmental problems of livestock farming.

P1 is true. So is P2, in part. The problem lies in P3: avoiding some harms doesn't make production low-impact, and emissions and land use remain high.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument compares "good" meat with "bad" meat, not with the alternatives:

    ComparisonResult
    Organic versus conventional meatFewer pesticides and antibiotics; similar or higher emissions
    Pasture-raised versus feedlotMore land per kilo; emissions per kilo often similar or higher
    Best beef versus worst plant proteinAbout 9 against 0.8 kg of CO₂ equivalent per 100 g of protein
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The best meat still emits a lot.

    According to Our World in Data, the 10% of beef producers with the lowest impact emit about 9 kg of CO₂ equivalent per 100 g of protein. The 10% of pea producers with the highest impact emit about 0.8.

    2. Organic doesn't mean low emissions.

    Organic certification concerns pesticides, fertilizers and antibiotics, not methane emissions or land use.

    3. Pasture-raised meat uses more land.

    Pasture-raised cattle grow more slowly and occupy more area. At scale, this would require even more land, and more deforestation (H1, H4).

    4. Local weighs little.

    Transport is a small fraction of meat's emissions (H7).

    5. "Good" meat is a small fraction of consumption.

    Most meat comes from conventional production (B2).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Our World in Data, less meat or sustainable meatThe lowest-impact beef emits about 9 kg of CO₂ equivalent per 100 g of protein; the highest-impact peas, about 0.8Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018
    Our World in Data, land use and dietsBeef requires about 50 to 100 times more land per kilocalorie than plant foodsBased on Poore and Nemecek, 2018
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: it's better than conventional meat

    The most serious version grants that organic, local meat isn't low-impact, but says it's better than conventional meat in some respects, such as welfare and chemical use.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Better than the worst isn't good.

    The relevant comparison is with plant alternatives.

    It isn't always better.

    On emissions and land use, pasture-raised meat can be worse than conventional.

    The animal is still killed.

    A better life doesn't change the ending (B2, B5).

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even the most sustainable meat still comes from an animal raised and killed. The environmental question is important, but it isn't the only one: if meat were neutral for the planet, the question about the animal would remain. And it still has no answer.

What the argument gets right

What is true

There are real differences between production systems: fewer pesticides, fewer antibiotics and sometimes better welfare. People who look for organic, local meat care about the impact of what they eat, and that concern is valuable.

But

But for the climate and for land, the best meat is still far worse than plants. And for the animal, the ending is the same.
