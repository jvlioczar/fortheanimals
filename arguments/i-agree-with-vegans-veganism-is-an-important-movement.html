E16

"I agree with vegans; veganism is an important movement"

The answer in one sentence

agreeing is good, and saying so out loud helps: it makes veganism more normal and opens the conversation. But agreeing changes nothing for animals. Veganism isn't a cause to cheer on from the sidelines, like a team or someone else's campaign; it is a question about what each person buys and eats. Recognizing the wrong, the contradiction or even one's own hypocrisy is a necessary step, but not a sufficient one: someone who agrees that exploitation is wrong has a reason to stop taking part in it, not just to applaud those who already have.

Short answer

That's great, and it matters: when people say they agree, veganism becomes more normal. But veganism isn't a cause to support from the outside, like cheering for a team. It's a choice about what each of us buys and eats. For animals, agreement only makes a difference when it becomes change, and that change doesn't have to be total or all at once. Recognizing is the first step; the next one can be small.

For children

Imagine someone says it's very important not to throw litter on the ground, but keeps doing it. Agreeing is good, but what keeps the street clean is stopping throwing litter. With animals it's similar: what really helps is stopping doing what hurts them, and you can start little by little.

Ask a question back

"If you agree, what would be a first step that makes sense for you?"

How it can show up

The strongest version

How it is said

"I agree with you, really. Veganism is a very important movement; we need more people like that."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Vegans' arguments are good, and the movement is important.
  2. P2Every cause needs support, not only from those fully dedicated to it.
  3. P3I can support a cause without changing my life for it, as I support other causes without getting personally involved.
  4. CSo agreeing and supporting is already my contribution.

P1 is the central concession, and there's nothing to dispute here. P2 is true: public support matters. The problem lies in P3, which treats veganism like causes in which the person isn't part of the problem. In many causes, such as fighting hunger in another country, what is asked is to help, and one can help more or less. In animal exploitation, the person's own consumption pays for the harm. No one can leave to the movement the task of not causing the harm they themselves fund.

The argument is valid, but not sound: P3 confuses the duty to help with the duty not to harm.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    What the person saysWhat it concedesWhat's left out
    "I agree with you"The arguments are goodThat they apply to them too
    "The movement is important"Ending exploitation is worthwhileThat they take part in the exploitation the movement fights
    "We need more people like that"More people should changeThat they are one of those people
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Not causing harm can't be outsourced.

    Philosophers distinguish the duty to help, which comes in degrees, from the duty not to harm, which is stronger. Supporting a campaign against hunger in another country is helping. Buying animal products is funding the harm. No one fulfils the duty not to harm by applauding those who fulfil it.

    2. "The movement is important" puts veganism outside oneself.

    The phrase treats veganism as a group's cause, which the person approves of from a distance. But the movement only exists because people changed their own choices. There is no veganism separate from the people who practise it.

    3. Recognizing is necessary, but not sufficient.

    Admitting the wrong, the contradiction or one's own hypocrisy is a real step, and often the hardest. Models of behaviour change, such as Prochaska and DiClemente's, describe this moment as contemplation: the person already sees the problem but hasn't acted yet. It is a point of passage, not a place to stay.

    4. Agreeing can become a licence not to change.

    Psychology describes moral licensing: after doing or saying something good, people sometimes feel entitled to relax in their next behaviour. Declaring support can give the feeling of having already contributed, and lower the pressure to act.

    5. Even so, agreement helps, and should be welcomed.

    Research on social norms shows that knowing other people are changing increases the willingness to change. Those who say publicly that they agree help create that climate. That's why the right response isn't to demand, but to thank them and invite the next step.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: not everyone has to be an activist, and demanding more pushes allies away

    The most serious version says: the movement needs sympathizers, not only activists. Those who agree and support already help, and demanding more turns allies into opponents.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's right about activism.

    No one has to be an activist, and veganism doesn't ask that. It asks something simpler: not to take part in exploitation, as far as is possible and practicable.

    It's right about tone.

    Demanding with hostility pushes people away. But inviting someone to a concrete step isn't demanding; it's taking seriously what the person themselves said.

    For animals, sympathy isn't enough.

    An animal isn't spared because someone agrees with veganism; they are spared when someone stops paying for their breeding and death.

Biases

Moral licensing

Merritt, Effron and Monin, 2010

Cognitive dissonance

Status quo bias

Keeping the habit seems the natural path, even while agreeing with change

Extrapolations

The principle being tested is: "if I agree with a cause and support it in words, I don't need to change what I do, even when I'm part of the problem".

Contradictions

Agrees that exploitation is wrong and pays for it.

Agreement and consumption point in opposite directions.

Says we need more people like vegans.

And doesn't count themselves among them.

Values consistency in other areas.

No one thinks it's enough to agree that tax evasion is wrong and keep evading taxes.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's right that agreeing matters: it's far better than hostility, it helps make veganism normal and it shows the arguments worked. It's also right that no one has to be an activist or change everything at once.

But

But agreement isn't a contribution when the person funds what they recognize as wrong. Recognition is where change begins, not where it ends.
