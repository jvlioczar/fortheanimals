H19

"Kangaroo meat is the most sustainable meat there is"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

kangaroo meat comes from wild animals, with no farming, little methane and no clearing for pasture, and it's true that its environmental impact is lower than beef's. But it comes from commercial shooting of wild animals at scale: in New South Wales alone, more than 500,000 kangaroos were killed in 2023. The rules allow shooting females with young, and the code provides for orphaned joeys to be killed by a blow to the head or decapitation. A lower environmental impact doesn't change what happens to the animals.

Short answer

It's true that kangaroo meat has a lower environmental impact than beef: they're wild animals, with no pasture or feed, and emit little methane. But it comes from commercial shooting at scale: in New South Wales alone, more than 500,000 kangaroos were killed in 2023. The rules allow shooting females with young, and the code provides for orphaned joeys to be killed by a blow to the head. A lower environmental impact doesn't change what happens to the animals.

For children

In Australia, people go out at night to shoot kangaroos and sell the meat. When they kill a mother kangaroo, the joey in her pouch is killed too. Kangaroo meat pollutes less than beef, but the kangaroos are still being killed.

Ask a question back

"If the problem is meat's environmental impact, is the answer to switch animals, or to eat foods that don't require killing any?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Kangaroo is the greenest meat there is: it's wild, doesn't emit methane and there are too many kangaroos in Australia."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Kangaroos are wild and need no farming, pasture or feed.
  2. P2They emit much less methane than cattle and sheep.
  3. P3Populations are abundant and the harvest is regulated by quotas.
  4. CSo eating kangaroo meat is the most sustainable choice.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 is partly true. The problem is that the conclusion treats environmental impact as the only question.

The argument is valid from an environmental standpoint. Its soundness fails when the conclusion is used as a moral justification.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    QuestionAnswer
    Is its environmental impact lower than beef's?Yes
    Does it require killing animals?Yes, at scale
    Are joeys affected?Yes; there is no official count
    Is there an alternative without killing?Yes, plant foods
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's shooting at scale.

    Australia's annual commercial quota runs into the millions, and the number of animals actually killed is below it. In New South Wales, the government recorded 509,671 kangaroos killed in 2023.

    2. Joeys are invisible victims.

    According to RSPCA Australia, the commercial code of practice provides for the young of shot females to be killed, and there are no official statistics on how many; organizations' estimates run to hundreds of thousands a year.

    3. "There are too many kangaroos" is a disputed claim.

    Populations vary greatly with droughts, and population control is a debate of its own, which doesn't depend on selling the meat (H18, N14).

    4. The best for the environment doesn't require killing.

    Swapping beef for plant foods cuts the impact even further, without shooting.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    New South Wales Government, 2023 annual report509,671 kangaroos killed in the state's commercial harvest in 2023, 27.5% of the quotaOfficial report
    RSPCA AustraliaWhat happens to joeys when females are shotAnimal protection organization
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: the harvest is regulated and populations are stable

    The most serious version points out that quotas are set from population counts, that shooting is done by licensed shooters, and that kangaroo populations remain abundant.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Regulating isn't making harmless.

    The rule sets how many die, not whether it's fair to kill them (B10).

    Oversight in the field is limited.

    Shooting happens at night, in remote areas, and the fate of joeys isn't counted.

    The environmental question has a better answer.

    If the goal is to cut meat's impact, plant foods do it without killing.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to kill wild animals at scale, joeys included, for food, when there are foods that don't require it. A lower environmental impact doesn't answer that question.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that kangaroo meat has a lower environmental impact than beef, that kangaroos are wild and abundant in many areas, and that swapping beef for it would cut emissions.

But

But the right comparison is with foods that don't require killing animals. And no environmental calculation answers why kill wild animals, and their young, when there's no need.
