F8

"What if they discover that plants feel?"

The answer in one sentence

today there's no evidence that plants feel (A4). But if that were ever discovered, the conclusion wouldn't be "so it makes no difference whether we eat animals". It would be the opposite: the sentience criterion would come to include plants, and the way to cause the least harm would still be to eat plants directly, because animals raised for food eat far more plants than we would. The discovery would increase the number of beings who count; it wouldn't reduce the weight of those who already count.

Short answer

Today there's no evidence that plants feel. But if it were ever discovered, the best way to cause less harm would still be to eat plants, because the animals that become food eat far more plants than we would eat directly. The discovery would increase the number of beings who count; it wouldn't make it acceptable to exploit those we already know feel.

For children

Imagine plants could feel a tiny bit. A little pig needs to eat lots of plants to grow. So if we eat the little pig, all the plants it ate count too. If we eat the plants directly, we use far fewer plants. Even then, eating plants would be the choice that hurts the least.

Ask a question back

"If plants did feel, would you choose the diet that kills more plants or the one that kills fewer?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"What if science discovers tomorrow that plants feel? What will you eat then?"

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Veganism rests on the sentience criterion: those who feel shouldn't be harmed unnecessarily.
  2. P2If plants are sentient, eating plants will also harm sentient beings.
  3. P3Since we have to eat something, it would be impossible to avoid harming sentient beings.
  4. CSo veganism would lose its point, and eating animals would be no worse than eating plants.

P1 is true. P2 and P3 would be true in this hypothetical scenario. The problem lies in the conclusion: from "we can't avoid all harm" it doesn't follow that "it makes no difference how much harm we cause".

The argument is invalid: the impossibility of reducing harm to zero doesn't make every choice equivalent.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument depends on a step that doesn't hold:

    StepWhat it claimsStatus
    If plants feel, every diet causes harmTrue in this scenarioPartly true today already: every diet causes some harm (H2)
    If every diet causes harm, it makes no difference which we chooseFalseHarms can be larger or smaller
    So eating animals is acceptableDoesn't followThe comparison of quantities still decides
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Today the hypothesis has no basis.

    Plant biologists have concluded that plants neither possess nor require consciousness: they have no neurons, no brain and no evolutionary reason for it (A4). Discussing the scenario is useful as an exercise, but it doesn't describe the world as it is.

    2. Even in the scenario, eating plants would cause less harm.

    About 36% of the calories from the world's crops go to animal feed, and only about 12% of those calories reach people as meat, milk and eggs. Eating animals means consuming, indirectly, far more plants. If plants mattered, eating animals would be the worst of both worlds.

    3. Not being able to reach zero harm doesn't license any harm.

    We can't prevent every road accident either, and no one concludes that driving carefully or drunk makes no difference. The ethics of taking sentience seriously has always been about reducing harm, not achieving purity (L12).

    4. The sentience criterion gets stronger, not weaker.

    It follows the evidence: if the evidence showed that plants feel, they would come to count, just as fish, octopuses and crabs came to count as the evidence grew. A criterion that adjusts to the facts is exactly what we should expect from an honest ethics.

    5. There would be degrees to consider.

    If plants had some form of experience, we'd need to estimate its probability and intensity, as we do with insects (A3). That would call for proportionate caution, not indifference.

    6. The human test.

    When it was recognized that more groups of people were capable of suffering than had been admitted, the right conclusion was to extend consideration to them, not to conclude that no one's suffering matters.

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: veganism would lose its clarity

    The most serious version says: veganism's appeal is the clear line between those who feel and those who don't. If plants felt, that line would vanish, and veganism would become a complicated calculation of quantities, without the moral force it has today.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Clarity was never the foundation.

    The foundation is not causing unnecessary harm to those who feel. If the line moved, the obligation would remain, just applied to more beings.

    The calculation already exists today.

    Animal advocates already think in quantities: animals killed in harvests, insects, fish. That doesn't weaken the position; it makes it more precise (H2, A3).

    The calculation would still favor plants.

    Even in the most complicated scenario, eating plants directly would cause less harm than eating those who ate them.

Contradictions

Doesn't apply the logic to other unavoidable harms.

No one concludes it makes no difference whether you drive carefully because accidents happen.

Eats more plants by eating animals.

If plants mattered, the concern would lead to eating plants directly.

Doesn't treat plants as feeling beings today.

The scenario is used only against veganism.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's right that ethics has to stay open to evidence: if it were ever discovered that plants feel, that would have to be taken seriously, and anyone who holds the sentience criterion couldn't ignore it. It's also right that no diet is free of causing some harm.

But

But the discovery wouldn't make everything equivalent. The way to cause the least harm would still be to eat plants, not animals fed on large quantities of them.
