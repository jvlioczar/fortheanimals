I20

"Animals in films, ads and videos are trained, well-cared-for actors"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the "no animals were harmed" credit doesn't guarantee it. In 2013, an investigation by The Hollywood Reporter reported injuries and deaths of animals in productions that received the credit, such as the deaths of 27 animals on a farm used during the filming of The Hobbit, and a tiger nearly drowning on Life of Pi. Outside film, social media videos of "cute" exotic animals can fuel the trade in those animals. Digital effects and footage of free animals make using animals on set less and less necessary.

Short answer

The "no animals were harmed" credit doesn't guarantee it: in 2013, an investigation by The Hollywood Reporter reported injuries and deaths of animals in certified productions, such as the deaths of 27 animals on a farm used during the filming of The Hobbit. Outside film, videos of "cute" exotic animals can fuel the trade in those animals. Digital effects and footage of free animals make using animals on set less and less necessary.

For children

Many films use real animals, and sometimes they get hurt, even when the film says they didn't. Today it's possible to make amazing animals with computers, as in many animated films. And videos of exotic pets can make more people want to buy those animals, which is bad for them.

Ask a question back

"If a whole lion can be created by computer, why put a real lion on a set?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"The end of the film says no animals were harmed. They're trained actors and well cared for."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Audiovisual productions follow animal protection rules.
  2. P2There are bodies that monitor and certify how the animals are treated.
  3. P3Animals trained with positive reinforcement don't suffer at work.
  4. CSo using animals in films and advertising is acceptable.

P1 and P2 are partly true. P3 may hold for some animals. The problem is that monitoring has failed in documented cases, and the use has consequences off the set.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2 and in the conclusion.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    SituationWhat can happen to the animal
    Filming on setStress, transport, risky scenes, long waits
    Between shootsKept at trainers' facilities, not always monitored
    Social media videosExotic animals kept as pets, in inadequate conditions
    Effect on the publicIncreased demand for animals seen in films and videos
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The credit has documented failures.

    The Hollywood Reporter's 2013 investigation reported that the organization behind the credit failed to protect animals in several productions, with injuries and deaths. The organization disputed the report and said it had changed its program.

    2. Monitoring doesn't cover everything.

    The credit covers filming time; breeding, transport and training off set are largely outside it.

    3. The screen influences the trade.

    Videos of exotic animals in "cute" situations make keeping them as pets seem normal, which can increase demand for them.

    4. Technology already substitutes.

    Digital effects create realistic animals, and carefully made footage of free animals shows natural behavior without training for the camera.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    The Hollywood Reporter, 2013Investigation into injuries and deaths of animals in certified productionsThe certifying organization disputed the report
    NPR, 2013Summarizes the investigation and the organization's responseJournalism
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: well-trained animals live well with their trainers

    The most serious version says many animal actors, such as dogs, live with trainers who treat them well, and work for short periods with positive reinforcement.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It may hold for some domesticated animals.

    But not for wild animals, such as tigers, primates and exotic animals, who shouldn't live to entertain.

    The effect on the public remains.

    Even an animal well treated on set may encourage people to buy others, in worse conditions.

    The alternative exists.

    When the scene can be made without animals, the use stops being necessary.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to use animals to entertain, when technology plays the same role and the use encourages other forms of exploitation.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that there are rules and monitoring, that many trainers take good care of their animals, and that films and videos can awaken affection for animals.

But

But monitoring has documented failures, the use encourages buying exotic animals, and technology allows the same stories to be told without animals on set.
