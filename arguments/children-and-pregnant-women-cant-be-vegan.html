G10

"Children and pregnant women can't be vegan"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

the world's largest association of nutrition professionals, the US Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, considers well-planned vegan diets adequate at all stages of life, including pregnancy, lactation and childhood. Other bodies are more cautious: in 2024, the German Nutrition Society neither recommended nor advised against it for these groups, for lack of data. Across all positions, the common ground is careful planning and professional supervision, not a need for meat.

Short answer

The US Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics considers well-planned vegan diets adequate at all stages of life, including pregnancy and childhood. Other bodies, such as the German Nutrition Society, are more cautious and call for nutritional knowledge and professional supervision. The common ground is planning: B12, iodine, vitamin D, iron, omega-3 and enough energy. The serious cases reported in the news involve poorly planned restrictive diets, not well-supervised vegan diets.

For children

Children need lots of nutrients to grow. A vegan child can grow up strong and healthy if the family plans meals well and visits the doctor or dietitian regularly, taking the vitamins they recommend. The important thing is to eat well, with lots of different foods.

Ask a question back

"Is a vegan diet planned with a dietitian the same as a poorly planned diet?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"That's child abuse. The baby won't develop. Pregnant women need meat."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Pregnant women, babies and children have greater and more delicate nutritional needs.
  2. P2Dietary mistakes at these stages can cause serious and sometimes irreversible harm.
  3. P3Vegan diets require planning and supplementation, and data on them in these groups is limited.
  4. CSo children and pregnant women shouldn't follow vegan diets.

P1, P2 and P3 are true. The problem lies in the conclusion: what the premises justify is extra care and supervision, not prohibition.

The argument is valid as a warning. It fails as proof that the diet is impossible or dangerous in itself.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The main bodies don't all say the same thing, but they all converge on one point:

    BodyPositionCommon ground
    Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics (US), 2016Adequate at all stages of life, if well plannedPlanning
    German Nutrition Society, 2024No recommendation for or against for pregnant women and children, for lack of data; calls for knowledge and supervisionPlanning and supervision
    Federal Council of Nutritionists (Brazil), 2022Possible at all life stages, if well planned; cites Brazil's dietary guidelines for children under 2, which accept a vegetarian diet in infancy with extra carePlanning

    None says meat is necessary; the disagreement is about the degree of caution.

  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The most cited nutrition body considers it adequate.

    The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics states that well-planned vegan diets are appropriate for pregnancy, lactation, infancy, childhood and adolescence.

    2. The cautious positions ask for care, not meat.

    In 2024, the German Nutrition Society moved from "not recommended" to "no recommendation for or against" for these groups, and stresses that mistakes can have serious consequences, which requires knowledge and supervision.

    3. Serious cases involve poorly planned diets.

    Reported cases of child malnutrition usually involve extremely restrictive diets, lack of B12 or energy, and no supervision. The same kind of neglect causes malnutrition on any diet.

    4. The critical nutrients are known.

    B12, iodine, vitamin D, iron, zinc, calcium, omega-3 and enough energy. With supplementation and supervision, they can be secured.

    5. Diets with meat can also be inadequate.

    Childhood obesity and anemia are common problems among children who eat meat.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016Well-planned vegan diets are adequate at all stages of life, including pregnancy, lactation, infancy, childhood and adolescenceOfficial position
    Brazil's Federal Council of Nutritionists, technical opinion, 2022 (in Portuguese)A strict vegetarian diet can meet needs at all life stages, if well plannedA Brazilian position
    German Nutrition Society (DGE), 2024 updateFor pregnant and breastfeeding women and children, no recommendation for or against for lack of data; requires nutritional knowledge and supervisionThe more cautious position, softer than in 2016
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: when in doubt, don't take risks with children

    The most serious version says that, given uncertainty and the risk of irreversible harm, the precautionary principle says not to take risks with children, who can't choose.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Precaution calls for supervision, not meat.

    The way to reduce the risk is to plan and monitor.

    Diets with meat carry risks too.

    Precaution would apply to them as well.

    Children don't choose any diet.

    Parents always decide; what matters is deciding responsibly.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    This is the argument where caution is most justified, because it involves those who can't choose. But what it asks for is responsible feeding, not the death of animals. If a well-supervised vegan diet can nourish a child, the moral question still stands.

What the argument gets right

What is true

This argument deserves more respect than many others. Pregnant women and children are vulnerable, mistakes can be serious, and there are serious bodies calling for caution for lack of data. Animal advocates shouldn't play this down.

But

But caution isn't prohibition. What all these bodies ask for is planning, knowledge and professional supervision, and with that a vegan diet can also nourish those just starting out in life.
