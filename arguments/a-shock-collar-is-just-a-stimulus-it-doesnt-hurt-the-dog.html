I32

"A shock collar is just a stimulus; it doesn't hurt the dog"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

a shock collar works because the stimulus is unpleasant: if it didn't bother the dog, it wouldn't stop barking. And it doesn't treat the cause of the barking, which is usually boredom, fear, separation anxiety or lack of exercise. A 2020 study compared training with an electronic collar to reward-based training and found no advantage for the collar; reward-based training had better results for some commands. Wales banned these collars in 2010, and England in 2024.

Short answer

The collar works because the stimulus is unpleasant: if it didn't bother the dog, it wouldn't stop barking. And it doesn't treat the cause, which is usually boredom, fear or separation anxiety. A 2020 study found no advantage of electronic-collar training over reward-based training, which had better results for some commands. Wales banned these collars in 2010, and England in 2024.

For children

A dog barks for a reason: because it's alone, scared, bored or wants to play. A shock collar gives it a nasty fright every time it barks, but it doesn't fix what it's feeling. It's better to find out why it barks and help, with walks, games and company.

Ask a question back

"If the shock didn't bother the dog, why would it stop barking?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"I put a shock collar on the dog to stop him barking. It's just a little vibration, it doesn't hurt."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Excessive barking causes conflicts with neighbors and can lead to the animal being abandoned.
  2. P2A shock collar reduces barking quickly.
  3. P3The stimulus is weak and causes no physical harm.
  4. CSo using the collar is acceptable, and even protects the dog from being abandoned.

P1 is true. P2 may be true in the short term. P3 is self-contradictory: if the stimulus weren't aversive, it wouldn't work. And the conclusion ignores alternatives that treat the cause.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    Common cause of barkingWhat the collar doesWhat solves the cause
    Boredom, lack of exercisePunishes the barkingWalks, play, enrichment
    Separation anxietyPunishes the barking and may increase fearGradual training, company, veterinary help
    Fear or alertnessLinks the stimulus to whatever causes fearDesensitization, safety
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The effect comes from discomfort.

    The collar reduces barking because the dog learns to avoid an unpleasant stimulus. Calling it "just a stimulus" doesn't change how it works.

    2. The cause remains.

    Barking is a symptom. Punishing it can silence the dog without solving the fear, boredom or anxiety, and can worsen its emotional state.

    3. Reward-based training works as well or better.

    China, Mills and Cooper compared, in 2020, training with an electronic collar to other methods, with 63 dogs. The reward-based group responded better to some commands, and the authors found no advantage for the collar. Other researchers criticized limitations of the study.

    4. Some countries have banned them.

    Wales banned shock collars in 2010, and England in February 2024.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    China, Mills and Cooper, 2020, *Frontiers in Veterinary Science*Reward-based training had better results for some commands, with no advantage for the electronic collar63 dogs; the study received methodological criticism
    The Kennel Club, ban in EnglandRemote-controlled shock collars have been banned in England since 1 February 2024British dog organization
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: for some dogs, nothing else worked

    The most serious version says some guardians have tried everything and that the collar was the last option before the dog was returned or abandoned.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Often "everything" didn't include treating the cause.

    Help from a behavioral vet and a trainer who uses positive reinforcement solves many of these cases.

    Silencing the barking isn't solving the suffering.

    An anxious, silent dog is still anxious.

    The comparison with abandonment doesn't justify the method.

    The problem is the lack of support for the guardian, not the lack of shocks.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The question is whether it's fair to cause an animal discomfort to solve our own annoyance, without addressing what it's feeling. When there are methods that work without pain, the choice is clear.

Contradictions

Says it doesn't hurt.

And expects the dog to stop because of it.

Loves the dog.

And doesn't ask why it barks.

Wouldn't use the same on a crying child.

And accepts it for the dog.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that excessive barking causes real conflicts, that guardians can feel they have no way out, and that the collar reduces barking in the short term.

But

But it works through discomfort, doesn't treat the cause, and there are methods that work without pain. The barking problem deserves to be solved from the dog's side.
