H15

"Leather and wool are more sustainable than synthetics, which are plastic"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's true that many synthetics are petroleum-based plastic and shed microplastics. But leather and wool also have a high impact, because they depend on raising animals, and estimates vary a lot depending on who does the math: an analysis by an ethical fashion organization estimated cowhide leather's carbon footprint at about seven times that of synthetic leather per square meter, while data backed by the leather industry cut the impact attributed to leather by more than 60% in an index used by the sector. Neither side settles the question alone, and the choice isn't only between leather and plastic: there are recycled, plant-based and second-hand materials.

Short answer

Many synthetics are plastic, but leather and wool also have a high impact, because they depend on raising animals. Estimates vary with who does the math: an ethical fashion organization put cowhide leather at about seven times the carbon footprint of synthetic per square meter, while leather industry data cut the impact attributed to it by more than 60%. The choice isn't only between leather and plastic: there are recycled, plant-based and second-hand materials.

For children

Some clothes and bags are made of plastic, which takes a long time to disappear from nature. Others are made from animals' skin and hair, and for that the animals have to be raised, eat a lot and be killed. There are also fabrics made from plants, recycled materials or used clothes, which can be the best choice.

Ask a question back

"If the alternative to leather doesn't have to be new plastic, does the argument still favor leather?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Vegan leather is plastic. Real leather is natural, lasts a lifetime and pollutes much less."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Synthetic leathers and fibers such as polyester come from oil and release microplastics.
  2. P2Leather and wool are biodegradable and durable.
  3. P3Leather uses hides that the meat industry would otherwise discard.
  4. CSo leather and wool are more sustainable than synthetics.

P1 is true. P2 is partly true: tanning, often with chromium, reduces biodegradability. P3 depends on how the impact of raising the animal is divided between meat and leather, and that's exactly what's in dispute.

The argument is valid. Its soundness depends on contested calculations.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    MaterialMain impacts
    Cowhide leatherRaising cattle (land, emissions), tanning with chemicals
    WoolRaising sheep (land, methane), practices such as mulesing in some countries
    Synthetic leather and polyesterOil, microplastics, little biodegradability
    Recycled, plant-based and second-handGenerally lower impact; varies by material
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The numbers depend on who calculates.

    The materials index used by the fashion industry (Higg MSI) cut, in 2024, based on data backed by the leather sector, the climate impact of cowhide leather from 36.8 to 14.6 points. Collective Fashion Justice, meanwhile, estimated cowhide leather's carbon footprint at about seven times that of synthetic leather per square meter. Both sources have interests, and the disagreement shows the question is far from settled.

    2. The impact of raising the animal has to count.

    How much of cattle farming's impact is attributed to leather is a methodological choice. Attributing almost nothing to leather, by calling it a byproduct, favors leather (I7).

    3. The comparison is usually with the worst synthetic.

    Recycled and plant-based materials and second-hand clothes can have a lower impact than both.

    4. The environment isn't the only question.

    Even if an animal material had a lower impact, it would still require raising and killing animals.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: leather lasts decades and cheap synthetics are soon trash

    The most serious version says durability changes the math: a leather shoe that lasts ten years may have a lower impact than several cheap synthetic shoes.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Durability depends on quality, not material.

    There are durable non-animal products and disposable leather ones.

    Buying less and using longer applies to any material.

    Durability is an argument for buying well, not for buying leather.

    The animal is still left out of the math.

    Even the most durable leather comes from an animal raised and killed.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The environmental question matters, but it doesn't decide the moral one: a lower-impact material would still require animals to be killed. The best choice is usually the one that avoids both problems.

Contradictions

Criticizes plastic.

And doesn't consider materials that are neither leather nor plastic.

Calls leather a byproduct.

And argues that durability justifies raising animals.

Cares about the environment.

And leaves cattle farming's impact out of leather's account.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that petroleum synthetics are a real environmental problem, that microplastics are a concern, and that durable products are better than disposable ones.

But

But leather and wool also have a high impact, the numbers are disputed, and the choice doesn't have to be between animal and plastic. And even once the environmental question is settled, the moral question remains.
