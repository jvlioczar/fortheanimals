G18

"Ultra-processed vegan foods are worse than meat"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's a false dichotomy. Veganism doesn't require ultra-processed food: rice, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit and grains are vegan, and they're the basis of the Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population, an international reference precisely because it recommends real food. And many processed meats, such as sausages, nuggets and other cured or reconstituted meats, are also ultra-processed, and processed meat is classified as carcinogenic by the WHO.

Short answer

Veganism doesn't require ultra-processed food. Rice, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit and grains are vegan, and they're exactly the basis that Brazil's dietary guidelines, often cited worldwide, recommend. Plant-based burgers are optional, useful for the transition or for variety. And many processed meats, such as sausages, nuggets and other reconstituted meats, are also ultra-processed, and processed meat is classified as carcinogenic. The fair comparison is real food against real food.

For children

Real food is food that comes from nature with few changes: rice, beans, fruit, vegetables. Almost all of it is vegan! Some store products, like sausages and nuggets, have lots of factory ingredients, and that goes for both the meat ones and the vegan ones.

Ask a question back

"Are rice, beans and salad ultra-processed?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Vegan meat is full of chemicals. I prefer real food."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Ultra-processed foods are linked to worse health outcomes.
  2. P2Many vegan products, such as plant-based burgers and cheeses, are ultra-processed.
  3. P3A piece of meat is an unprocessed or minimally processed food.
  4. CSo eating meat is healthier than eating vegan products.

P1 is true. So is P2. P3 is true for some cuts, but not for processed meat. The problem lies in the conclusion: it compares meat with the worst vegan products and ignores most of the vegan diet, which is made up of unprocessed foods.

The argument is valid for the comparison it makes. It fails because it's a false dichotomy.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument compares different categories:

    CategoryWith meatVegan
    Unprocessed or minimally processedCuts of meat, eggs, milkRice, beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit, grains, nuts
    ProcessedCheeses, cured meatsTofu, breads, canned foods
    Ultra-processedSausages, nuggets, bologna and other reconstituted meatsIndustrial plant-based burgers and cheeses

    The fair comparison is within the same category.

  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's a false dichotomy.

    The choice isn't between meat and a plant-based burger. Most of a healthy vegan diet is made up of unprocessed foods.

    2. A leading dietary guideline itself favors a plant diet.

    The 2014 Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population, which popularized the "ultra-processed" category, recommends basing one's diet on unprocessed and minimally processed foods, in great variety and mainly of plant origin.

    3. Many meats are also ultra-processed.

    Sausages, nuggets and other reconstituted meats are ultra-processed, and the WHO's cancer agency classifies processed meat, which also includes cured and smoked meats, as carcinogenic.

    4. Ultra-processed vegan products are optional.

    They can help with the transition and with convenience, but no one needs them. It's like saying eating meat requires eating sausages.

    5. "Chemicals" isn't a criterion.

    Everything is chemistry, including meat. What matters is nutritional composition and the degree of processing.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Brazil's Ministry of Health, *Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population* (2014, in Portuguese; an English edition was published in 2015)Recommends basing one's diet on unprocessed or minimally processed foods, in great variety and mainly of plant origin; avoiding ultra-processed foodsAn international reference
    IARC, press release no. 240 (2015)Processed meat is carcinogenicShows that meat is also among the problematic ultra-processed foods
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: in practice, vegans eat a lot of ultra-processed food

    The most serious version grants that a vegan diet can be made of real food, but says that in practice many vegans depend on industrial products.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Meat eaters also eat a lot of ultra-processed food.

    Consumption of ultra-processed food is high on every diet.

    The problem is ultra-processing, not veganism.

    The recommendation is the same for everyone: eat real food.

    Many traditional plates are already vegan at their base.

    Rice and beans, dal, lentil soups, salads and vegetables.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Concern about ultra-processed food applies to every diet, and doesn't justify eating animals. If it's possible to eat real food without meat, the moral question returns: why choose the option that requires someone's death?

What the argument gets right

What is true

Concern about ultra-processed food is legitimate, and many industrial vegan products aren't healthy. Real food is better than factory products, and veganism shouldn't be presented as a synonym for plant-based burgers.

But

But that concern applies to everyone. The best answer is to eat grains, beans, vegetables and fruit, a diet that in many cuisines is already traditional and almost vegan.
