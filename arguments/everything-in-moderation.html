E5

"Everything in moderation"

The answer in one sentence

moderation is a good rule for things that affect only the person choosing, like sweets or hours of sleep. It doesn't work for harm to others: no one defends stealing "in moderation". The middle ground between causing a harm and not causing it is still causing a harm. The middle position isn't automatically the most reasonable.

Short answer

Moderation makes sense for things that affect only you, like sweets or coffee. For harm to others, it doesn't: no one defends stealing or assaulting "in moderation". The middle ground between causing a harm and not causing it is still causing a harm. And not hurting someone isn't an extreme; it's the starting point. Eating less meat is better than eating more, but that doesn't make what's left justified.

For children

Eating candy "in moderation" makes sense: a little won't hurt you. But hitting classmates "in moderation"? That makes no sense, because hurting others doesn't become right just because it's a little. When someone gets hurt, the best thing isn't to hurt them less: it's not to hurt them.

Ask a question back

"Is there anything you think is wrong to do, even in moderation?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Nothing to extremes. Balance is the secret. Extremes are bad."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Extreme positions tend to be unrealistic and to ignore complexity.
  2. P2Veganism is an extreme position; eating meat without limits is too.
  3. P3Virtue usually lies in the middle.
  4. CSo eating meat in moderation is the most reasonable position.

The strong version rests on an old and respectable idea: Aristotle saw virtue as a mean between excesses. The problem lies in P3 applied to this case: Aristotle himself said some actions have no mean.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2, which calls simply not causing a harm "extreme", and at P3, which doesn't hold for harm to others.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument applies moderation to a kind of choice where it doesn't work:

    Kind of choiceDoes moderation make sense?Example
    Affects only the person choosingYesSweets, alcohol, screen time
    Causes harm to othersNo: the goal is to avoid the harmTheft, assault, cruelty
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's the false middle ground.

    Assuming the position between two options is the right one is a fallacy. The middle between truth and a lie isn't an acceptable half-truth.

    2. Not causing a harm isn't extreme.

    The argument presents "not eating animals" and "eating animals without limits" as two symmetrical extremes. But not doing something that harms another is the starting point of ethics, not an excess.

    3. Moderation is about the eater, not the eaten.

    For the animal who dies, it makes no difference whether the person eating it is moderate.

    4. Aristotle didn't apply the mean to everything.

    In the Nicomachean Ethics, Aristotle says some actions, like theft and murder, admit no mean: they are bad in themselves.

    5. Reducing is better, but it isn't justifying.

    Eating less meat reduces the harm, and that's good (J12). But what remains still needs a justification.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, book IIVirtue is a mean, but some actions admit no meanFull text on Project Gutenberg; the example of actions with no mean, like adultery, is in book II
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: moderation is more realistic

    The most serious version doesn't say moderation is the moral ideal, but that it's what most people can manage, and that asking for total abstention drives people away, while asking for moderation produces more harm reduction.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's about strategy, not justification.

    It can be useful to promote reduction as a step; that doesn't make the remaining consumption right.

    It admits the ideal is something else.

    If moderation is merely the realistic option, the ideal is still not causing the harm.

    For those who can do more, moderation isn't an excuse.

    Realism applies to those with real difficulties (E6).

Contradictions

Doesn't apply moderation to other harms.

No one defends moderate violence.

Wouldn't find it moderate to eat dogs in moderation.

Which shows the real criterion is species.

Admires "extreme" positions of the past.

Abolishing slavery was "extreme" too.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Moderation is a real virtue in many areas, and rigid positions sometimes drive people away. It's also true that eating less meat is better than eating more, and that gradual change is, for many people, the most realistic path.

But

But moderation is a rule for looking after oneself, not for deciding how much harm to cause others. For whoever suffers the harm, there's no middle ground.
