G15

"Plants have antinutrients"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

antinutrients exist, but in the usual amounts and with the usual preparation they matter little, and many of them bring benefits. Cooking, soaking, sprouting and fermenting reduce lectins, phytates and oxalates. Raw beans are harmful; cooked beans are a very nutritious food. And in large studies, eating legumes and whole grains is linked to lower mortality.

Short answer

Antinutrients exist, but cooking, soaking, sprouting and fermenting reduce most of them. Raw beans are harmful; cooked beans are healthy, which is why no one eats raw beans. Many of these compounds, such as phytates and polyphenols, are even linked to benefits. And diets rich in legumes and whole grains are among those most linked to a long life.

For children

Some plants have substances that can cause a bit of trouble, like raw beans. That's why we cook beans! Once cooked, they're tasty and very healthy. And scientists show that cooked beans are good for you.

Ask a question back

"Do you eat your beans raw or cooked?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Beans have lectins. Spinach has oxalates. Plants have poisons to defend themselves."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Plants produce compounds that can reduce nutrient absorption or cause unwanted effects, such as lectins, phytates and oxalates.
  2. P2Plant-based diets contain more of these compounds.
  3. P3These compounds can harm health.
  4. CSo plant-based diets are harmful, and animal foods are safer.

P1 is true. So is P2. The problem lies in P3: with the usual preparation, these compounds have small effects, and several of them are linked to benefits.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3, which ignores dose and preparation.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument ignores dose, preparation and positive effects:

    CompoundWhere it's foundWhat reduces itReal effect
    LectinsRaw beans and grainsCookingPractically eliminated by cooking
    PhytatesGrains, legumes, seedsSoaking, sprouting, fermentingSlightly reduce mineral absorption; linked to antioxidant effects
    OxalatesSpinach, beetrootCooking, varietyRelevant only for people prone to kidney stones
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Dose and preparation matter.

    Lectins are practically eliminated by proper cooking. Phytates and oxalates are reduced by simple techniques.

    2. Many antinutrients bring benefits.

    The Harvard School of Public Health's nutrition page summarizes studies indicating that phytates, polyphenols and other compounds may have protective effects, and that the foods containing them are linked to lower disease risk.

    3. Foods with antinutrients are healthy.

    Legumes, whole grains and vegetables are among the foods most linked to good health and longevity in large studies.

    4. Animal foods also contain problematic compounds.

    Processed meat is carcinogenic, and red meat, probably carcinogenic (G13).

    5. Plants aren't "poison".

    That a plant produces defensive compounds doesn't mean it's toxic to those who prepare and eat it in normal amounts.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Harvard T. H. Chan School of Public Health, antinutrientsAntinutrients are reduced by preparation and many have beneficial effects; the foods that contain them are healthyA reference page on nutrition
    Seidelmann et al., 2018, *Lancet Public Health*Replacing carbohydrates with plant sources was linked to lower mortalityAlso used in G12
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some people are sensitive

    The most serious version grants that, for most people, antinutrients aren't a problem, but points out that some people have sensitivities, such as intolerances or a tendency to kidney stones.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Sensitivities call for adjustments, not meat.

    People prone to stones can cut back on high-oxalate foods.

    These are specific cases.

    They don't justify conclusions for everyone.

    Variety solves it.

    A varied plant diet doesn't depend on any one food.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    If antinutrients are controlled by preparation and the foods that contain them are healthy, the argument doesn't justify eating animals. The question returns: what justifies their suffering when there are safe, nutritious plant foods?

Contradictions

Eats cooked beans.

And feels no effect from the lectins.

Consumes coffee, chocolate and wine.

Which also contain plant defense compounds.

The animals they eat are fed on plants.

Grain and soy in their feed.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that plants produce defense compounds and that some of them can reduce mineral absorption or cause discomfort. Proper preparation matters, and people with specific conditions should take care.

But

But dose and preparation turn what would be a problem into a detail. Foods rich in antinutrients are among the healthiest there are.
