G6

"Milk is necessary for your bones"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

we need calcium, not milk. Calcium is found in dark green vegetables such as kale and broccoli, in tofu made with calcium salts, in fortified plant drinks, in sesame and in legumes. About two thirds of the world's adults lose the ability to digest lactose well, and billions of people live without dairy. Strong bones also depend on vitamin D, physical activity and not smoking.

Short answer

We need calcium, not milk. Calcium is found in kale, broccoli, tofu made with calcium salts, fortified plant drinks, sesame and beans. About two thirds of the world's adults digest lactose poorly. And bone health also depends on vitamin D, physical activity and not smoking. Milk is one source of calcium, not the only one.

For children

Calcium helps make bones strong. It's in milk, but also in kale, broccoli, tofu, sesame and plant milks with added calcium. Playing, running and getting some sun also help your bones a lot.

Ask a question back

"If calcium exists in vegetables and fortified foods, is the need for calcium or for cow's milk?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Milk makes bones strong. You'll get osteoporosis. Children need milk."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Calcium is essential for bone health.
  2. P2Milk is a rich, well-absorbed source of calcium.
  3. P3Without milk, it's hard to reach the recommended amount of calcium.
  4. CSo milk is necessary for bone health.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: there are many plant and fortified sources of calcium, and the need is for the nutrient, not the food.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument confuses the nutrient with the source:

    What bones needPossible sources
    CalciumMilk, but also kale, broccoli, calcium-set tofu, fortified plant drinks, sesame, almonds, beans
    Vitamin DSunlight, fortified foods, supplements
    Mechanical loadPhysical activity
    Adequate proteinLegumes and other sources
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Calcium exists outside milk.

    Low-oxalate dark green vegetables, such as kale and bok choy, have well-absorbed calcium. Tofu made with calcium salts and fortified plant drinks provide amounts similar to milk.

    2. Most of the world's adults don't digest lactose well.

    About 65% to 70% of adults lose lactase, the enzyme that digests milk sugar; keeping it into adulthood is a trait of some populations. Billions of people live without dairy.

    3. Bones depend on more than calcium.

    Vitamin D, physical activity, adequate protein and not smoking are important factors for bone health.

    4. Nutrition bodies consider the diet adequate.

    With planned calcium sources, vegan diets meet requirements.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Harvard T. H. Chan School of Public Health, calciumLists non-dairy calcium sources, such as calcium-set tofu, fortified drinks, kale and bok choy, and explains that spinach has low absorption because of oxalatesA reference page on nutrition
    Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016Well-planned vegan diets, with calcium sources, are adequateOfficial position
    Tong and colleagues, 2020, *BMC Medicine*In EPIC-Oxford, vegans had a higher risk of fractures than meat eaters, especially hip fracturesThe authors point to lower calcium and protein intake and lower body weight as possible factors
    Misselwitz and colleagues, 2017, review on lactaseAbout 65% to 70% of the world's adults don't retain lactaseA review in Current Gastroenterology Reports
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: vegans may take in too little calcium

    The most serious version cites a real finding: in the large British EPIC-Oxford study (2020), vegans had more fractures than meat eaters, especially hip fractures. On average, they also weighed less and took in less calcium and protein. And it's easy to fall below the recommended amount without planning calcium sources.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The finding calls for attention, not milk.

    The authors themselves link the risk to low calcium and protein intake, which can be corrected with plant and fortified sources, vitamin D and physical activity. But it's a point that deserves care and shouldn't be minimized.

    The risk points to planning.

    Information about calcium sources solves most of the problem.

    The moral question remains.

    Even if calcium were easier to get from milk, that wouldn't justify what milk production does to cows and calves (C11).

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Milk isn't necessary for the bones of those with access to other calcium sources. And milk production involves repeated pregnancies, separating calves and slaughtering cows. If there are other ways to get calcium, the question is whether that cost to the animals is justified.

Contradictions

Knows many people are lactose intolerant.

And live well without milk.

Doesn't drink milk from other species.

Cow's milk is a cultural choice.

Doesn't think adults of other mammals need milk.

Adult cows don't drink it.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Calcium is essential, and milk is indeed a rich source of it. Whoever gives up dairy needs to learn about alternative sources, and studies show that very low calcium intakes increase risks. This is a legitimate concern.

But

But what bones need is calcium, vitamin D and movement, not cow's milk. With plant and fortified sources, bones do fine, and so do cows.
