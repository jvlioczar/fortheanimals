H1

"Vegans' soy is destroying the Amazon"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

about 77% of the world's soy becomes animal feed, and only about 7% becomes foods such as tofu and soy milk. Meat eaters consume far more soy, indirectly, than vegans. And the main driver of tropical deforestation is pasture for cattle: between 2005 and 2013, it accounted for about 41% of deforestation in the tropics.

Short answer

About 77% of the world's soy becomes animal feed, and only about 7% becomes tofu, soy milk and other foods. Meat eaters consume far more soy, indirectly, than vegans. And the main driver of tropical deforestation is pasture: about 41% of deforestation in the tropics between 2005 and 2013. If the concern is the forest, the problem lies on the plate with meat.

For children

Most of the world's soy doesn't become tofu: it becomes food for pigs, chickens and cattle. And many forests are cut down to make pasture for cattle. So people who eat meat use much more soy and land than people who eat tofu.

Ask a question back

"If 77% of soy becomes feed, who eats more soy on average: the vegan or someone who eats chicken and pork?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Tofu causes deforestation. You eat the soy that destroys forests."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1The expansion of soy contributes to deforestation in the Amazon and the Cerrado.
  2. P2Vegans eat soy products, such as tofu and soy milk.
  3. P3So vegans contribute to deforestation.
  4. CSo veganism isn't environmentally superior.

P1 is true. So is P2. P3 is true on a small scale. The problem lies in the conclusion: it ignores where almost all soy goes, and who consumes it.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails because it leaves out that livestock farming consumes most of the soy and is the main driver of deforestation.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument ignores where soy goes:

    Where soy goesApproximate share
    Animal feed77%
    Oil, biofuels and industrial usesAbout 16%
    Direct foods (tofu, soy milk, tempeh, edamame)About 7%
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Most soy feeds animals.

    According to Our World in Data, 77% of the world's soy is used as feed. Only a small share becomes direct human food.

    2. Meat uses far more soy.

    Because animals convert feed into meat with large losses, people who eat chicken and pork indirectly consume far more soy than people who eat tofu.

    3. Pasture is the main driver.

    Between 2005 and 2013, the expansion of cattle pasture was responsible for about 41% of tropical deforestation, according to a study by Pendrill and colleagues. Soy, oil palm and other oilseeds together accounted for about 18%, and much of that soy becomes feed.

    4. Plant diets use far less land.

    According to Poore and Nemecek, a global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75%.

    5. Soy can come from sources less linked to deforestation.

    It's possible to prefer traceable soy, and there are many other legumes. The Amazon Soy Moratorium, in force since 2006, already restricts purchases of soy from deforested areas in the Amazon, although the Cerrado remains under pressure.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Our World in Data, soy77% of soy becomes feed; about 7% becomes direct human foodGlobal data
    Our World in Data, drivers of deforestationCattle pasture caused about 41% of tropical deforestation; oilseeds, including soy and oil palm, about 18%Based on Pendrill et al., 2019, data from 2005 to 2013
    Our World in Data, land use and dietsA global vegan diet would reduce farmland by about 75%Based on Poore and Nemecek, 2018
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: tofu soy can also cause deforestation

    The most serious version grants all this, but says that, even in a small share, the soy vegans eat may come from deforested areas.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The proportion matters.

    Eating less meat reduces soy use far more than avoiding tofu.

    Origin can be chosen.

    Vegans can prefer brands with traceable soy, or other legumes.

    The argument favors veganism.

    If the concern is soy, the solution is to eat fewer animals.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The environmental argument, examined properly, favors plant diets. But beyond the forest, there are the animals: the soy that becomes feed nourishes billions of animals raised to die. The moral question returns: what justifies raising and killing them?

Present in a great many kitchens.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Soy expansion does contribute to deforestation, and where food comes from matters for everyone, vegans included. It's legitimate to demand traceability and to call for an end to deforestation linked to any crop.

But

But the data show that soy is mostly feed, and that pasture is the biggest driver of deforestation. If the concern is the forest, a plant diet is the choice that protects it most.
