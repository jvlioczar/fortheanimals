B14

"A lobster boiled alive feels nothing and tastes fresher"

The answer in one sentence

there is serious evidence that decapod crustaceans, such as lobsters and crabs, feel pain. A review of more than 300 studies led the United Kingdom to recognize them as sentient in 2022, and Switzerland has required since 2018 that they be stunned before being killed and banned transporting them alive on ice. Boiling the animal alive is choosing the slowest way to kill it for the sake of freshness.

Short answer

The evidence indicates that lobsters and crabs feel pain: a review of more than 300 studies led the United Kingdom to recognize them as sentient in 2022. Switzerland has required since 2018 that they be stunned before being killed and banned transporting them alive on ice. Boiling the animal alive is choosing the slowest way to kill it, and freshness doesn't depend on it.

For children

Lobsters and crabs move and try to escape when they're put into hot water. Scientists studied them and found that they probably feel pain. That's why some countries require them to be knocked out first, instead of being boiled alive.

Ask a question back

"If the lobster feels nothing, why does it try to escape the pot?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Lobster has to be boiled alive, it's fresher. And it doesn't feel anything, it's basically a sea insect."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Crustaceans have a much simpler nervous system than mammals.
  2. P2Without a complex nervous system, there is no pain.
  3. P3Boiling alive guarantees freshness and food safety.
  4. CSo boiling lobsters alive is acceptable.

P1 is true. P2 is the premise in dispute, and the evidence points against it. P3 has some truth about freshness, but it doesn't require boiling a conscious animal.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P2.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    What the argument assumesWhat the evidence shows
    No cortex, no painAnimals with other nervous structures show signs of pain
    Movement in the pot is a reflexCrustaceans learn to avoid harmful stimuli and trade off benefits to avoid them
    Boiling alive is necessaryThere are electrical or mechanical stunning methods before cooking
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The evidence of pain is serious.

    The review commissioned by the UK government from the London School of Economics assessed more than 300 studies and concluded there is strong evidence of sentience in decapod crustaceans and cephalopods. On that basis, the UK's 2022 animal sentience law included these animals (A2).

    2. Countries have already changed the rules.

    Switzerland, since March 2018, requires lobsters to be stunned before being killed, by electric shock or mechanical destruction of the brain, and banned transporting them on ice.

    3. Freshness doesn't require cruelty.

    Stunning immediately before cooking keeps the freshness. The tradition of boiling alive is habit, not necessity.

    4. Doubt calls for care.

    Even for those who still have doubts, the cost of being wrong is high: if the animal feels, it goes through a slow and painful death.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Birch et al., LSE, 2021Review of more than 300 studies: strong evidence of sentience in decapod crustaceans and cephalopodsCommissioned by the UK government
    UK Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022Includes decapods and cephalopods among sentient animals2022 law
    Time, 2018Switzerland began requiring lobsters to be stunned before cookingRule in force since March 2018
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: science isn't certain yet

    The most serious version points out that some researchers, such as those at the University of Maine's Lobster Institute, doubt that lobsters process pain.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Uncertainty doesn't license the worst method.

    Faced with doubt, choosing the slowest way to kill is betting against the animal.

    The weight of evidence has shifted.

    Governments that reviewed the evidence began protecting these animals.

    The underlying question remains.

    Even stunned, the animal is killed for a dish no one needs to eat.

Biases

Mind denial

Bastian et al., 2012

Status quo bias

The traditional way of cooking seems the only one

Contradictions

Says the lobster doesn't feel.

And explains away the noise in the pot to feel better.

Wants the animal fresh.

And rejects the stunning that would keep it fresh.

Wouldn't accept the same with a cat.

Boiling an animal alive would be obvious cruelty.

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that crustaceans are very different from us, that there is still scientific debate about their experience, and that freshness matters for food safety.

But

But the latest evidence led governments to protect them, and freshness doesn't require boiling the animal alive. When in doubt, care is the reasonable choice, and the next question is whether it needs to be killed at all.
