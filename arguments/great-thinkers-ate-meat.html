D10

"Great thinkers ate meat."

The answer in one sentence

someone's greatness in one field doesn't make all their practices right. Many great thinkers also defended hierarchies among humans that we now reject: Aristotle defended natural slavery. And the list cuts both ways: Pythagoras, Plutarch and Leonardo da Vinci are remembered as critics of eating meat. An authority only counts as an argument when it offers reasons, not by name alone.

Short answer

Great thinkers also got the customs of their time wrong: Aristotle argued that some humans were slaves by nature. So what they did doesn't prove it was right. Besides, the list cuts both ways: Pythagoras, Plutarch and Leonardo da Vinci are remembered for criticizing the eating of meat. What matters isn't who ate what, but what reasons each one gave.

For children

Very smart people make mistakes too. Some great thinkers of the past thought it was right for some people to be enslaved. They were very smart about many things, but they were wrong about that. Being smart doesn't mean everything a person does is right.

Ask a question back

"Aristotle also defended slavery. Does that make slavery right?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Aristotle ate meat. Einstein wasn't vegan. Do you think you're smarter than them?"

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Many of history's greatest thinkers ate meat.
  2. P2These thinkers reflected deeply on ethics.
  3. P3If eating meat were clearly wrong, they would have noticed.
  4. CSo eating meat is probably not wrong.

P1 and P2 are true. The problem lies in P3: these same thinkers missed many injustices we now consider obvious.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3, which assumes great minds don't get the customs of their time wrong.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument uses prestige in place of reasons:

    Legitimate use of authorityFallacious use
    Citing the reasons a thinker gaveCiting only the name or the habit
    Consulting experts in the field in questionTreating general prestige as proof on any subject
    Considering the current consensus of expertsUsing practices from centuries ago as the standard
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's an appeal to authority.

    Someone's intellectual greatness doesn't make all their practices right. A brilliant physicist isn't an authority on ethics.

    2. Great thinkers got a lot wrong.

    Aristotle defended natural slavery and the inferiority of women (C9). Kant held that we have only indirect duties toward animals. Many thinkers accepted the hierarchies of their time.

    3. The list cuts both ways.

    Pythagoras and Plutarch criticized eating meat in antiquity, and Leonardo da Vinci is often remembered as a vegetarian. If the argument held, they would count too.

    4. Habit isn't an argument.

    What a thinker ate isn't part of their philosophy. More relevant would be the reasons they gave, and many never addressed the subject.

    5. Today's ethics has arguments they didn't know.

    Philosophy about animals has developed a great deal since the 1970s, and the science of animal sentience even more (E11).

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Aristotle, *Politics*, book IArgues that some humans are slaves by natureFull text on Project Gutenberg; also used in C9
    Plutarch, *On the Eating of Flesh*An ancient critique of eating meatFull text in English (Loeb), on LacusCurtius

  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: the weight of the philosophical tradition

    The most serious version doesn't cite habits but the tradition: most Western philosophy, from Aristotle to Kant, placed humans above animals, and that should count as evidence.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The same tradition excluded many humans.

    The classical philosophers' moral circle was narrow among humans too.

    Tradition isn't an argument.

    What matters are the reasons, and they're examined in the chapters of family A.

    The tradition is changing.

    A considerable share of today's philosophers accept or lean toward vegetarianism or veganism.

Contradictions

Doesn't follow these thinkers on other topics.

No one defends slavery because Aristotle did.

Doesn't cite the thinkers who criticized meat.

The selection reveals the real criterion.

Disagrees with great thinkers on many subjects.

Prestige decides nothing in those cases.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Great thinkers deserve to be heard, and it's reasonable to hesitate before concluding that all of them were wrong about something. The philosophical tradition has a lot to teach.

But

But listening to great thinkers means examining their reasons, not copying their habits. They themselves taught us to question customs, including the ones they followed.
