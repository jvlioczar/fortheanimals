H3

"Regenerative grazing sequesters carbon"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about the environment and agriculture, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

well-managed grazing can improve soil and store some carbon, but the effect is limited, temporary, reversible and not scalable. A University of Oxford report concluded in 2017 that this sequestration is far smaller than the emissions of the animals themselves. And pasture-raised meat requires even more land per calorie. There's no such thing as "carbon-neutral meat" at scale.

Short answer

Well-managed grazing can help the soil and store some carbon, but not much. The University of Oxford's Grazed and Confused report concluded in 2017 that this gain is small, temporary and reversible, and far smaller than the animals' own emissions. And pasture-raised meat requires even more land per calorie. If that land were rewilded, with forests and native vegetation, it would store much more carbon.

For children

Soil can store a bit of carbon, which is good for the climate. Some people say cattle help the soil store more. But scientists found that cattle release more of the gases that warm the planet than the soil can store. So even with care, cattle still warm the planet.

Ask a question back

"If the soil stops storing carbon after a few years but the cattle keep emitting methane, does the balance stay neutral?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Well-managed cattle restore the soil. Holistic grazing reverses desertification. Carbon-neutral beef."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Degraded soils can recover organic matter with rotational grazing practices.
  2. P2Soil organic matter stores carbon.
  3. P3If the carbon stored offsets the cattle's emissions, meat can be carbon neutral or even negative.
  4. CSo regenerative grazing is a climate solution.

P1 and P2 are true in some contexts. The problem lies in P3: the evidence shows that sequestration is far smaller than the animals' emissions and that it saturates over time.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails at P3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument compares sequestration with zero, not with the emissions or the alternatives:

    FactorRegenerative grazingRewilded land
    Carbon stored in soilSome, temporary, until it saturatesMore, in soil and vegetation
    Methane and nitrous oxide emissionsContinue as long as there are cattleNone from cattle
    Land needed per calorieVery highFreed up
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Sequestration is limited.

    The Grazed and Confused report concluded that carbon sequestration by grazing occurs only under specific conditions, and even then is small and far smaller than the animals' emissions.

    2. Sequestration is temporary.

    The soil reaches a new equilibrium after a few decades and stops accumulating carbon. The cattle's emissions continue.

    3. Soil carbon is reversible.

    A drought, a fire or a change of management can return the carbon to the atmosphere.

    4. Pasture-raised meat uses far more land.

    Per kilocalorie, beef requires about 50 to 100 times more land than plant foods. At scale, there isn't enough land.

    5. The alternative is rewilding.

    If land used for pasture returned to native vegetation, it would store far more carbon than any grazing management.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Garnett et al., *Grazed and Confused* (2017)Carbon sequestration by grazing is small, temporary, reversible and far smaller than the animals' emissionsFood Climate Research Network, University of Oxford
    Our World in Data, land use and dietsBeef requires about 50 to 100 times more land per kilocalorie than plant foodsBased on Poore and Nemecek, 2018
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: it's better than conventional farming

    The most serious version grants that regenerative grazing isn't carbon neutral, but says it's better than conventional intensive farming and should be encouraged.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Better than the worst isn't good.

    Comparing with the worst option hides the best.

    It requires more land.

    If all meat were pasture-raised, far more land would be needed, which would mean more deforestation.

    The relevant comparison is with plant diets.

    No form of livestock farming comes close to them in emissions and land use.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    Even if regenerative grazing were carbon neutral, the cattle would still be killed young. The climate debate is important, but it doesn't answer the moral question: is it fair to kill animals when we don't need to?

What the argument gets right

What is true

Grazing management matters: better practices can restore degraded soils and reduce some harms. In regions that already have cattle, improving management is real progress, and some producers are sincerely committed to it.

But

But the promise of carbon-neutral meat at scale doesn't hold up. The most efficient way to store carbon and free up land is to eat fewer animals and let nature recover.
