G6

"Only fish provides omega-3"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about health and nutrition, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

fish produce little long-chain omega-3 (EPA and DHA): most of it comes from the algae at the base of the marine food chain, which fish eat directly or indirectly. Algal oil supplements provide the same DHA at the source, without the fish. And short-chain omega-3 (ALA) is found in flaxseed, chia and walnuts.

Short answer

Fish make little omega-3: most of it comes from algae, directly or indirectly. Algal oil supplements provide the same DHA, straight from the source. Flaxseed, chia and walnuts provide ALA, another type of omega-3. Fish are middlemen, and they bring risks such as mercury and other contaminants with them. The need is for omega-3, not for fish.

For children

Omega-3 is a fat that's good for the brain. Fish have omega-3 because they eat algae, tiny sea plants that make this fat. We can get omega-3 straight from algae, without needing the fish. And there's omega-3 in flaxseed, chia and walnuts too.

Ask a question back

"If the omega-3 in fish comes from algae, why not go straight to the source?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"You have to eat fish twice a week. Flaxseed doesn't give you DHA."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Long-chain omega-3 (EPA and DHA) is important for the heart, brain and eyes.
  2. P2Common plants provide only ALA, which the body converts to EPA and DHA in small proportions.
  3. P3Oily fish are the main dietary source of EPA and DHA.
  4. CSo you have to eat fish to get adequate omega-3.

P1, P2 and P3 are true. The problem lies in the conclusion: the EPA and DHA in fish come from algae, which can be consumed directly as a supplement.

The argument is valid. Its soundness fails because it ignores the original source of omega-3.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument ignores where omega-3 comes from:

    Type of omega-3Who produces itWhere to find it
    ALA (short chain)PlantsFlaxseed, chia, walnuts, canola oil
    EPA and DHA (long chain)Marine algaeAlgae, algal oil supplements; fish, who accumulate them
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. Algae are the source.

    The EPA and DHA in fish come from the microalgae at the base of the marine food chain. Farmed fish are often given fish oil or algal oil in their feed.

    2. Algal oil provides DHA directly.

    Algal oil supplements are a vegan source of EPA and DHA, recognized by the US National Institutes of Health.

    3. ALA is in common plants.

    Flaxseed, chia and walnuts provide ALA. Conversion to DHA is limited, which is why many dietitians recommend an algal supplement on vegan diets.

    4. Fish carry contaminants.

    Fish can accumulate mercury and other pollutants. Microalgae grown in tanks for supplements are kept away from that contamination.

    5. The recommendation is for omega-3, not fish.

    Advice to eat fish exists because it's a common source, not because it's the only one.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    NIH, omega-3, fact sheet for health professionalsEPA and DHA come from fish and algal oil; ALA comes from flaxseed, chia, walnuts and plant oilsOfficial fact sheet of the US National Institutes of Health
    Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016Well-planned vegan diets are adequate; mentions microalgae as a source of DHAOfficial position
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: supplements aren't the same as food

    The most serious version grants that algal oil provides DHA, but says eating fish also brings other nutrients, and that there are more studies on fish than on algal supplements.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    The other nutrients in fish are in other sources.

    Protein, iodine and vitamin D can be obtained in vegan ways.

    DHA is the same molecule.

    The body doesn't distinguish its origin.

    Fishing has enormous costs.

    An estimated 0.8 to 2.3 trillion fish are caught each year, and fishing damages the oceans (H10).

    Trials of supplements have mixed results.

    That applies to fish oil as much as to algal oil; the argument doesn't favor fish.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    If omega-3 can be obtained straight from algae, the need to fish disappears. And fish, according to current science, are sentient beings (A6). The question becomes moral again: what justifies killing fish to obtain something algae provide directly?

Contradictions

Accepts supplements in other cases.

Many people take fish oil capsules, which are also supplements.

Doesn't eat fish twice a week.

Most people who use the argument don't follow the recommendation.

Worries about mercury.

And mercury is found precisely in fish.

What the argument gets right

What is true

Long-chain omega-3 is important, and it's true that conversion from common plant sources is limited. Vegans should consider a source of DHA, such as algal oil, and it's good to say so clearly.

But

But fish are middlemen. The source is algae, and it's available directly, without fishing and without contaminants.
