J9

"It's hard; I don't have time"

Before answering: an indirect argument

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about cost, practicality and the economy, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended for being cheap, practical or good for the economy, any situation in which exploitation were cheaper or created more jobs would justify it. No one accepts this reasoning when the victims are human: exploiting people was once profitable too.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

initial difficulty isn't impossibility, and inconvenience doesn't justify harm. Changing habits takes effort at the start, like learning anything new. But everyday vegan food can be as quick as food with meat: rice, frozen beans, roasted vegetables, pasta with sauce. And the time it takes to learn a few recipes is small next to the lives of those who stop being killed.

Short answer

Changing takes effort at first, like any learning, but then it gets easy. Everyday vegan food can be as quick as food with meat: rice, beans cooked in bulk and frozen, roasted vegetables, pasta with sauce, sandwiches. And the change can be gradual, one meal at a time. The inconvenience of learning a few recipes doesn't outweigh an animal's life.

For children

Learning to ride a bike is hard at first, but then it gets easy. Changing what you eat is like that too. At first you need to learn new recipes, but soon it becomes normal. And lots of vegan foods are quick: rice, beans, pasta with tomato sauce, fruit.

Ask a question back

"If vegan food could be as quick as what you make today, what else would stop you changing?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"I work all day. I can't cook."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Changing eating habits takes time, information and effort.
  2. P2Many people have little time and many responsibilities.
  3. P3It isn't reasonable to demand hard changes of people who are already overloaded.
  4. CSo the person has no obligation to become vegan.

P1 and P2 are true. P3 has a point: demands should take real conditions into account. The problem lies in the conclusion: the difficulty is mostly at the start, and the change can be gradual.

The argument is valid as a request for understanding. It fails as a permanent justification.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument treats an initial difficulty as permanent:

    StageDifficulty
    First weeksGreater: learning recipes, changing shopping, discovering options
    After a few monthsSmaller: the routine adjusts
    Long termSimilar to any diet
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The difficulty is mostly at the start.

    Like any new habit, vegan eating gets easier with time.

    2. Vegan food can be quick.

    Many simple everyday dishes are already vegan or can be adapted with little effort.

    3. The change can be gradual.

    Starting with a few meals a week and building up reduces the difficulty.

    4. Inconvenience doesn't justify harm.

    The trouble of learning something new doesn't outweigh an animal's life.

    5. Many people with little time make the change.

    Workers, parents, students and people on low incomes become vegan every day.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Brazil's Ministry of Health, *Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population* (2014, in Portuguese)Recommends unprocessed and minimally processed foods, mainly of plant origin, and gives practical advice for cooking with little timeAlso used in G14
    Faunalytics, study of current and former vegetarians and vegans13% of those who quit cited inconvenienceShows the difficulty is real for some, and that practical support matters
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: some people really can't

    The most serious version points out that some people work exhausting hours, care for others and have nowhere to cook, and that demanding hard changes of them is unfair.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    That justifies exceptions and one's own pace.

    Veganism is "as far as is possible and practicable" (D3).

    It doesn't apply to most people.

    Those who use the argument can usually make at least some changes.

    It calls for support, not giving up.

    Simple recipes and ready-made options help.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The difficulty is real, and it deserves understanding. But it doesn't answer the moral question: does the trouble of changing justify continuing to pay for animals to be killed? For most people, the answer involves starting, even if slowly.

Biases

Status quo bias

The current habit seems easier than any change

Present bias

Immediate effort weighs more than future benefit

What the argument gets right

What is true

Lack of time is real for many people, and changing eating habits takes effort. Overloaded people deserve understanding, not judgment, and the vegan movement should offer simple, practical paths.

But

But the difficulty is greatest at the start, and the change can be gradual. With a few recipes and a new routine, vegan food becomes as practical as any other.
