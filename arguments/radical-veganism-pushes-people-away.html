K6

"Radical veganism pushes people away."

Before answering: an indirect argument

This argument is about effectiveness and individual responsibility, not whether animals deserve respect. Many activists question this kind of argument in defending animals, because it sidesteps what matters: no one defends the rights of people who face discrimination by saying that treating them fairly is good for the environment.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended only by each person's impact, anyone who proved their purchase changes nothing would be let off. But no one accepts this for other harms: taking part in an injustice is still wrong even when each person's share is small.

When used to justify exploitation, arguments like this one usually fail twice. They get the facts wrong, which often arrive distorted. And they get the morality wrong, because they leap from what is to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it.

So show the facts and correct what is wrong, but always come back to what matters: respect for animals and justice toward them. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

there may be some strategic truth here: certain tones and tactics put some people off. But that isn't a moral refutation, nor a personal excuse. That a messenger is unpleasant doesn't make the message false. And research on social movements shows that radical voices can even increase support for moderate ones, the so-called radical flank effect.

Short answer

It may be that some tones and tactics put some people off, and it's worth discussing the best way to communicate. But that doesn't change whether it's right to eat animals. An unpleasant messenger doesn't make the message false. And research on social movements suggests radical voices can even increase support for moderate ones. The question for you remains: do animals deserve to be spared, regardless of who speaks for them?

For children

Sometimes a person speaks in a way that makes us angry, even when they're right. But the way they speak doesn't change whether something is right or wrong. If someone rudely warns us that we're hurting someone, it's still worth stopping.

Ask a question back

"If a vegan were kind to you, would the answer about the animals change?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"You scare people off. Why don't you just promote reduction? Toxic veganism."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Messages perceived as radical or judgmental provoke rejection.
  2. P2Rejection reduces people's willingness to change.
  3. P3A more moderate approach, such as encouraging reduction, would reach more people.
  4. CSo radical veganism harms its own cause.

P1 and P2 have support in some contexts. P3 is a plausible strategic hypothesis. The problem is that the conclusion, even if correct, is about strategy, not about whether it's right to eat animals.

The argument is valid as a strategic criticism. It fails when used as a justification for not changing.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument mixes a strategic question with a moral one:

    QuestionTypeDoes the argument answer it?
    What's the best way to persuade people?StrategicPartly
    Is it right to eat animals?MoralNo
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The form doesn't change the content.

    That a message is delivered unpleasantly doesn't make it false.

    2. It isn't a personal excuse.

    Other people's tone doesn't excuse anyone from examining the question for themselves.

    3. The radical flank effect can be positive.

    In 2022, experiments with more than 2,700 participants, including one about the animal movement, found that the presence of a radical group increased support for moderate groups in the same movement, who came to seem more reasonable by comparison.

    4. "Radical" often just means consistent.

    Saying animals shouldn't be killed without need is called radical, but it applies a principle almost everyone accepts.

    5. Eating more meat out of irritation is a curious effect.

    If irritation with vegans leads someone to harm animals more, the problem isn't with the animals.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Simpson, Willer and Feinberg, 2022, *PNAS Nexus*The presence of a radical flank increased support for moderate groups in the same movement, including the animal movementPerception experiments; the effect comes from tactics, not goals
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: strategy matters for the animals

    The most serious version says that if the goal is to help animals, the effectiveness of communication is a moral question, because it determines how many animals are spared.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It's valid for activists.

    Animal advocates should think about the best strategy.

    It doesn't apply to whoever receives the message.

    For that person, the question is still about their own plate.

    Different strategies can coexist.

    Moderate and firmer voices can complement each other.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The argument is about vegans, not animals. But animals don't choose who speaks for them. The moral question stays the same, whoever the messenger: is it fair to make them suffer and die when we don't need to?

What the argument gets right

What is true

The way we communicate matters, and some animal advocates do push people away with judgment, aggression or contempt. Discussing communication strategies is legitimate and necessary, and the movement should learn from criticism.

But

But criticism of the messenger doesn't answer the message. Whatever the tone of whoever speaks, the question about the animals is still waiting for an answer.
