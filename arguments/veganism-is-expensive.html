J1

"Veganism is expensive"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about cost, practicality and the economy, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended for being cheap, practical or good for the economy, any situation in which exploitation were cheaper or created more jobs would justify it. No one accepts this reasoning when the victims are human: exploiting people was once profitable too.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

beans, rice, lentils, grains, potatoes, oats and seasonal vegetables and fruit are among the cheapest foods there are. What's expensive is some processed products, such as plant-based cheeses and meats, which are optional. A 2021 study of 150 countries concluded that vegetarian and vegan diets were generally the cheapest of the healthy and sustainable dietary patterns, and can cut food costs by up to about a third in upper-middle and high-income countries.

Short answer

Beans, rice, lentils, chickpeas, potatoes, oats and seasonal vegetables and fruit are among the cheapest foods in the shop, and they're vegan. What's expensive is the processed products that imitate meat and cheese, and they're optional. A 2021 study of 150 countries concluded that vegetarian and vegan diets were generally the cheapest of the healthy and sustainable dietary patterns.

For children

Rice, beans, potatoes, bananas, carrots, oats and lentils are cheap foods, and they're vegan. What's expensive is some store products that imitate meat or cheese. But nobody needs them to be vegan.

Ask a question back

"Is a kilo of beans more or less expensive than a kilo of meat?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Vegan food is for the rich. Vegan cheese costs a fortune."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Processed vegan products, such as plant-based meats and cheeses, tend to cost more than their animal-based equivalents.
  2. P2Many people are on a tight budget.
  3. CSo veganism is expensive and out of reach for many.

P1 is true for many products. So is P2. The problem lies in the conclusion: it confuses veganism with processed products, which aren't necessary.

The argument is valid for those who depend on such products. Its soundness fails because the basis of a vegan diet is cheap.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument compares specific products, not diets:

    Type of foodRelative price
    Beans, rice, lentils, potatoes, oats, seasonal vegetablesAmong the cheapest
    Meat, cheese, fishMore expensive
    Processed plant-based meats and cheesesOften expensive, but optional
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. The basis of a vegan diet is cheap.

    The foods that make up a healthy vegan diet are among the cheapest, and many are already staples of traditional cuisines around the world.

    2. Studies confirm it.

    Springmann and colleagues analyzed diets in 150 countries and concluded that vegetarian and vegan variants were generally the most affordable of the healthy and sustainable diets, with cost reductions of up to about 22% to 34% in upper-middle and high-income countries.

    3. Processed products are optional.

    No one needs vegan cheese to be vegan, just as no one needs imported cheese to eat cheese.

    4. Meat is expensive.

    In many places, protein from beans and lentils costs a fraction of protein from meat.

    5. The situation varies.

    In low-income countries, healthy diets in general cost more than current diets, which is a problem of poverty, not of veganism.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Springmann et al., 2021, *Lancet Planetary Health*Vegetarian and vegan diets were generally the most affordable healthy and sustainable patterns; up to 22% to 34% cheaper in upper-middle and high-income countriesAn analysis of 150 countries; in low-income countries, healthy diets in general cost more
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: time costs too

    The most serious version grants that basic foods are cheap, but says cooking takes time and skill, and ready-made products with meat are more convenient for people who work long hours.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    It changes the argument.

    It stops being about money and becomes about time (J3).

    There are convenient, cheap options.

    Beans cooked in bulk and frozen, rice, roasted vegetables, canned legumes.

    Ready-made products with meat aren't healthy either.

    And they're often more expensive.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    If a vegan diet can be cheaper, the cost argument doesn't justify eating animals. The moral question remains: for those who can choose what they eat, what justifies choosing the option that requires someone's death?

Contradictions

What the argument gets right

What is true

It's true that many processed vegan products are expensive, and that price is a real barrier for those who depend on them. It's also true that in some places access to fresh, varied food is limited (J2).

But

But the basis of vegan eating is made up of some of the cheapest foods there are. For most people, veganism can cost less, not more.
