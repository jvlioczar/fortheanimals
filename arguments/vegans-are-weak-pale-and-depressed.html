G12

"Vegans are weak, pale and depressed"

Before answering: an indirect argumentThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

The answer in one sentence

it's a stereotype. There are vegans of every kind, including elite athletes. On depression, some meta-analyses find an association between vegetarian diets and depressive symptoms, but the direction isn't clear: there are signs that people start avoiding meat after a diagnosis, not the other way round. A correlation doesn't tell us what causes what.

Short answer

It's a stereotype: there are vegans of every kind, including elite athletes. On depression, some meta-analyses find an association, but the studies are mostly cross-sectional and don't show what causes what. There are signs that many people start avoiding meat after a diagnosis, not the other way round. Deficiencies in B12 or iron can affect mood, but they're preventable with planning.

For children

You can't tell what someone eats just by looking at them. There are strong vegans, athletes, doctors, artists and people of every kind. Thinking that everyone in a group is the same isn't fair, and it isn't true.

Ask a question back

"If many people stop eating meat after a diagnosis, did the diet cause the depression, or the other way round?"

Como pode aparecer

The strongest version

How it is said

"Every vegan looks sick. They're missing meat in their heads."

Strongest version (steelman):

  1. P1Some studies link vegetarian and vegan diets to a higher risk of depressive symptoms.
  2. P2Deficiencies in B12, iron and omega-3 can affect mood and energy.
  3. P3So the vegan diet can cause weakness and depression.
  4. CSo the vegan diet harms physical and mental health.

P1 is true: some meta-analyses find an association. So is P2. The problem lies in P3: the association doesn't show causation, and the deficiencies are preventable.

The argument is valid if P3 is true. Its soundness fails because P3 isn't established.

Full answer

  1. a

    Reconstruction and diagnosis

    The argument mixes a stereotype with a statistical association:

    ClaimStatus
    Vegans are weak and paleStereotype: there are vegans of every kind
    There's an association between vegetarianism and depressive symptomsFound in some meta-analyses
    The diet causes depressionNot established: the direction may be reversed
  2. b

    Where the argument breaks

    1. It's a stereotype.

    Appearance doesn't reveal diet. High-level vegan athletes and vegans of all ages contradict the image (G11).

    2. The association doesn't show the direction.

    Meta-analyses from 2021 found an association between vegetarian diets and depressive symptoms, but most of the studies are cross-sectional, and the authors themselves point out that it's impossible to know whether the diet leads to depression or the reverse.

    3. There are signs of reverse causation.

    A longitudinal German study found that many people began avoiding meat after a diagnosis of a mental disorder. Sensitivity to others' suffering may also be linked to both factors.

    4. The deficiencies are preventable.

    A lack of B12, iron or omega-3 can affect energy and mood, but it's prevented with supplementation and planning (G4, G5, G7).

    5. There's also a social hypothesis.

    People who perceive animal suffering and live with its normalization may feel isolation and distress. It's a hypothesis, still little studied, not a conclusion; but it shows that not every association with diet is nutritional.

  3. c

    Data and sources

    SourceWhat it saysNote
    Ocklenburg and Borawski, 2021, *Journal of Affective Disorders*Vegetarians had higher depression scores, with great heterogeneity between studiesThe authors stress that the direction of the relationship can't be determined
    Michalak, Zhang and Jacobi, 2012Adopting a vegetarian diet tended to happen after the onset of mental disorders; there was no evidence of a causal role for the dietA representative sample from Germany, with more than 4,000 people
  4. d

    The strongest counter-reply: even without certainty, there's a risk

    The most serious version grants that causation is uncertain, but says that, given an association, it would be prudent not to adopt a vegan diet.

  5. e

    Why the counter-reply doesn't reach the conclusion

    Prudence calls for supervision, not meat.

    Preventing deficiencies takes care of the nutritional part of the risk.

    If the cause is reversed, changing diet doesn't help.

    Going back to meat wouldn't treat depression that came before the diet.

    Diets with more plants are linked to benefits in other outcomes.

    The body of evidence doesn't favor meat.

  6. f

    Back to the moral question

    The stereotype isn't an argument, and the association with depression doesn't show that meat is necessary. The question becomes moral again: if vegans can be healthy with planning, what justifies the animals' suffering?

Contradictions

What the argument gets right

What is true

The association between vegetarian diets and depressive symptoms appears in some studies and deserves serious investigation. Nutritional deficiencies can affect mood, and vegans should take care of them. It's also true that some vegans are in poor health, like people on any diet.

But

But a stereotype isn't evidence, and an association isn't a cause. With planning, a vegan diet supports healthy bodies and minds.
