Nirvana fallacy

If it isn't perfect, it doesn't count

What it is

Rejecting a solution because it is not perfect, by comparing it with an impossible ideal. Reducing a harm is still worthwhile even when it cannot be eliminated entirely.

An analogy: Giving up seat belts because they do not prevent every road death.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this error appears in 13 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • C7 We are animals and part of the ecosystem; no one lives without impact: Because harm can't reach zero, it treats reducing and not reducing as equivalent
  • E10 Nobody's perfect; I do what I can: Since perfection is impossible, nothing needs to be done
  • F5 What about the insects you kill while driving?: If not all harm can be avoided, none would need to be
  • F8 What if they discover that plants feel?: If harm can't be zero, its size doesn't matter
  • H2 Harvests kill animals; vegans kill too: Since no diet is perfect, all would be equivalent
  • H3 Being vegan isn't enough: if you really cared, you'd only eat from gardens or pesticide-free farms: Either total purity, or veganism isn't enough
  • I3 If you take medicine, you can't oppose testing: Demands total purity before allowing any criticism
  • K5 There is no ethical consumption under capitalism: If nothing is perfect, nothing matters
  • K10 Vegans will always be a minority: If the ideal world isn't reached, no step would be worth it
  • L11 You use a phone, so you're a hypocrite: Assumes that if perfection isn't possible, trying isn't worth it
  • L12 Veganism isn't 100% cruelty-free: If it isn't perfect, it doesn't count
  • L13 A vegan who buys from companies using slave labor isn't really vegan: If the vegan isn't consistent in everything, veganism doesn't count

In helping wild animals

  • N12 It's impossible to solve suffering in nature; it's too big and complex: If everything can't be solved, nothing is worth doing

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V33 This partial law is unfair to the other animals: It proves too much: since all exploitation can't be banned at once, a real reduction in suffering is better than none.
  • V59 Food that may contain traces of milk isn't vegan: "May contain traces" is a warning for people with allergies: the product went through machines that also process that ingredient.
  • V62 Anyone who buys products from big companies isn't vegan: Veganism is about the exploitation of animals. A vegan product from a big company involves no animal exploitation in the product itself.
  • V64 Anyone who supports step-by-step change isn't really vegan: Veganism is defined by refusing animal exploitation as far as is possible and practicable, not by a position on strategy.
