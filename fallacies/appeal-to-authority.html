Appeal to authority

"So-and-so said it or did it"

What it is

Accepting a conclusion because someone important defended or practiced it, instead of examining the reasons. Experts deserve attention in their field, but a name does not replace an argument, and competence in one area does not transfer to another, such as ethics.

An analogy: Buying a medicine because a famous actor said it works.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this error appears in 7 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • B9 Animal scientists take good care of the animals: Uses technical competence to settle a moral question
  • D8 Great thinkers ate meat: The name replaces the reasons
  • D10 God gave us dominion over animals: "God said so" ends the discussion
  • D11 Jesus ate fish: One figure's example settles the question
  • E8 AIs say that not being vegan isn't wrong: Takes the AI's answer as a verdict, without examining the reasons
  • G15 My doctor told me to eat meat: Individual opinion replaces the evidence
  • I2 Science shows animal testing works, so it's ethical: Transfers technical authority to the moral question

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V20 Jesus was a vegetarian: The Gospels describe Jesus eating and offering fish, and the theories that he was vegetarian, for example through a link with the Essenes, have no solid historical basis.
  • V21 Great geniuses like Einstein and Da Vinci were vegan: It's the same appeal to authority as the opposing argument, just from the other side.
  • V27 God didn't make animals for humans: Religious arguments of this kind depend on belief, not on reasons anyone can share, so they don't persuade people of a different faith.
  • V47 Every nutrition body in the world approves vegan diets for everyone: It overstates the consensus: several bodies endorse a well-planned vegan diet, but some are cautious about pregnancy, children and older people.
