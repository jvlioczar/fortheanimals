Hasty generalization

Drawing a rule about everyone from a few cases

What it is

Drawing a conclusion about all from a few cases, or from cases that do not represent the whole. A few bad experiences do not say what the whole group is like.

An analogy: Getting poor service at one shop and concluding that every shop in town is bad.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this error appears in 36 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V1 Going vegan will make you healthier: It's imprecise: any badly planned diet carries risks.
  • V4 Going vegan prevents pandemics: It claims more than the evidence supports: most diseases that pass from animals to people come from wild animals, not farms.
  • V6 Meat eaters are psychopaths: It's inaccurate and counterproductive. Most of these people have genuine emotional bonds.
  • V7 Non-vegans are our enemies, and hating them is normal: Almost every vegan was once a non-vegan. People who eat animals today are, in general, where we used to be: repeating a habit learned in childhood, without ever having stopped to examine it.
  • V15 Being vegan is very expensive: Coming from a vegan, the phrase confirms one of the myths most used against veganism, and pushes away people who might join.
  • V17 All animals are slaughtered fully conscious, without stunning: It's a generalization: the EU, Brazil and the US require stunning before slaughter for most farmed animals, though US federal law excludes poultry.
  • V22 Animals are skinned alive to make fur coats: There are documented cases, but they aren't the industry's standard method, and generalizing them undermines the speaker's credibility.
  • V41 It's proven that meat played no role in human evolution: It overstates a real study: the data weaken one hypothesis about meat in evolution, but don't prove it played no role.
  • V45 All the B12 in meat comes from supplements: It's an exaggeration. Farmed pigs and chickens get B12 in their feed, but in cattle B12 is produced by bacteria in the rumen from cobalt, which doesn't always need to be added.
  • V47 Every nutrition body in the world approves vegan diets for everyone: It overstates the consensus: several bodies endorse a well-planned vegan diet, but some are cautious about pregnancy, children and older people.
  • V48 Doctors don't know anything about nutrition: It's a generalization. Nutrition training in medical school is often short, but that doesn't mean no doctor understands the subject, and many specialize in it.
  • V52 It's proven that vegans are more empathetic: The claim usually relies on a 2010 neuroimaging study of 60 people, which measured brain activation in response to images, not behavior.
  • V54 Oysters and mussels feel pain like any other animal: The evidence of sentience in bivalves is weak: they have very simple nervous systems with no centralized brain, and even animal advocates admit they may not feel.
  • V57 Animal testing is useless: 90% of drugs fail: It's an exaggeration. About 90% of drug candidates fail in human trials, and species differences are one of the causes.
