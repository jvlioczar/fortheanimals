Cherry picking

Selecting only the favorable data

What it is

Choosing only the data, studies or examples that favor one's position and ignoring those that contradict it. Each piece of data cited may be true, and the picture presented may still be false.

An analogy: Showing only the photos where the team is winning and concluding it never lost.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this error appears in 17 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A1 Animals aren't conscious and don't feel pain like we do: Cites only one side of the debate (for example Key, 2016) and omits the dozens of replies
  • A2 Fish and invertebrates don't feel pain: Cites the minority position and omits the published replies
  • A9 Humans are superior, so we can use the inferior: Counts only the capacities in which humans excel
  • C1 It's natural to eat meat: Picks from the human past only what confirms the habit
  • D8 Great thinkers ate meat: Cites thinkers who ate meat and omits those who didn't
  • G5 You'll lack iron, zinc and other micronutrients: Ignores that iron deficiency is common among people who eat meat and other animal products
  • G8 Plants have antinutrients: Highlights the compounds and ignores the foods' benefits
  • G16 A carnivore, paleo or keto diet is healthiest: Selects short-term studies and ignores cohorts
  • G17 The WHO exaggerates; red meat isn't bad for you: Selects criticisms and ignores the whole
  • H5 Avocados and almonds use a lot of water: Picks the plants that use the most water
  • H9 Regenerative grazing sequesters carbon: Uses specific cases of sequestration and ignores the whole
  • H11 Cattle prevent wildfires; they're firefighter cattle: Cites prevention and omits the fire used to clear pastures
  • H13 Local, organic meat is sustainable: Grass-fed beef usually needs more land and emits as much or more per kilo
  • H16 Leather and wool are more sustainable than synthetics, which are plastic: Uses the calculation that favors leather without mentioning others
  • I2 Science shows animal testing works, so it's ethical: Cites only the 86% concordance or only the 5% approval
  • J19 Veganism has become big business: Looks at the vegan sector's profit and ignores the meat sector's

In the long term and with new minds

  • N25 The future will certainly be better, so there's no need to worry so much: Uses the decline of violence among humans and ignores the growth of animal exploitation

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V28 Pigs are smarter than a three-year-old child: The studies behind the comparison are weak and weren't replicated, and measuring the intelligence of species by a single measure has no solid basis.
  • V53 Koko the gorilla held conversations in sign language: Koko learned many signs, but the claim that she used a language, with grammar and new sentences, does not hold up: the interpretation depended heavily on her trainers.
  • V55 Livestock pollutes more than all cars, planes and ships combined: The phrase comes from the 2006 FAO report, which compared livestock's 18% of emissions with about 13% for transport.
  • V56 Livestock causes 51% of the world's greenhouse gas emissions: The figure comes from a 2009 piece published by the Worldwatch Institute, which counted, for example, animal respiration, something international methodologies do not count as emissions.
