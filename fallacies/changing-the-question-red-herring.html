Changing the question (red herring)

Swapping "is it wrong?" for another question

What it is

Diverting the discussion to another subject, which may even be true and interesting, but does not answer the question under debate. Also called a red herring.

An analogy: Asked whether he did the homework, the student answers by talking about how unfair the teacher is.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this error appears in 46 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A3 Where do you draw the line? Insects? Bacteria?: Swaps farmed animals for bacteria and insects
  • A12 It's natural to put our own first: Swaps "may I exploit?" for "may I prefer?"
  • B1 There is humane slaughter: Answers "how to kill" when the question is "whether killing is acceptable"
  • B4 Free-range animals are happy: Answers about life when the question includes death
  • B9 Animal scientists take good care of the animals: Answers how the animal lives and stays silent about whether it should die
  • C11 Cows need to be milked: Talks about the full udder and sets aside the cow's exploitation and death
  • D4 My grandparents ate meat and lived to 90: Answers about health to a question about justice
  • D14 I'll pay for my mistakes in my next life: Moves from the harm to the animal to the consequence for the one who eats
  • D15 Being vegan doesn't make you more spiritually evolved: Moves from the harm to the animal to the spiritual worth of whoever is vegan
  • E1 It's my personal choice; don't impose your morals: Swaps "is it wrong?" for "who gets to decide?"
  • E2 The state can't ban what I eat: From "is it wrong?" to "should it be banned?"
  • E13 He eats meat, but he's a good person: Swaps "does the act harm someone?" for "is the person good?"
  • E17 Being vegan is admirable, but it goes beyond duty: Praise for the person replaces the question about the harm to animals
  • E18 Why should I be ethical if it doesn't benefit me?: Answers "what's in it for me?" when the question is "is it right?"
  • F11 What about meat that gets thrown away?: From buying to leftovers
  • H14 What matters is how food is grown, not whether it comes from animals: Answers "how was it produced?" in place of "was anyone killed?"
  • H15 If meat is sustainable, there's no problem: Swaps the moral question for the environmental one
  • H21 If everyone goes vegan, we'll have to clear forests to grow crops: Swaps the question about animals for one about land
  • I37 Feeding a dog or cat vegan food is animal abuse: Uses the cat's diet to discuss the human diet
  • J6 Meat tastes good: Answers "do I like it?" when the question is "is it fair?"
  • J14 Vegans are against farmers: Swaps "is it just to kill animals?" for "are you grateful to those who feed you?"
  • J15 Farmers and slaughterhouse workers would lose their jobs: An argument used against every moral reform; transitions can be planned
  • J18 Billions of people depend on fish for protein: Swaps "is it just to kill?" for "how do we kill quickly?"
  • K4 The problem is the industry, not consumers: Swaps "should I buy it?" for "who is most to blame?"
  • K9 Radical veganism pushes people away: From the moral question to the strategic one
  • L1 Vegans are annoying and think they are better: Tone matters more than the animals
  • L2 Vegans are all hippies: Swaps the question about animals for one about people
  • L5 Vegan activists are radicals and extremists: Methods matter more than the animals
  • L16 Vegans are speciesists too: The vegan's character takes the place of what happens to animals
  • L19 There are sexist, racist and transphobic vegans: The messenger's consistency takes the place of what happens to animals
  • L22 Comparing animal farming to slavery or the Holocaust is offensive: From the question of animals to the way of speaking
  • L23 Vegans are racist, because they criticize Afro-Brazilian religions: The debate about the critic takes the animal's place
  • L24 Vegans are against religion: The messenger's faith takes the animal's place
  • L27 Vegan documentaries are manipulated: The quality of the film takes the place of what happens to animals
  • L29 Vegans treat vegetarians as enemies: Relations among humans take the place of what happens to cows and hens
  • L30 Vegans think they're the only ones who really defend animals: The contest of merits takes the animals' place
  • L31 Defending animals is an emotional appeal, not an argument: The form of the message replaces the question about animals
  • L32 Defending animals is middle-class moralism: The advocates' class replaces the question about animals
  • L33 Animal advocates want to get rid of pets: The discussion about companion animals replaces the question about animals bred for food
  • L35 My food beats yours: Swaps "is it just?" for "is it tasty?"
  • L36 Look what you're missing: Swaps "is it just?" for "is it tasty?"
  • L37 I hate vegans: The feeling about vegans replaces the question about animals
  • M7 I don't want to be labeled; the label brings pressure: Answers "how do others see me?" when the question is "what happens to the animals?"
  • M8 It became an obsession; I lived with guilt: Swaps "what is fair to the animals?" for "was I being pure enough?"
  • M9 I was vegan and started getting too radical, too rigid: Swaps "what is fair to the animals?" for "what kind of person did I become?"
  • M10 I used to be vegan; I know what I'm talking about: Answers "was it hard?" when the question is "is it right?"

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V3 Go vegan for the planet: If the reason is the environment, cleaner animal farming would solve the problem without solving anything for the animals.
  • V4 Going vegan prevents pandemics: It claims more than the evidence supports: most diseases that pass from animals to people come from wild animals, not farms.
  • V5 Superbugs come from the antibiotics given to animals: It has a real basis, but it moves animals out of the center: antibiotic use can be regulated without ending exploitation.
  • V51 Vegans are sexier and better in bed: It takes the focus off the animals and appeals to the listener's self-interest.
