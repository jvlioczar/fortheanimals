Equivocation

Using the same word in two different senses

What it is

Using the same word in two different senses within the same reasoning, as if it were one. The conclusion seems to follow from the premises, but only because the meaning of the word shifted along the way.

An analogy: "A feather is light; what is light is not dark; so a feather is not dark."

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this error appears in 47 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A4 Plants feel too: Uses "feel" and "communicate" in a metaphorical sense and concludes in the literal sense
  • A9 Humans are superior, so we can use the inferior: "Superior" shifts from descriptive to evaluative
  • A13 I respect animals, but humans come first: "Coming first" oscillates between "greater harm" and "trump card"
  • A15 Animals aren't rational: "Rational" changes meaning between P1 and P2
  • A16 Animals don't have language: "Language" alternates between communication and language with grammar
  • A17 Animals don't have souls: "Soul" alternates between principle of life, mind and immortal soul
  • A20 Animals aren't persons: Alternates between biological, psychological and legal senses
  • B10 The law guarantees animal welfare: "Welfare" used to mean "within the rules", not being well
  • B16 It's a fair trade: we care for them and feed them, and they give us meat: "Trade" used without the consent the word presupposes
  • B17 Animals don't mind, they even like it and, in a way, they consent: "Accepting" used in the sense of consenting
  • B18 The problem is suffering, not use: "Use" shifts from ideal, harmless use to real use, with death
  • B19 Eating animals isn't cruelty; it's for nourishment: "Cruelty" shifts from a character trait (sadism) to a description of what the animal suffers
  • C1 It's natural to eat meat: "Natural" changes meaning: statistical, evolutionary, biological, non-artificial
  • C2 We have canine teeth; we are omnivores: Uses "omnivore" as if it meant "carnivore"
  • C3 We naturally crave meat: Uses "the body asks for it" as if it meant "the body needs it"
  • C8 We're at the top of the food chain: Uses "top" sometimes as trophic level, sometimes as hierarchy of value
  • C9 Might makes right: Confuses "fit" with "strong"
  • C10 Farm animals exist for this: Confuses assigned function with one's own good
  • D5 I don't want to dishonor my parents and my ancestors: Uses "honor" now as respecting people, now as repeating their acts
  • D10 God gave us dominion over animals: "Dominion" read as ownership, not as responsibility
  • E4 Eating meat isn't an ideology; veganism is: The dominant norm is invisible to those who follow it (carnism, Joy 2009)
  • E17 Being vegan is admirable, but it goes beyond duty: "Admirable" comes to mean "optional"
  • G1 Eating animals is necessary to survive: "Necessary" shifts from "what you'd die without" to "what I'm used to doing"
  • H8 Cow methane is biogenic; it doesn't count: "Doesn't warm further" used as "doesn't warm"
  • I1 Animal testing has saved millions of lives: "Necessary" slides from the trivial sense to "the only means", and "the only means" is treated as "justified"
  • I5 Medical and veterinary students need to practice on animals: "Necessary" slides between "useful" and "indispensable"
  • I12 Bees make extra honey: Confuses honeybees with bees in general
  • I18 Wildlife sanctuaries protect animals: "Sanctuary" is used in two senses: the shelter and the brand
  • J14 Vegans are against farmers: "Farming" means now the whole sector, now animal farming
  • J19 Veganism has become big business: Calls the plant-based product market "veganism"
  • J24 A party with only vegan food excludes people who eat meat: "Excluding" used to mean "not offering the favorite dish"
  • K9 Radical veganism pushes people away: Uses "radical" as if it meant "extremist"
  • L9 Hitler was a vegetarian: Slides from "vegetarian" to "vegan", which aren't the same thing
  • L16 Vegans are speciesists too: Calls any preference or difference in treatment speciesism
  • L18 If vegans eat fake meat, they want meat: Reads culinary names as claims of origin
  • L33 Animal advocates want to get rid of pets: "Getting rid of pets" shifts between ending the trade and ending companionship
  • M2 I was vegan for my health, and I changed my mind: Uses "vegan" for a diet, and draws a conclusion about an ethical position

In helping wild animals

  • N1 Wild animals live free and happy: Uses "free" as if it meant "well"
  • N3 A balanced nature is good for animals: Uses "balance" as if it meant "wellbeing"
  • N4 Nature is fair, every animal has weapons to escape: Treats "a balanced contest" as "a good situation"
  • N5 Helping the weak ruins natural selection: Treats "fitter" as if it meant "happier" or "better"
  • N8 Who are we to interfere with nature?: Uses "freedom" as if it meant "wellbeing"
  • N11 Saving a wild animal only prolongs its agony: Treats "prolonging life" as if it were "prolonging suffering"
  • N15 Sentientists are against preserving species and biodiversity: Uses "has no value in itself" as if it meant "has no value"
  • N16 Sentientists want to get rid of forests: Treats "not expanding natural areas" as if it were "destroying existing ones"
  • N20 Sentientists give the same weight to insects, invertebrates and mammals: Treats "equal consideration" as "equal weight"

In the long term and with new minds

  • N22 Those not yet born don't matter: Confuses "doesn't exist now" with "won't be affected"

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V11 Destroying property isn't violence: It stretches the concept. We commonly call someone violent who destroys something in a rage, even without hurting anyone: smashing equipment with a sledgehammer looks violent to any observer.
  • V12 We need to give animals rights: It mixes two senses of "right". Legal rights are created by laws and courts, and those can indeed be given.
  • V18 In India almost everyone is vegan: It confuses vegetarianism with veganism. About 39% of Indian adults say they are vegetarian, and milk consumption in the country is very high.
  • V43 Broccoli and spinach have more protein than meat: The confusion comes from charts that count protein per calorie, not per weight.
  • V60 Cultivated meat isn't vegan, because it comes from animals: It confuses the letter of veganism with its spirit: it isn't a list of banned ingredients but a commitment not to cause harm.
