Begging the question

The conclusion is hidden in the premise

What it is

Using as a premise exactly what one wanted to prove. The argument seems to move forward but goes in a circle: the conclusion was already hidden in the starting point.

An analogy: "This newspaper is reliable because it says so itself."

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this error appears in 13 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A8 They're just animals: Species matters because it matters
  • A9 Humans are superior, so we can use the inferior: The yardstick of superiority is chosen from human traits
  • A13 I respect animals, but humans come first: Assumes the higher status it should prove
  • A14 Humans have intrinsic value; animals have instrumental value: Defines value by the trait one wants to use to exclude
  • A17 Animals don't have souls: The soul is attributed exactly to the members of the species one wants to protect
  • A18 It's normal for humans to be rational: The relevant norm is chosen after the desired result is known
  • A21 No duties, no rights: The "moral community" is defined so as to include exactly those one wants to include
  • A25 Treating animals well is a matter of compassion, not justice: Defines justice so as to exclude those under discussion
  • A27 Human life can't be compared with an animal's life: Assumes the uniqueness of human life to conclude it can't be compared
  • C10 Farm animals exist for this: Defines the animal by the use one wants to justify
  • E1 It's my personal choice; don't impose your morals: Calls a choice personal that is only personal if the animal doesn't count

In helping wild animals

  • N7 Domestic animals are our responsibility, wild ones are not: Assumes the relationship is a condition of the duty, which is what it needs to show

In the long term and with new minds

  • N21 Machines will never feel; robots and AIs need no rights: Assumes only the biological can feel, which is what's under discussion

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V26 Animals have a right to life, period: Without a reason, the sentence begs the question: it says killing animals is wrong because they have a right not to be killed, which only restates the conclusion in other words.
