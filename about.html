About the guide

A guide for people who want to talk better and think more clearly about animals, veganism and animal ethics. It systematically catalogs every idea used to exploit, harm or kill sentient beings, or to avoid helping them, and takes seriously, explaining in depth the objections and biases of those who oppose antispeciesism.

The guide

The project

Every objection deserves to be taken seriously and discussed honestly.

The guide was launched in November 2026 by the activist Julio Cesar Prava. Its aim is to map every argument used to justify animal exploitation and speciesism, and to help vegans and non-vegans improve the debate: to know the strongest version of each objection, where it fails, what it gets right and where the conversation usually goes next.

What the guide contains

More than 290 arguments are catalogued, each with its common wordings and its main flaw, and every one has a full chapter: the reply, the step-by-step rebuttal, the errors and biases, the consistency tests and the connections to other arguments. The guide also includes 80 weak arguments vegans should drop and a section on what goes beyond veganism.

How to use the site

  1. Replying to comments online. The simplest use: search for the sentence you read, copy the reply or make an image of it and post it in the thread, with the link to the chapter for anyone who wants to read more.
  2. Fact-checking in debates about veganism and animals. Each chapter separates what the argument gets right from what it gets wrong and points to the sources, which helps you check a claim before repeating or challenging it.
  3. Training AI systems not to be speciesist. The arguments, the answers and the flaw in each one can be used to build datasets and evaluation tests for language models. For this, the site offers plain-text versions: llms.txt, the index, and llms-full.txt, with every argument and its flaw.
  4. Activism and real-time debates. In street conversations, at events or in live debates, the guide works as a quick reference, including through AI assistants on wearable devices, such as glasses and earbuds, that can look up the answer during the conversation.

Method

The approach

The guide follows the tradition of analytic philosophy and, in epistemology, contemporary analytic epistemology, fallibilist and naturalistically oriented: it seeks clear concepts, valid arguments and premises backed by evidence; it treats knowledge as revisable; it tries to match its confidence in each belief to the strength of the evidence; and, on questions of fact, such as sentience, health or the environment, it relies on the sciences and on the consensus of those who study them. The naturalism here is moderate: philosophy works in continuity with the sciences without giving up the task of assessing what counts as a good reason, as logic and ethics do. The ethics guiding it is based on sentience, sentientism: whoever can have good or bad experiences matters morally, whatever their species. The guide also shows that ideas like reason and justice should not be bent to serve one's own interest. And fallibilism is also one of the guide's values: any answer here may be wrong or incomplete, which is why the sources are in plain view, objections are presented in their strongest version and corrections are welcome.

Health, environmental and economic arguments appear as corrections of fact, not as moral justification: the guide follows the criticism, voiced by many activists, of indirect arguments that steer away from respect for animals (learn why).

About the sources

Empirical claims need a source. The links in the references lead to the sources checked while writing. When a table says "source not yet checked", the reference exists but has not yet been verified against the original.

This English edition was translated from the Portuguese original and adapted for an international reader: where an example was specifically Brazilian, an international equivalent with a checked source was used, and Brazilian facts were kept where they are the evidence itself. Sources available only in Portuguese are marked.

About the tone

The guide discusses arguments, not people. It presents the strongest version of each objection and says what it gets right, because only then is the answer worth anything. When it compares the logical structure of arguments, it is not equating victims or historical suffering.

The author

Julio Cesar Prava is Brazilian, lives in Australia and is a graphic designer and animal advocate. He stopped eating meat in 2009, when he became a lacto-ovo vegetarian, and became vegan in 2017. In 2021, he came to consider himself a sentientist.

In 2017, he founded Vegpedia, to map the vegan movement in Brazil and around the world. In 2018, he created a Portuguese-language website to promote Dominion, a documentary about animal exploitation, and in 2020 he launched Veganismos, a digital magazine about veganism. He took part in the Ethicast podcast and, occasionally, in street outreach with groups such as Anonymous for the Voiceless and VEDDAS.

He has taken courses in animal ethics at UFMG and UFU, in partnership with Animal Ethics, and at the Center for Effective Vegan Advocacy, as well as a course on alternative proteins at the GFI. He is a consequentialist and appreciates science and philosophy communication and evidence-based practice. He values intellectual honesty and intellectual and moral openness, so that we can improve our thinking and our choices and help defend sentient beings. In 2026, he launched the Guia de Argumentação Pelos Animais, published in English as For the Animals.
