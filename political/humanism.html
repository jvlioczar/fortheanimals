"I'm a humanist: human dignity comes first, and putting animals on the same level demeans humans."

Humanism and anthropocentrism: the principle

Value human dignity, reason and well-being, and base ethics on reason, empathy and evidence, not on dogma.

Short answer

Considering animals takes nothing away from human dignity: no one loses rights because a pig is no longer confined. Humanism rests on reason, empathy and evidence, and the evidence says many animals feel pain and fear. Not by chance, Peter Singer, author of Animal Liberation, was named Australian Humanist of the Year in 2004.

Ask a question back

Which human right is lost when an animal is no longer bred and killed to become food?

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • Considering animals takes nothing away from human dignity: no one loses rights because a pig is no longer confined. Human dignity never depended on despising those who are not human (A9, A13).
  • Humanism rests on reason, empathy and evidence. The evidence says that many animals feel pain and fear (A1), empathy does not stop at the species line, and reason asks that similar cases be treated alike.
  • Much of the modern critique of speciesism was born within secular humanism: Peter Singer, author of Animal Liberation, was named Australian Humanist of the Year in 2004.

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version says that only humans are moral agents, capable of responsibility, and that ethics is therefore, by definition, a relationship among humans.

The answer

Being a moral agent defines who has duties, not who can be harmed. Babies are not moral agents, and they are not left out of ethics for that. What humanism values in humans, such as the capacity to suffer and to have a life that goes well or badly, is exactly what many animals also have (A14, A22).

Who defends animals from within this theory

Authors, activists and groups who start from this theory and show why it has to include animals and other sentient beings.

  • Peter Singer: Australian philosopher; author of Animal Liberation (1975), named Australian Humanist of the Year in 2004
