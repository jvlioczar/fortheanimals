"Exploitation is a relation between human classes. Animals aren't workers, and the struggle is a different one."

Marxism, socialism and communism: the principle

End exploitation and the turning of beings and lives into commodities.

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • Billions of animals are bred, used and sold as commodities, on a scale no other exploitation reaches. The critique of commodification applies to them directly.
  • Whether "labor" applies to animals is a real debate: Marx himself distinguished human labor from animal activity. But authors such as Ted Benton and Kendra Coulter show that cows, horses and working dogs perform productive functions without choice or compensation.
  • Even those who reject calling it labor must explain why the exploitation of a sentient being would be acceptable just because it is not human.

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version says the priority must be the human working class, and that the animal cause diverts energy from the revolution.

The answer

Ending the exploitation of animals does not compete with the workers' struggle, and industrial animal farming also exploits the humans who work in it (J3). And "first one cause, then the other" has been used against almost every struggle that came after (J6, K4).
