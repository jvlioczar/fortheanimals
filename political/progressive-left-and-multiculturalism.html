"The animal cause is a white middle-class concern that ends up attacking the cultural practices of minorities."

The progressive left and multiculturalism: the principle

Defend vulnerable groups, fight domination and respect cultural diversity.

Short answer

The criticism of selectivity is fair: campaigns have targeted minority practices and ignored the far more numerous practices of the majority. But that is an argument against selectivity, not against animals. Almost all animal deaths happen in exploitation carried out by majorities and large companies, such as industrial fishing and farming. And defending animals does not require denying anything to any human.

Ask a question back

If the problem is targeting only minority practices, wouldn't the answer be to start with the majority's industrial farming?

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • Will Kymlicka and Sue Donaldson show that the animal cause is almost invisible on the left, despite its affinities with feminism, social justice and disability rights, and that this exclusion does not follow from the left's own principles.
  • Part of the resistance comes from fear that talking about animal rights lowers the human dignity of groups that have already been dehumanized. But defending animals does not require denying anything to any human.
  • Animals are today, by far, the group with the most individuals deliberately killed by humans. And almost all of those deaths happen in exploitation carried out by majorities and large companies, such as industrial fishing, industrial animal farming and insect farming, not in the practices of human minorities.

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version points to a real problem: animal campaigns have targeted minority practices, such as ritual slaughter or hunting by Indigenous peoples, while ignoring far more numerous majority practices.

The answer

The criticism is fair and should be taken seriously: the standard has to be the same for everyone, and starting with the most widespread exploitation is the most consistent course. That is an argument against selectivity, not against animals. A respect for diversity that did not count animals would repeat the very cut it fights (D6, J16).

Who defends animals from within this theory

Authors, activists and groups who start from this theory and show why it has to include animals and other sentient beings.

  • Will Kymlicka and Sue Donaldson: Canadian philosophers; Kymlicka is one of the main theorists of multiculturalism and argues, with Donaldson, that the left should include animals
  • Claire Jean Kim: American political scientist; in Dangerous Crossings (2015), she analyzes conflicts between the defense of animals and of minorities and proposes taking both seriously at once
  • Maneesha Deckha: Canadian legal scholar; in Animals as Legal Beings (2021), she starts from feminism and postcolonial thought to argue for a new legal status for animals
