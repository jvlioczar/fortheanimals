Libertarianism and anarcho-capitalism: the principle

Self-ownership and the non-aggression principle: no one may initiate violence against another individual.

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • Self-ownership rests on being a subject, someone whose life can go well or badly. Sentient animals are subjects in this sense too.
  • The non-aggression principle forbids initiating violence against someone who threatened no one. Breeding, mutilating and killing an animal is initiating violence.
  • Saying the animal is property is the conclusion in dispute, not a premise. Beings who can be victims do not become property without an argument, and that argument is missing (A17).

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version says rights come from agreements between agents able to respect non-aggression, and that animals, who cannot respect it, are outside the agreement.

The answer

Babies cannot respect non-aggression either, and no libertarian treats them as property. The capacity to be aggressed against, not the capacity to sign an agreement, is what makes aggression wrong (A7, A23).
