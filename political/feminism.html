"Feminism is about women; talking about cows and hens is changing the subject, or even offensive."

Feminism: the principle

End oppression linked to sex and control over the body and reproduction.

Short answer

Milk and egg production is based on controlling the reproduction of females: insemination with no possibility of refusal, offspring taken away, slaughter when production falls. Autonomy over one's own body is at the heart of feminism, and it is exactly what these females lack. This does not equate women and cows: it points to a shared structure of control over bodies that feel.

Ask a question back

If controlling someone's body and reproduction is oppression, why does it stop being so when the body is a cow's?

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • Milk and egg production is based on controlling the reproduction of females: repeated insemination, separation from their young and disposal when production drops (B5, C11).
  • Bodily autonomy is at the heart of feminism, and it is exactly what exploited females lack. Cows, sows, ewes, goats, mares and turkey hens are restrained and forcibly inseminated, with instruments or a person's arm inserted into their bodies, or are made to mate when the farmer decides. None of them can consent or refuse.
  • This repeats in cycles: pregnancy, birth, removal of the young, new insemination. When her body no longer gets pregnant or produces less, the female is sent to slaughter and becomes meat. And her young are exploited too: the females repeat their mother's cycle, and the males are killed early or raised for slaughter.
  • This observation does not equate women with cows, nor victims with each other. It points to a shared structure of control over bodies that feel (L21).
  • Part of feminism itself made this link, as Carol J. Adams did in The Sexual Politics of Meat, although not every current accepts it.

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version says comparisons between women and animals have historically served to dehumanize women.

The answer

Care with such comparisons is legitimate, and the argument does not depend on them (L21). The principle is enough: controlling the body and reproduction of someone who feels, for others' profit, is what feminism fights.

On the comparison with sexual violence

Some feminists who defend animals describe forced insemination as analogous to rape: it is the penetration of a female's body, with no possible consent, to control her reproduction for the benefit of others. The comparison is between the structures of the two acts, not between the victims, and it does not say the experiences are the same.

Many people are offended by this comparison, and it is worth asking why. Unease about the history of women being demeaned through comparisons with animals is legitimate and deserves care. But often the offense comes from another premise: that a cow is so inferior that nothing done to her body can be taken seriously. That premise is speciesism. It does not change the facts: cows are females, they feel, and they are harmed by forced insemination, by having their young taken and by slaughter.

Anyone who prefers not to use the word does not need to. Describing the facts is enough, and the principle is the same: no one who feels should have their body and reproduction controlled by force for the profit of others.

Who defends animals from within this theory

Authors, activists and groups who start from this theory and show why it has to include animals and other sentient beings.

  • Carol J. Adams: American writer; in The Sexual Politics of Meat (1990), she shows how the oppression of women and of animals reinforce each other
  • Josephine Donovan: American scholar; author of "Animal Rights and Feminist Theory" (1990)
  • Lori Gruen: American philosopher; with Carol J. Adams, she edited Ecofeminism: Feminist Intersections with Other Animals and the Earth (2014)
  • Greta Gaard: American ecofeminist scholar; she edited Ecofeminism: Women, Animals, Nature (1993)
  • Pattrice Jones: American activist and writer, cofounder of VINE Sanctuary; she writes on feminism, LGBTQ+ people and animal liberation
  • Frances Power Cobbe: Irish suffragist (1822–1904); she founded the first organizations against vivisection
