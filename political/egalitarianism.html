Home / Political and ethical objections / Egalitarianism and prioritarianism

"Equality is among human citizens; animals belong to another category."

Egalitarianism and prioritarianism: the principle

Reduce unjust inequalities and give priority to those who are worst off.

Short answer

If priority goes to those who are worse off, animals in intensive farming and many wild animals are among the worst-off beings there are. Egalitarianism fights precisely the disadvantages nobody chose, and nobody chooses the species they are born into. Excluding animals is deciding in advance who can be worse off, which contradicts the principle itself.

Ask a question back

If priority goes to the worst off, why can't the worst off be an animal?

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • If priority goes to the worst off, animals in intensive farming and many wild animals are among the worst-off beings there are.
  • That inequality between species is natural does not make it just: egalitarianism fights precisely the disadvantages no one chose.
  • Excluding animals is deciding in advance who can be worst off, which contradicts the principle.

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version says the relevant equality is equality of political status, which only makes sense among members of a society.

The answer

Even if political status is discussed separately, priority to the worst off refers to well-being, and an animal's well-being can go badly. Among theories that disagree on almost everything, this is a point of convergence.

Who defends animals from within this theory

Authors, activists and groups who start from this theory and show why it has to include animals and other sentient beings.

  • Peter Vallentyne: philosopher; in the article "Of Mice and Men: Equality and Animals" (2005), he discusses what egalitarianism implies once animals are counted
  • Nils Holtug: Danish philosopher; author of "Equality for Animals" (2007)
  • Oscar Horta: Spanish philosopher; in "Egalitarianism and Animals" (2016), he argues that egalitarianism gives priority to animals, because they are worse off
  • Catia Faria: Portuguese philosopher; she argues that equality and priority to the worst off also apply to wild animals
  • Luciano Carlos Cunha: Brazilian philosopher, with a PhD in ethics and political philosophy and coordinator of Animal Ethics in Brazil; he defends equal consideration of the interests of all sentient beings and has devoted works to the suffering of wild animals, who are often the worst off
