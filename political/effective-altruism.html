"Resources for doing good are limited, and should go to humans first."

Effective altruism: the principle

Use evidence and reason to do the most good possible with the resources available.

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • Effective altruism's criteria, scale, neglectedness and tractability, favor the animal cause: the number of victims is enormous and the resources devoted to it are small.
  • Excluding animals in advance is deciding the result before doing the math, the opposite of what the approach proposes.
  • Not eating animals takes no resources from any other cause (J6).

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version says it is hard to compare well-being across species, and that uncertainty favors the better-known human causes.

The answer

The uncertainty is real, but it cuts both ways, and the scale is so large that the animal cause stays a priority even with heavy discounts on the weight of each animal.
