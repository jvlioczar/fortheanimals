"Human pleasures are worth more: better to be a human dissatisfied than a pig satisfied."

Utilitarianism: the principle

Maximize well-being and minimize suffering for everyone affected, counting each one's interest equally.

Why excluding animals contradicts the principle

  • Bentham, one of the founders of utilitarianism, already said the question is not whether animals can reason, but whether they can suffer. Excluding them contradicts the theory itself.
  • Mill's line compares kinds of pleasure, but it does not zero out animal suffering. Even if intellectual pleasures were worth more, an animal's pain would still count (A16, A22).
  • Scale changes the sum: tens of billions of land animals and trillions of fish every year.

The strongest counter-reply

The most serious version grants that animals count, but says that farming with a good life and a painless death could be justified in the calculation, because it brings happy animals into existence (B6).

The answer

That is a real debate within utilitarianism, but it does not describe actual animal farming, in which most animals live in intensive systems. And the idea that one animal can be replaced by another faces serious objections (B6, A16).
