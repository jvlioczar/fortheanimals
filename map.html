Map of connections

Whoever loses an indirect argument usually retreats to a direct one, and vice versa. Knowing the chain helps anticipate the next objection.

The three roots

Almost every chain of objections, once exhausted, ends in one of these three. The rest work as protective layers: answer the layer, but aim at the root.

A10

They're just animals

Explicit speciesism: species membership as a moral criterion, like race in racism

Full chapterJ11

Meat tastes good

Taste as a justification for killing: no other context accepts this

Full chapterE12

I know it's wrong, but I won't change

Admitted weakness of will: concedes the moral point and refuses its consequence

Natural, normal, necessary and nice

The families fit the "4Ns" of Piazza and colleagues (2015), the four most common rationalizations for eating meat: natural (family C), normal (D and E), necessary (G, H and J) and nice (J11).
