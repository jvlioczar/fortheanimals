E. Relativism, freedom and evasions

13 arguments in Part DIR.

Underlying question: Is it just a matter of choice?
Dominant error: Epistemological and metaethical

E1

It's my personal choice; don't impose your morals.

Personal choice ends where there is a victim; every rule against harm "imposes"

Full chapterE2

Morality is relative.

Self-refuting and applied selectively; it confuses taste with a claim about harm to others

Full chapterE4

The state can't ban what I eat.

Switches from "is it wrong?" to "should it be banned?"

Full chapterE5

Everything in moderation.

False middle ground: moderation doesn't make an unjustifiable harm acceptable

Full chapterE6

Nobody's perfect; I do what I can.

True in general, but used to avoid doing what is easy

Full chapterE7

I don't want to see those videos.

Willful ignorance: avoiding the information to avoid the obligation

Full chapterE8

I love animals, but...

A contradiction between stated values and practice (the meat paradox)

Full chapterE9

Eating meat isn't an ideology; veganism is.

The dominant norm is invisible to those who follow it (carnism, Joy 2009)

Full chapterE10

Veganism is opinion, not science.

No moral position is "just science", including eating meat

Full chapterE11

Even philosophers don't agree.

Disagreement isn't a tie; there is strong convergence against factory farming

Full chapterE12

I know it's wrong, but I won't change.

Admitted weakness of will: concedes the moral point and refuses its consequence

Full chapterE13

Defending animals is just utilitarianism

Equal consideration is a formal principle accepted by rival theories; knocking down utilitarianism doesn't rescue speciesism

Full chapterE14

He eats meat, but he's a good person

Confuses judging the person with judging the act: good people also take part in injustices that custom accepts
