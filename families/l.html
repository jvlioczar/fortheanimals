L. Attacks on veganism and vegans

32 arguments in Part IND.

Underlying question: Can the people saying this be trusted?
Dominant error: Logical (ad hominem, tu quoque)

Family of indirect argumentsThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about who defends veganism, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the question depended on who defends animals, vegans being pleasant and consistent would make the argument valid, and being annoying and inconsistent would make it invalid. But the animals suffer just the same, whoever the messenger is.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

Arguments in this family

L1

Vegans are annoying and think they are better

Ad hominem: the messenger's behavior doesn't change whether the message is valid

Full chapterL2

Vegans are all hippies

A stereotype and an attack on the person: animals' suffering doesn't depend on the lifestyle of whoever points it out

Full chapterL3

Vegans are crazy; they have mental problems

An attack on the person: the asker's health doesn't change the answer, the link with depression doesn't show cause, and using mental disorders as insults reinforces stigma

Full chapterL4

Sentientists are even crazier than vegans

Attack on the person and appeal to ridicule: strangeness is not error, and the fair test is to apply the same scenario to humans and examine the premise

Full chapterL5

Vegan activists are radicals and extremists

Judging a cause by the methods of a minority

Full chapterL6

Meat is for real men

A gender stereotype; masculinity is neither a moral nor a nutritional criterion

Full chapterL7

Veganism is a cult, a religion

A label used to avoid evaluating; veganism rests on public arguments

Full chapterL8

Veganism is moral puritanism

A label in place of a rebuttal: veganism is about harm to third parties, not the purity of the eater, and every morality goes against natural impulses

Full chapterL9

Hitler was a vegetarian

Reductio ad Hitlerum and guilt by association; historically doubtful

Full chapterL10

Raising vegan children is imposing an ideology

Every family passes values on to its children, and serving meat is also the parents' choice; what matters is caring for the child's health and explaining the reasons

Full chapterL11

You use a phone, so you're a hypocrite

Tu quoque: someone's inconsistency doesn't make what they say false

Full chapterL12

Veganism isn't 100% cruelty-free

Nirvana fallacy: reducing harm doesn't require eliminating it entirely

Full chapterL13

Vegans shouldn't use artificial intelligence

Veganism doesn't promise zero impact, only not to exploit animals; AI's cost is real, but diet weighs far more, and 'it isn't natural' settles nothing

Full chapterL14

Vegans shouldn't watch violent films or play violent games

Depicting violence isn't committing it, and fiction has no victims; when an animal was really harmed on set, the criticism is fair and avoiding the work is consistent

Full chapterL15

Vegans are speciesists too

Tu quoque: having a bias doesn't license harming anyone, it calls for correction; and speciesism is the unjustified disregard of interests, not any preference

Full chapterL16

You don't feel empathy for every animal in the world either

The question isn't empathy: it is limited and biased, and where it fails we use reason and principles, as we do with distant humans

Full chapterL17

If vegans eat fake meat, they want meat

The problem was never the taste; it was the victim

Full chapterL18

There are sexist, racist and transphobic vegans

Tu quoque and an attack on the messenger: a vegan's failing in another cause doesn't change what happens to animals, and the same reasoning would bring down any cause

Full chapterL19

Vegans defend animals, but they support abortion

Tu quoque and generalization: veganism has no position on abortion, the two cases have different structures, and someone's inconsistency wouldn't justify exploiting animals

Full chapterL20

Vegans care more about animals than people

False dilemma and generalization; empathy is not a zero-sum resource

Full chapterL21

Comparing animal farming to slavery or the Holocaust is offensive

Distinguish structural analogy from moral equivalence; handle with care and sources

Full chapterL22

Vegans are racist, because they criticize Afro-Brazilian religions

Generalizes the selective criticism of some: the consistent criterion criticizes every avoidable killing of animals, starts with the industry and doesn't pick a people as its target

Full chapterL23

Vegans are against religion

False generalization: there are vegans in every religion, whole traditions teach not harming animals, and veganism is compatible with faith and with the absence of faith

Full chapterL24

Veganism is just a fad diet

Confuses an ethical position about not harming animals with a menu chosen for health or looks

Full chapterL25

Vegans support pseudoscience

Some vegans spread pseudoscience, but the case for animals rests on sentience and the nutritional consensus; judging an idea by its worst advocates would bring down any cause

Full chapterL26

Vegan documentaries are manipulated

Generalizes from some films' errors to everything: the footage shows standard, legal practices, and the moral conclusion doesn't depend on any film

Full chapterL27

Most vegans give up

Difficulty sticking to it says nothing about whether it is right

Full chapterL28

Vegans treat vegetarians as enemies

An attack on the messenger: some vegans' hostility is a mistake, but the criticism is of milk and egg production, not of people

Full chapterL29

Vegans think they're the only ones who really defend animals

Generalizes from some vegans and confuses criticizing an inconsistency with despising people; those who rescue animals already accept that animals matter

Full chapterL30

I'll eat double to make up for you

An emotional reaction (reactance), not an argument

Full chapterL31

My food beats yours

A taunt, not an argument: the pleasure of taste doesn't weigh against someone's life, and much vegan food is the same food as always

Full chapterL32

Look what you're missing

Showing the plate doesn't answer the question about who became the plate; temptation doesn't make the act just
