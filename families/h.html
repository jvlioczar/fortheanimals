H. Environment, ecology and agriculture

16 arguments in Part IND.

Underlying question: Is veganism worse for the planet?
Dominant error: Empirical (selective data)

Family of indirect arguments

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the environment and agriculture, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on environmental grounds, low-impact exploitation would be acceptable, even if it killed more sentient beings. And it is already being promoted: insect farming, "sustainable" hunting, "sustainable" leather. It is possible to reduce environmental impact and increase exploitation at the same time.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

H1

Vegans' soy is destroying the Amazon

Most soy becomes animal feed; pasture is the main driver of deforestation

Full chapterH2

Harvests kill animals; vegans kill too

Animal farming uses far more cropland (for feed) and land

Full chapterH3

Regenerative grazing sequesters carbon

Limited, temporary sequestration that cannot scale; it needs more land per calorie

Full chapterH4

Cattle graze where crops can't grow

Partly true, but that land could be rewilded and produces few calories

Full chapterH5

Cow methane is biogenic; it doesn't count

Methane has a strong short-term effect; growing herds increase warming

Full chapterH6

Avocados and almonds use a lot of water

A selective comparison; per nutrient, animal products lead in water and land use

Full chapterH7

Imported vegan food pollutes in transport

Transport is a small share of emissions; what you eat matters more than how far it traveled

Full chapterH8

Local, organic meat is sustainable

Grass-fed beef usually needs more land and emits as much or more per kilo

Full chapterH9

Agriculture can't work without manure

Green manures and compost exist; the manure comes from crops grown for feed

Full chapterH10

Sustainable fishing and aquaculture solve it

Bycatch, overfishing and feed made from wild fish; the suffering continues

Full chapterH11

Hunting controls populations

It often causes the imbalance it claims to fix; non-lethal methods exist

Full chapterH12

Livestock pollutes little; the problem is fossil fuels

False dichotomy; food systems account for about a third of emissions

Full chapterH13

If meat is sustainable, there's no problem

Reduces a moral question about victims to an environmental one

Full chapterH14

Eating insects is the sustainable protein of the future

Trades environmental impact for trillions of probable victims; plant proteins are just as efficient, with no one to suffer

Full chapterH15

What matters is how food is grown, not whether it comes from animals

False dichotomy: you can choose plants and care about how they are grown; the type of food weighs more than the production method, and none of it answers what happens to the animal

Full chapterH16

Plant monoculture destroys as much as industrial animal farming

Most soy and much of the grain feed farmed animals; eating plants reduces agricultural land, and comparing the worst cropland with the best animal farming is unfair
