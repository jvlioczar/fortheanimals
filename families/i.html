I. Other uses: testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companionship

32 arguments in Part IND.

Underlying question: And beyond the plate?
Dominant error: Empirical and moral

Family of indirect argumentsThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about the usefulness of testing, clothing, entertainment, work and companion animals, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If the defense of animals depended on showing that testing, clothing or entertainment with animals are useless, one useful use would be enough to justify them. But usefulness does not settle the question: no benefit justifies using people who did not consent, and the same holds for animals.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

Arguments in this family

I1

Animal testing has saved millions of lives

A benefit doesn't justify using those who can't consent; translational failure rates are high

Full chapterI2

If you take medicine, you can't oppose testing

Tu quoque; testing is required by law, not chosen by the patient

Full chapterI3

Cosmetics tested on animals are safer

Cosmetics aren't medicine; the EU has banned it since 2013 and Brazil restricts it since 2023, and safety is ensured with known ingredients and animal-free methods

Full chapterI4

Medical and veterinary students need to practice on animals

Systematic reviews show that harm-free methods teach as well or better; living tissue can be practiced on without killing healthy animals

Full chapterI5

Pig organs will save thousands of lives

The organ shortage is real, but there are paths that do not breed animals for their organs: human donation, artificial and grown organs

Full chapterI6

Traditional medicine has used animal parts for millennia

Being old does not prove efficacy; bears are caged for years for bile, and the useful compound is already synthesized

Full chapterI7

Leather is just a byproduct of meat

Profitable byproducts subsidize slaughter

Full chapterI8

A fur coat is natural and comes from regulated farms

The animals live in wire cages, fur has a high environmental impact, and several countries have banned the farms

Full chapterI9

Sheep need to be shorn

Only because they were bred for excessive wool; mulesing, and slaughter when production ends

Full chapterI10

Silk and down are natural and don't harm animals

In conventional silk the silkworm is boiled in its cocoon, and plucking feathers from live birds causes pain, according to EFSA

Full chapterI11

Bees make extra honey

Honey is the bees' food reserve; honeybees compete with native pollinators

Full chapterI12

Zoos conserve species and educate

Few of the species kept are endangered or reintroduced

Full chapterI13

Aquariums and dolphin parks are harmless

Severe captivity for wide-ranging animals with complex social lives

Full chapterI14

Wildlife sanctuaries protect animals

The name is not regulated: many "sanctuaries" are zoos that breed, sell and display animals; real ones rescue, don't breed and don't let the public handle them

Full chapterI15

Safaris and animal tours are harmless tourism

Watching free animals from a distance is not the same as riding, holding or swimming with captive ones; in an Oxford study, 18 of 24 types of attraction harmed welfare

Full chapterI16

Rodeos, bullfights and circuses are culture

Appeal to tradition (D1) applied to suffering imposed for entertainment

Full chapterI17

A rodeo animal is a well-treated athlete and only bucks for a few seconds

The animal bucks because the flank strap and spurs provoke it, doesn't choose to compete, and the rodeo law itself recognizes the risk of cruelty

Full chapterI18

The fighting bull lives free in the field and dies fighting

A good life doesn't justify a slow death as a spectacle, the fight isn't fair because the bull is weakened first, and preserving the breed benefits no bull

Full chapterI19

Circus animals are well treated and part of the family

A bond isn't welfare; traveling life doesn't meet these animals' needs, and more than 40 countries restrict it

Full chapterI20

Animals in films, ads and videos are trained, well-cared-for actors

The credit has documented failures, videos of exotic animals encourage the trade, and digital effects replace the use

Full chapterI21

Horses like working and being ridden

A convenient projection by those who benefit

Full chapterI22

Racehorses and sporting dogs live better than many people

The care lasts while the animal earns, racing kills and discards, and human poverty is an injustice to fix, not a yardstick for using animals

Full chapterI23

In sport fishing the fish is released

Injury, stress and post-release mortality; pain as a pastime

Full chapterI24

I fish what I eat; it's more honest than buying at the store

Being honest about a harm doesn't make it necessary; a fish out of water dies by asphyxia, with minutes of pain

Full chapterI25

Trophy hunting funds conservation

Little money reaches conservation; it uses individuals as tools

Full chapterI26

The cart is the carter's livelihood, and the horse is well cared for

The worker's income is real; the way forward is to replace the horse with a vehicle, with support, not to keep the animal on the asphalt

Full chapterI27

Guide and assistance dogs are well cared for and do important work

It's a borderline case, discussed with nuance; a hard case doesn't make the easy ones acceptable, such as breeding for slaughter

Full chapterI28

Police and detection dogs are well cared for and save lives

The dog is put at risk without being able to choose, and the care depends on usefulness; a use that saves lives doesn't justify those that don't

Full chapterI29

Buying a pedigree dog or cat doesn't hurt anyone

Commercial breeding uses breeding females, shapes breeds with health problems and competes with shelter adoption

Full chapterI30

The bird was born in a cage; it wouldn't survive if released

That calls for caring well for the bird that exists, not buying or breeding others to live caged; the market feeds trafficking

Full chapterI31

Having a pet is exploitation too, and your cat eats meat

Adopting and caring differs from breeding to use; cats need nutrients, not meat; and none of this licenses eating animals

Full chapterI32

A shock collar is just a stimulus; it doesn't hurt the dog

It works because it's unpleasant, doesn't treat the cause of barking, and reward-based training works as well or better
