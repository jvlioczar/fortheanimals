A. Sentience and moral status

24 arguments in Part DIR.

Underlying question: Do animals count morally?
Dominant error: Moral (arbitrary criterion)

A1

Animals aren't rational.

Rationality isn't what makes suffering matter, and the criterion would exclude humans who lack it

Full chapterA2

Humans are superior, so we can use the inferior.

Superior abilities give no right to dominate

Full chapterA3

Animals don't have language.

An irrelevant criterion for pain; it excludes babies and non-verbal people

Full chapterA4

Animals don't have souls.

An unfalsifiable premise used as a public criterion

Full chapterA5

Animals aren't conscious and don't feel pain like we do.

Contradicts the neuroscientific evidence (Cambridge Declaration, 2012; New York Declaration, 2024)

Full chapterA6

Fish and invertebrates don't feel pain.

Growing evidence of sentience in fish, cephalopods and decapods

Full chapterA7

No duties, no rights.

Confuses moral agents with moral patients; it would exclude children

Full chapterA8

Animals aren't persons.

A persuasive definition: pick the concept that excludes them, then conclude they are excluded

Full chapterA9

Rights are a human invention, only for humans.

Genetic fallacy: where a norm came from doesn't set how far it reaches

Full chapterA10

They're just animals.

Explicit speciesism: species membership as a moral criterion, like race in racism

Full chapterA11

It's normal for humans to be rational.

Treats individuals by what others have, not by what they have

Full chapterA12

Human babies have the potential to be rational.

Potential doesn't confer the rights of the actual, and it doesn't cover humans without potential

Full chapterA13

It's natural to put our own first.

Partiality lets us prefer our own; it does not let us exploit others

Full chapterA14

Plants feel too.

No nervous system and no evidence of experience; and if they did feel, animal farming kills more plants

Full chapterA15

Where do you draw the line? Insects? Bacteria?

Continuum fallacy: a fuzzy boundary doesn't erase clear cases

Full chapterA16

Killing without pain isn't harm.

Death takes away good future experiences; the same holds for humans with no sense of time

Full chapterA17

Animals are property.

Describes a legal status; doesn't justify it (as with slavery)

Full chapterA18

What matters is the species, not the individual.

Species don't feel; individuals do

Full chapterA19

Vegans anthropomorphize animals.

Denying similar mental states is also an error (anthropodenial), and the evidence contradicts it

Full chapterA20

Humans have intrinsic value; animals have instrumental value.

Circular: it assumes the conclusion

Full chapterA21

I respect animals, but humans come first

A special status no one explains and that wouldn't pass the veil of ignorance: the weight should depend on the size of the harm, not on species

Full chapterA22

Humans suffer more than animals

Suffering more in some cases doesn't license ignoring those who suffer less; and less cognition often means more suffering and more vulnerability

Full chapterA23

Morality exists to protect us from each other

It would also exclude babies and vulnerable people; and explaining where a practice came from does not justify it

Full chapterA24

Species is biology, race is a social construct

A biological criterion can be as unjust as a social one: what makes discrimination unjust is giving someone's interests biased weight
