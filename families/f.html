F. Edge cases and hypotheticals

10 arguments in Part DIR.

Underlying question: What about the desert island?
Dominant error: Logical (hasty generalization from an exception)

Arguments in this family

F1

On a desert island, would you eat an animal?

Extreme necessity doesn't justify the practice in normal conditions

Full chapterF2

If a dog and a child were drowning, which would you save?

A preference in a tragic conflict doesn't license exploiting those who aren't in one

Full chapterF3

So should we stop lions from hunting?

Wild animal suffering is a serious question, and it does not favor eating animals

Full chapterF4

What about the insects you kill while driving?

Accidental, unavoidable harm differs from intentional, avoidable harm

Full chapterF6

What about the eggs from my rescued hen?

A rare, debatable case with no link to the industry; it doesn't legitimize buying

Full chapterF7

Oysters and mussels don't feel

Partly plausible; it confirms that sentience is the criterion

Full chapterF8

What about meat that gets thrown away?

Eating without creating demand is a different case; it still reinforces seeing animals as food

Full chapterF9

You also kill mosquitoes and rats at home

Self-defense and real conflict differ from breeding animals to eat

Full chapterF10

Would you eat an animal that died of natural causes?

No one was killed, so the moral problem is much smaller; but that meat never reaches the market, and refusing to eat a body can be respect, as we do with humans

Full chapterF11

What if they discover that plants feel?

Even in that scenario, eating plants would cause less harm, because animals eat far more plants; not being able to reach zero harm doesn't make everything equivalent
