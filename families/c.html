C. Nature, biology and evolution

11 arguments in Part DIR.

Underlying question: Is what is natural justified?
Dominant error: Logical (appeal to nature, Hume's law)

C1

It's natural to eat meat.

Appeal to nature: natural doesn't mean right

Full chapterC2

Lions eat other animals.

Lions aren't moral agents and have no alternative

Full chapterC3

We have canine teeth; we are omnivores.

Being able to eat it isn't needing to; anatomy creates no duty

Full chapterC4

We're at the top of the food chain.

Ecologically false (the average human trophic level is about 2.2) and morally irrelevant

Full chapterC5

Eating meat made us evolve.

An adaptive past doesn't dictate present morality

Full chapterC6

Might makes right.

Social Darwinism: it describes a process, not a norm

Full chapterC7

It's the circle of life.

A euphemism: breeding, confining and slaughtering is an industry, not a natural cycle

Full chapterC8

If we don't eat them, animals will take over the planet.

Gets the cause backwards: there are so many because they're bred

Full chapterC9

Farm animals exist for this.

An imposed purpose: the "function" is assigned by whoever profits

Full chapterC10

Cows need to be milked.

Only because they were made pregnant and bred for overproduction

Full chapterC11

We naturally crave meat.

A learned and reinforced preference; desire is no justification
