D. Tradition, culture and religion

14 arguments in Part DIR.

Underlying question: Does custom justify it?
Dominant error: Logical (appeal to tradition and authority)

Arguments in this family

D1

We've always done it

Appeal to tradition: the same phrase defended slavery and the subjugation of women

Full chapterD2

Everybody eats meat

Appeal to popularity: popular isn't right

Full chapterD3

Barbecues bring the family together

The bond is the gathering, not the animal

Full chapterD4

My grandparents ate meat and lived to 90

Anecdotal evidence and survivorship bias

Full chapterD5

I don't want to dishonor my parents and my ancestors

Confuses honoring people with repeating what they did: every generation revises earlier practices, and judging a practice is not condemning those who followed it

Full chapterD6

It's cultural; criticizing it is ethnocentric

Selective cultural relativism: not applied to practices the person condemns

Full chapterD7

Indigenous and traditional peoples eat animals

Uses those with no alternative to justify those who have one; veganism already says "as far as is possible and practicable"

Full chapterD8

Great thinkers ate meat

Appeal to authority; many also defended human hierarchies we now reject

Full chapterD9

It's legal; the law allows it

Legalistic fallacy: the law follows morality, with a delay

Full chapterD10

God gave us dominion over animals

Many traditions read dominion as stewardship; and it is a theological premise, not a public one

Full chapterD11

Jesus ate fish

Appeal to authority; a historical context of necessity doesn't carry over

Full chapterD12

My religion approves of eating animals

Permitting is not requiring, and religious traditions themselves forbid unnecessary suffering, which today's production causes on a massive scale

Full chapterD13

Animal sacrifice in religious rituals is freedom of worship

Freedom of worship answers whether the state can ban the rite, not whether killing the animal is fair; that question applies equally to the rite and the slaughterhouse, and criticizing only Afro-Brazilian religions is inconsistent

Full chapterD14

I'll pay for my mistakes in my next life

The phrase admits the wrong and postpones the bill, but the animal pays now; the traditions of karma teach non-violence
