B. Harm, death and welfare

9 arguments in Part DIR.

Underlying question: Does killing without suffering harm the individual?
Dominant error: Moral and empirical

Arguments in this family

B1

There is humane slaughter

A contradiction in terms: there is no compassionate way to kill someone who does not want to die

Full chapterB2

Free-range animals are happy

They're still killed young; labels have little to do with practice, and changing the scale doesn't change the act

Full chapterB4

The problem is the industry, not consumers

The industry exists to meet demand: system and consumer sustain each other

Full chapterB5

They had a good life and a quick death

A good life doesn't license taking it; the idea is absurd applied to dogs or humans

Full chapterB6

Without farming, these animals wouldn't even exist

Bringing someone into existence gives no right to kill them

Full chapterB7

Milk and eggs don't kill anyone

Male chicks and calves are discarded, and cows and hens are slaughtered when production drops

Full chapterB8

The law guarantees animal welfare

Laws allow confinement, mutilations without anesthesia and the killing of newborns

Full chapterB9

Those videos are exceptions or staged

Much of what they show is standard, legal industry practice

Full chapterB10

Stressed animals make bad meat, so farmers take good care of them

The incentive is small and partial; it doesn't cover suffering that doesn't affect profit
