G. Health and nutrition

20 arguments in Part IND.

Underlying question: Do we need animal products?
Dominant error: Empirical

Family of indirect argumentsThe answer to this argument is indirect. Tap to see why it isn't enough, and how to use it better.

This is an indirect argument, and the answer to it is indirect too. It is about health and nutrition, not about whether animals deserve respect. The underlying question in the defense of animals runs in parallel to this one: it is sentience, the capacity animals have to be harmed and benefited (see what supports animal ethics).

Many activists question using this kind of argument to defend animals, because it sidesteps what matters. Other movements are not defended this way: no one makes the case for anti-racism by saying it is good for the environment, for the health of white people or for the economy. The harm done to the victims is already the reason.

Why it is not good to use it, or why always to pair it with an ethical argument: If veganism were defended on health grounds, anyone who manages to be healthy eating animals, and many people do, would have no reason to change. And if an animal product turned out to be demonstrably healthier, the same argument would start defending exploitation. The eater's health says nothing about whether what is done to the animal is just.

Indirect arguments also do not lead to veganism on their own. Often they support, at most, vegetarianism, eating less or environmentalism, which accept the uses of animals seen as healthy, cheap or low-impact.

So discuss the facts as facts: correct what is wrong and acknowledge what is true. But do not treat the answer as a moral foundation, because what is does not, on its own, get you to what ought to be: even a real benefit would not show it is just to make someone suffer and die to obtain it. To really answer, show that the moral debate is a different one: speciesism and what happens to animals as individuals. Learn why

Arguments in this family

G1

Eating animals is necessary to survive

Necessary for whom? Where the alternative exists it is a choice, and the definition of veganism already speaks of doing what is possible and practicable; and the argument concedes the principle that undoes it, since without necessity there is no justification

We need animal protein

Protein needs are met by legumes, grains and soy

Full chapterG3

Plant protein is incomplete

A 1970s myth; variety over the day provides all the essential amino acids

Full chapterG4

If you need B12 supplements, it isn't natural

Farm animals also get supplemented B12; and natural is not the criterion (C1)

Full chapterG5

You'll lack iron, zinc and other micronutrients

Plannable with a varied diet; iron deficiency is also common in meat eaters

Full chapterG6

Milk is necessary for your bones

Calcium is found in plants and fortified foods

Full chapterG7

Only fish provides omega-3

Fish get their DHA from algae, a direct source available to us

Full chapterG8

Plants have antinutrients

Doses and preparation make them irrelevant; populations eating plant-based diets live longer

Full chapterG9

Soy causes female hormones and cancer

Phytoestrogens aren't estrogen; meta-analyses don't support the myth

Full chapterG10

Children and pregnant women can't be vegan

Major nutrition bodies consider it appropriate at every life stage, with planning

Full chapterG11

Athletes need meat

There are elite vegan athletes; adequate protein intake is what matters

Full chapterG12

Vegans are weak, pale and depressed

A stereotype; the existing correlations have uncertain or reverse causation

Full chapterG13

I know someone who got sick being vegan

Anecdotal evidence; it confuses a badly planned diet with a vegan diet

Full chapterG14

My body can't adapt

The blood type diet has been refuted; individual complaints are rarely investigated

Full chapterG15

My doctor told me to eat meat

An appeal to individual authority against institutional positions

Full chapterG16

A carnivore, paleo or keto diet is healthiest

Weak, short-term evidence that contradicts the large cohort studies

Full chapterG17

The WHO exaggerates; red meat isn't bad for you

Cherry picking against the consensus (IARC: processed meat Group 1, red meat Group 2A)

Full chapterG18

Ultra-processed vegan foods are worse than meat

False dichotomy: veganism doesn't require ultra-processed foods

Full chapterG19

Fruitarianism isn't healthy, so veganism isn't either

Veganism isn't fruitarianism: an all-fruit diet is risky, but a varied, planned vegan diet with B12 is considered adequate

Full chapterG20

Veganism is an eating disorder in disguise

The association exists, but the disorder usually comes before the diet; community vegans show no more signs of disorder, and the cause of a tragedy is the illness
