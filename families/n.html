N. Wild animals, new minds and the future

21 arguments in Part DIR.

Underlying question: Should we help, not just stop harming?
Dominant error: Moral (omission bias, appeal to nature)

Arguments in this family

N1

Wild animals live free and happy

The adults we see are the exception: in most species, almost all young die shortly after birth, from hunger, cold, disease or predation

Full chapterN2

Suffering and death are part of nature

Diseases are part of human life too, and that was never a reason not to treat them; the harm isn't smaller for the victim because no one is to blame

Full chapterN3

A balanced nature is good for animals

Balance measures numbers, not wellbeing; and environmental programmes kill thousands of animals for goals we would never accept for humans

Full chapterN4

We didn't cause it, so we have no duty to help

Omission bias: no one would accept this reasoning about a drowning child or flood victims

Full chapterN5

Who are we to interfere with nature?

We interfere all the time, with cities, farming and medicine; and no one calls helping an injured person arrogant

Full chapterN6

Meddling with nature is wrong; we could make everything worse

Risk calls for study and caution, as in medicine, not for omission: that is the proposal of welfare biology

Full chapterN7

It's impossible to solve suffering in nature; it's too big and complex

Not being able to solve everything is no reason to do nothing: we already vaccinate foxes, rescue animals in disasters, and can research to do more

Full chapterN8

Using medicine and genetics to end suffering is playing God

We already use anaesthesia, vaccines and painkillers without calling it playing God; the issue is safety and consent, not the natural origin of pain

Full chapterN9

Helping wild animals isn't veganism; it's a different cause

If sentience is what matters, suffering doesn't stop counting because we didn't cause it; and one cause doesn't stop us researching the other

Full chapterN10

Sentientists are against preserving species and biodiversity

Species and biodiversity don't feel: the sentientist supports preservation when it is good for individuals and questions it only when it harms them

Full chapterN11

Sentientists want to get rid of forests

Straw man: the real proposals are research, targeted help and caution, and recognizing that there is suffering in a place isn't proposing to destroy it

Full chapterN12

Sentientists want to get rid of predators

Killing predators is what the position rejects, for the very ecological reason the objection gives; what exists is long-term research on reducing predation without causing harm

Full chapterN13

Sentientists defend parasitism

Incomplete count: without treatment many parasites die with the host, death is only a harm to those who feel and would have a good life, and preventing is not killing

Full chapterN14

Sentientists support population control and antinatalism for wild animals

It's partly true, but there's no ready-made answer: it depends on which animals would be harmed and which would benefit, and the serious position is to research before acting

Full chapterN15

Sentientists give the same weight to insects, invertebrates and mammals

Confuses equal consideration with equal weight: similar interests weigh the same, and each being's weight depends on how much it can feel, which is uncertain but not zero

Full chapterN16

Machines will never feel; robots and AIs need no rights

If an artificial mind ever feels, its substrate matters no more than species does; and uncertainty calls for precaution before creating minds at scale

Full chapterN17

Those not yet born don't matter

Whoever is born will feel what we leave them: that's why burying toxic waste is wrong, even if the victims don't exist yet

Full chapterN18

We have to worry about future humans, not about animals

It has already accepted the hard part, that future beings count; what is missing is why, once that future arrives, only the humans in it would count, and most of those who will feel will not be human

Full chapterN19

There's no way to plan for the long term, since we can't predict a distant future

It works against plans that depend on getting one scenario right, not against actions that pay off in almost all of them; and uncertainty cuts both ways

Full chapterN20

The future will certainly be better, so there's no need to worry so much

Moral progress is not automatic: every advance had someone making it happen; and with animals the recent trend runs the other way, because power grew without consideration keeping up

Full chapterN21

That's science fiction; we have real problems now

An uncertain but immense risk can deserve attention, and much of what reduces future suffering, like widening the moral circle, also helps now
