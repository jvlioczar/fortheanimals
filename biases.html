Biases

Cognitive dissonance (the meat paradox)

Loving animals and eating them creates tension, resolved by denying minds to the animals eaten (Bastian et al., 2012)

A5A14A16A19A25A26B1B5B6B9C1C7E1E2E7E8E12F11G20H13J11J12K1L4L22

Motivated reasoning

Looking for reasons for the conclusion you already want

A1A4A11A12A14A15A18A21A23A24B4C1C2C8C11C12D3D4D5D11D12E2E4E10E11E13E16F1F2F3F4F6F7F8F9F11G11G13H2H5H11H12H15I2I3I6I7I9I10I11I12J2J5J6J18J21K1K4K9L10L17L20L25M8M9M10

Confirmation bias

Accepting only what confirms a prior belief

B9C5D9D10E16G1G2G4G7G10G12G13G14G15G17G18G19H1H3H6H16L10L15L20M1M5M9

Status quo bias

Preferring the current state because it is current

A5A9A17B6C1C8D1D2D6D7D8D13E6E9E11E12E15G20H4H9I1I8J3J5J9J11J13J14J15J18K10L13L27M1M2M3M4N6N8N15N18N19N20

System justification

Legitimizing the existing order to reduce discomfort

A2A7A9A17A20B1B8B9B10C6C9D1D4D7H4H5H11I3I6I7J3J4J19

Willful ignorance

Avoiding information that would create an obligation

B1B2B5B7B8B9B10C10E7L20

Functional categorization

"Food" animals are granted less mind than "pet" animals

A1A8A10A17A19C9C10E8E9F6F8F10H13I4I9I11L22

Ingroup bias

Valuing "our own" and devaluing outsiders

A2A7A8A9A10A11A12A13A15A16A20A21A24A25D2D8D13I8J4J7J14J23L2L12L14L17L18L23M10N3N4N10N15

Scope insensitivity

Billions of victims don't weigh more than one

A6A14A15A18B1B2F11H2H14J22J23K1L18L24N3N7N12N14N16N17N18N19

Diffusion of responsibility

Shared blame feels like no blame

B4H12J15K1K2K4K7K9

Psychological reactance

Pushing back when freedom feels threatened

E1E4J7J16J17K6L1L9L21L26M6M7

Identity-protective cognition

Rejecting ideas that threaten the group or one's identity

D1D13J7J14J19L4L12L19L23L24L25M10

Affect heuristic

"I like it, so it's good"

B7J10J11L3L7L18M8

Moral licensing

One good act licenses a bad one

B2B4B7C12E5E6H15J12L4L21L22

Optimism bias and exceptionalism

"My meat is different"

B1B2B5B10H3H8H15H16K5

Survivorship bias

We only see those who made it

D1D9G9G12N1

The 4Ns rationalization

Justifying eating animals by saying it is natural, normal, necessary and nice (Piazza and colleagues, 2015).

A13B6C1C2C3C4C5C7C10C11C12D1D2D3D5D6D8D11D13E1E9E14F1G1G2G3G4G5G6G8G9G12G15G18I4J1J10J11J16J17J18J22K10L16M1

Hypocrisy hunting

Looking for an inconsistency in whoever defends an idea so as not to examine the idea.

F4F9F10H1H2H6H7H9H16I2I11L5L8L17L18L19L23M7

Mind denial

Attributing less mind and less capacity to suffer to the animals we eat.

A3A4A5A6A8A10A12A19A22A25A26E7E8E10F7G6H10H14I5I10J22J23N14N15

Do-gooder derogation

Belittling people who act for moral reasons, because their conduct feels like an accusation.

J20L1L2L3L5L6L9L11L13L19L21L22L26

Reactive devaluation

Rejecting a proposal because it came from the other side.

J19K6L1L11L20N11

Messenger effect

Judging the message by who says it rather than by what it says.

J21L1L3L4L17L19L20M6

Bandwagon effect and social conformity

Adopting a habit or an idea because many people around you do.

D1D6D13E16J13J20M2M3M7M9

Pluralistic ignorance

Many disagree in private but think everyone else agrees.

D6D8J20

Default effect

Sticking with the option that comes ready-made, like the plate with meat.

D1E1E5J9

Illusory truth and mere exposure effects

What you hear often seems true, and what is familiar seems better.

C3G1G2G5G7

Omission bias and perpetrator bias

Judging as less serious the harm we don't cause with our own hands, or that results from not acting.

F3K2K7N2N4N5N6N7N8N9N12N13

Intention bias

Reacting more strongly to harm when someone caused it on purpose than to equal harm from causes with no one to blame.

N2N4N9

Teleological bias and agency detection

Seeing purpose and intention in nature: animals would "exist for" something.

C7C9D4

Collapse of compassion

The more victims, the less we feel for each; one identifiable victim moves us more than millions.

A18F4H14J23K1L18M8N1N10N16N17

Loss aversion and the endowment effect

Losing what you have weighs more than gaining something equivalent.

J10J11J13J20

Sunk cost and effort justification

Carrying on because you've invested a lot, not because it's worth it.

E12J14

Rosy retrospection

Remembering the past as better than it was.

D1D8D9

Overconfidence (Dunning-Kruger)

People who know little about a topic tend to overestimate what they know.

G12G13

Naive realism and the bias blind spot

Thinking we see the world as it is and only others are biased.

E9E10L2L17

Anthropocentric thinking

Using humans as the measure of everything, including of what counts as feeling.

A19A22

Naturalness bias

What seems natural is judged better and safer.

C1C3C12F3G3G14L26N10N11N13

Availability bias

Judging by the easiest memory, like an acquaintance's case, rather than by the data.

D9G8G10G18G19J1J21L11L21L25M4M6M8M10

Authority bias

Accepting a claim because of the prestige of whoever makes it.

B8D10E16G17M9

Present bias

Giving too much weight to now and too little to future consequences.

E12J9J11K5N16N18N20

Halo effect

One good quality, like "local" or "organic", colors the judgment of everything else.

E14H8H10H15H16I5N10

Social dominance orientation

Preferring hierarchies between groups, including between species.

A2A7C4C6I12

Psychological essentialism

Believing each kind of being has a fixed essence that defines its place.

A4A8A10A11A12A20C9

Masculinity and meat

Associating eating meat with manliness, which makes change feel like a threat to identity.

G7G9G16L8L12

Stereotyping

Judging someone by a simplified image of the group they belong to.

G16J2J19J21K6L14L19L21L23L24L25L26L27M6M7N11N13

Dissociation and euphemism

Separating the animal from the food, including through the words we use.

C7C12D12E15K2K7M2

False consensus

Overestimating how many people think like us.

A23

Food neophobia

Distrusting unfamiliar foods.

J10

Nocebo effect

Expecting something to do harm produces real symptoms.

G11

Salience bias

Giving weight to what grabs attention, like the distance traveled, rather than to what matters most.

H7H16L24N11

Middle-ground bias

Thinking the middle position is always the most sensible.

E5H15

Empathy gap

Feeling less empathy for an animal the more evolutionarily distant it is from us.

A15H14N14

Sizeism

Giving an animal less importance because it is small.

H14N14

Intelligence bias

Giving an animal less importance because it is seen as not very intelligent.

A25A26

Substratism

Giving a being less importance because of the material it is made of, as with non-organic beings.

N15

Proportion bias

Judging help by the fraction of the problem it solves rather than by the number of individuals helped.

K1N7

Just-world hypothesis

Believing the world is fair, so those who suffer are getting what is due to them.

D12N2

Neglect of probability

Deciding under uncertainty without weighing the probability, treating a risk as certain or as nonexistent.

N20

Ambiguity effect

Avoiding options whose chance of success is unknown, even when they may be the best ones.

N6N8

Evaluability bias

Giving more weight to what is easy to measure than to what matters most.

N20

Duration neglect

Judging an experience by its striking moments and its end, without counting how long it lasted.

B5

Pro-innovation bias

Overestimating what an innovation will solve and not seeing its limits.

K5

Belief digitization

Treating a probability as if it were zero or one instead of taking the uncertainty into account.

A15N15

Belief perseverance

Holding on to a belief even after learning of evidence against it.

G13N1

Continued influence effect

Information that has already been corrected keeps influencing what a person thinks.

G2G7

Zero-sum bias

Seeing a situation as a contest in which one side's gain is the other's loss, even when it is not.

J6L6

Availability cascade

An idea gains credit by being repeated a lot in public, and the more credit it gains, the more it is repeated.

A14

Illusory correlation

Seeing a link between two things that merely happened together.

G11M1

Framing effect

The same information leads to different judgments depending on how it is presented.

B1B2J22

Illusion of explanatory depth

Thinking you understand how something works much better than you actually do.
