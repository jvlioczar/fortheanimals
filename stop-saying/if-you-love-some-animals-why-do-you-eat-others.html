V24

"If you love some animals, why do you eat others?"

Wrong criterion

They ground the defense of animals in faith, affection or hierarchy, not in sentience.

Why it is weak

The question points to a real inconsistency, which is why it is tempting: treating a dog as family while paying for the death of a pig, who feels just the same, has no justification. But, as it is usually put, it rests on affection, and affection is not what supports the defense of animals.

The conclusion can be reversed. If dogs and pigs have the same value, there are two ways to end the inconsistency: stop eating pigs or start eating dogs. The question only demands consistency; on its own, it gives no reason why killing is wrong.

The line between pets and food animals varies from culture to culture. The listener can reply that where dogs are eaten there is no inconsistency at all, which takes the conversation into cultural relativism.

The question assumes the person claims to love all animals. Many people only like their pets, and don't see themselves as someone who loves all of wildlife.

It invites a symmetrical counter-attack: "what about plants, which you also appreciate?". That counter-attack fails, because plants are not sentient, but the original question, based on affection, opens the door to it.

Resting the defense of animals on love weakens the conclusion and leaves out those who don't inspire affection: fish, crustaceans and insects would be left unprotected, although the reason to respect them is the same, the capacity to feel.

How to say it better

Point out the inconsistency, but rest the conclusion on what holds for everyone: a pig feels fear and pain just like a dog, and no one needs to love an animal in order not to cause it unnecessary suffering and death. For example: "You wouldn't do that to your dog because he feels. The pig feels too. What changes?"

The criticism is not aimed at noticing the inconsistency, which is real and studied as the "meat paradox". It is aimed at resting on affection: the duty not to exploit and not to kill without necessity doesn't depend on loving anyone.

Also heard as: “Why do you love your dog and eat a pig?”, “You pet your cat and eat a chicken?”.
