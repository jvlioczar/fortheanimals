"In a room with a rabbit and an apple, a child eats the apple and plays with the rabbit."

A fact with no moral weight

They bring up a biological curiosity as if it settled the question. What matters is what happens to the animals.

Why it is weak

It's an appeal to nature: it suggests that what is right is what a child would do by instinct. Natural doesn't define right, and vegans point out this same mistake in "it's natural to eat meat". If children had the urge to bite the rabbit, that would not make it just to kill it.

The fact also proves little. The child wouldn't kill the rabbit, but they wouldn't harvest wheat or peel a potato either: small children eat what adults prepare and put almost anything in their mouths. And the same child eats chicken nuggets without hesitation. What we eat is learned, and the scene does not show a "natural diet" of the species.

The line swaps the ethical question for a dispute about instincts, which is the favorite ground of those who defend eating meat. It only takes a reminder that humans have hunted for hundreds of thousands of years for the argument to fall, and the animals stay in the same situation.

The scenario is flawed, but the connection it suggests matters. The scene illustrates the concept of the absent referent, from the author Carol J. Adams: most of the time, the animal who became food is absent from the plate, hidden by words like "steak" and "chicken" and by packaging. Making the link between what we eat and the living animal is the central point. A viral 2017 video, shot by an Egyptian filmmaker in Saudi Arabia, shows two children, asked to prepare a chicken sandwich, who refuse to kill the live animal and cry. According to Fabio Chaves's investigation, no one knows what happened to the chicken, which probably met the fate of the others raised for consumption. If the scene is true, it shows that many people pay for an animal to be killed but would not have the courage to do it, and that the presence of the living animal awakens the refusal the plate hides. In other words, the discomfort with death exists, and habit and distance silence it. The rabbit line remains fallacious, because the reaction of children in a specific situation does not decide what is right, but that link deserves to be made.

How to say it better

Say what the research actually shows: young children value animals' lives more than adults do, and many do not even know that meat comes from animals. The split between "animals to love" and "animals to eat" is taught. That does not prove what is right, but it shows that eating animals is a learned habit, and habits can be revised.

In a 2021 study of 4- to 7-year-olds in the United States, many classified animal-based foods, such as bacon and hot dogs, as plants. In another, from 2022, children were less speciesist than adults.
