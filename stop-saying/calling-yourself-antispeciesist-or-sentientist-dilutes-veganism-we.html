V33

"Calling yourself antispeciesist or sentientist dilutes veganism: we don't need new terms, we need to strengthen the old ones."

Objection in disguise

They look like concern for strategy or for the animals, but become a ready excuse to change nothing.

Why it is weak

It starts from a reasonable concern: too many terms can confuse newcomers, and "vegan" is already a well-known word.

But the terms aren't synonyms. Veganism is rejecting animal exploitation. Antispeciesism is giving equal weight to similar interests, whatever the species, which also applies outside exploitation, as with wild animal suffering or the treatment of animals seen as pests. Sentientism is taking sentience as the criterion for moral consideration, which includes possible artificial minds. A person can be vegan and still speciesist, which is why the words help name different things.

Nor are they so new. "Speciesism" was coined by the psychologist Richard Ryder in 1970, and the word "vegan" itself was created in 1944 precisely to name a position that "vegetarian" no longer described. Rejecting a useful distinction out of attachment to an old term is the mistake that would have been made by rejecting the word vegan.

How to say it better

Use each term for what it means, and explain what you mean when you use it. No one needs to label themselves with all of them. What matters is the idea: animals shouldn't be exploited, and similar interests count equally, whatever the species.
