V24

"Animals have a right to life, period."

Wrong criterion

They ground the defense of animals in faith, affection or hierarchy, not in sentience.

Why it is weak

Without a reason, the sentence begs the question: it says killing animals is wrong because they have a right not to be killed, which only restates the conclusion in other words. Someone who does not yet accept the conclusion has no reason to accept the premise.

The flaw is not unique to vegans. The philosopher Nathan Nobis, in Animals & Ethics 101 (2016), shows that it appears on both sides: "the fetus has a right to life" and "we have a right to eat animals" also restate the conclusion, if nobody says why that right would exist.

The sentence also skips a question: a right to what? There is no right in general, only rights to something or against something, such as not being killed, not suffering or not being treated as property, and each one needs its own justification. The "period" ends the conversation exactly where it should begin.

How to say it better

Say why: animals are sentient, have an interest in going on living and are harmed by death. That reason is what supports the right, and it is what the other person can evaluate.

None of this means animals have no right to life. Philosophers such as Tom Regan argue that they do. The problem is asserting the right without the reason that supports it.
