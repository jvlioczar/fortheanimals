V74

"Veganism shouldn't ally itself with other causes, such as feminism, abolitionism or transhumanism."

The cause as a tool

They use the animal movement for another purpose, or pretend two goals never conflict.

Why it is weak

It has a legitimate concern: an alliance can shift focus, make the animal cause look like part of a political package and push away those who disagree with the other causes. And there's the opposite risk, of another cause using the animals' cause merely as a tool.

But turning this into a fixed rule is a mistake. The animal movement has learned a lot from others: the term abolitionism and several strategies come from the fight against slavery, and feminism and ecofeminism brought critiques of the logic of domination. Sentientist transhumanism argues for reducing the suffering of all beings. And campaigns that link the conditions of slaughterhouse workers to those of the animals can achieve more than each cause alone.

Conflicts exist too: some human causes defend practices that exploit animals, and alliances can demand silence about them. So the useful question isn't whether to ally or not, but whether this alliance, in this case, helps animals without harming other groups.

How to say it better

Veganism's focus is animal exploitation, and no one is obliged to embrace other causes. But alliances can be judged case by case, by what they bring to animals, and never at the expense of other oppressed groups.

It's a contested question in the movement: some defend single-issue veganism and others intersectional veganism. The guide doesn't pick a side in advance; it asks that each alliance be judged by its effects.
