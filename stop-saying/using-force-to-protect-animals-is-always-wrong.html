V7

"Using force to protect animals is always wrong."

Exaggeration or attack

They claim more than the evidence supports, or attack people instead of discussing what they do.

Why it is weak

The question is more open than that certainty suggests. Absolute pacifism trips over its own logic: refusing to stop a greater harm, in practice, lets it happen. And we accept forceful intervention for humans who cannot defend themselves, such as a child being attacked.

"It turns the public away" has been repeated for decades with little solid evidence. Concern for animal welfare grew during the period when direct action groups were already operating, which proves nothing in either direction.

The best-documented cost is a different one: harsher laws against activism, such as the US Animal Enterprise Terrorism Act and the "ag-gag" laws that criminalize filming farms. On the other side of the scale are rescues and exposés that saved animals. No one has shown which side weighs more.

The mirror-image error is just as bad: saying force works. Reviews of social movements suggest confrontational tactics have ambiguous effects, and actions meant to be non-lethal have had tragic consequences for the animals themselves.

There is a parallel with the paradox of tolerance, described by the philosopher Karl Popper in 1945: unlimited tolerance, which tolerates even those who want to destroy it, ends up disappearing. In a similar way, refusing any force whatsoever, in any situation, leaves the way open for the violence already being done to animals: what could be called a paradox of nonviolence. The parallel has limits. Popper held that the intolerant should first be answered with arguments, and accepted force only against those who refuse debate and turn to violence. What the comparison shows is that absolute pacifism also has a cost, not that force is justified in each case.

How to say it better

Separate the cases, because rescue, property damage and violence against people are different things, and ask what each one actually produces, with evidence instead of conviction.

This guide does not recommend any violent or illegal action. The point is the quality of the argument: violence against people finds very little support even among theorists sympathetic to the discussion, and the philosophical question about the other cases remains open.
