V8

"If humans disappeared, the planet and the animals would be better off."

Exaggeration or attack

They claim more than the evidence supports, or attack people instead of discussing what they do.

Why it is weak

It's false. Without humans, animal exploitation would end, but the suffering of animals in the wild would go on: hunger, cold, disease, parasites and predation affect enormous numbers of animals, and in most species almost all young die shortly after birth. Given the number of animals involved, this is probably the greatest suffering there is, and it could even get worse in some scenarios.

Only humans have moral agency, that is, only they can recognize the suffering of other beings and choose to act to reduce it. Without humans, there would be no one left who could help an injured animal, vaccinate wild populations or research ways to reduce suffering in nature. Oral rabies vaccination of foxes, for example, eliminated the disease from much of Western Europe.

Animal exploitation can end without humans disappearing: it depends on human choices, and it can end through a collective change in those choices.

It is also said that, without humans, the extinction of other species would drop sharply and ecosystems would recover. That has a basis: changes in land and sea use and direct exploitation, caused by humans, are today the main causes of species loss. But species and ecosystems don't feel; individuals do, and fewer extinctions or more stable ecosystems don't mean animals living better: an ecosystem's balance is maintained precisely by the death of most of the young. Without humans, natural areas would probably expand, and with them the number of wild animals, most of whom are born only to die early, of hunger, cold, disease or predation. The arguments on why species is not what matters morally (A10), on the difference between ecological balance and welfare (N3) and on preserving species (N15) explain the mistake, and the page Wild animal suffering shows the scale of that suffering.

The phrase is also misanthropic, and it pushes away exactly the people who would need to change. It reinforces the accusation that vegans care more about animals than about people (L21).

How to say it better

Say that the problem is human practices, not the existence of humans. In any scenario, the best path is for humans to include animals in their moral consideration: to end exploitation and, in the future, to help animals in the wild too.

This doesn't mean humans don't cause enormous harm to animals and the environment. It means the solution lies in changing what humans do, because only they can change.
