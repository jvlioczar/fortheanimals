V12

"We need to give animals rights."

Exaggeration or attack

They claim more than the evidence supports, or attack people instead of discussing what they do.

Why it is weak

It mixes two senses of "right". Legal rights are created by laws and courts, and those can indeed be given. Moral rights, if they exist, do not depend on being granted: enslaved people already had a right to life and liberty before the law recognized it, which is why slavery was unjust even when it was legal.

Talking about "giving" moral rights unwittingly concedes the claim of those who say rights are a human invention (A22): if they depended on the will of whoever grants them, animals would have none until someone decided to give them, and nobody would wrong them in the meantime.

The philosopher Nathan Nobis, in Animals & Ethics 101 (2016), points to this assumption in the question "should we give animals rights?". The moral question is whether they have rights, and to what; the political question is whether the law should recognize and protect them.

How to say it better

Separate the two things: recognizing the moral rights animals already have, because they are sentient and have interests, and creating the legal rights that protect them. "Recognizing animal rights" and "protecting animals by law" say this without ambiguity.

For legal rights, "giving rights" is correct, and it is how lawyers usually speak. The problem is using the phrase for moral rights. And some defend animals without talking about rights, such as Peter Singer (E7).
