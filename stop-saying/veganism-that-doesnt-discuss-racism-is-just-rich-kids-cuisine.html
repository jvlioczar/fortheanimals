V79

"Veganism that doesn't discuss racism is just rich kids' cuisine."

The cause as a tool

They use the animal movement for another purpose, or pretend two goals never conflict.

Why it is weak

The concern has a basis. Racism can affect who has access to healthy food and who does the hardest jobs in the meat chain, and Black authors such as A. Breeze Harper, in Sistah Vegan (2010), and Aph Ko and Syl Ko, in Aphro-ism (2017), have criticized the way veganism became associated with white, middle-class people, leaving aside those who already lived these issues.

And vegans do need to be antiracist, so as not to contradict themselves. All people are sentient beings, and discriminating against someone for a biological trait, such as skin color, is the same mistake speciesism makes with species: using a trait that doesn't change the capacity to suffer to give less weight to someone's interests. Whoever rejects speciesism and accepts racism abandons their own criterion.

But turning that debate into a condition for veganism to have value is the same move as saying that veganism without class struggle is gardening: it makes another cause a prerequisite. Veganism rests on the fact that animals feel, and the injustice against them doesn't stop existing when someone doesn't discuss another topic.

"Rich kids' cuisine" also swaps the argument for a label about the person: it says who they supposedly are, not whether the case for animals is right. And fighting racism, including within the movement, doesn't depend on every action for animals including that debate.

How to say it better

Fight racism, including among vegans, and show where the causes meet, such as access to food and working conditions in slaughterhouses, without saying that veganism without that debate is worthless.

This is a contested question in the movement: some defend intersectional veganism and others single-issue veganism. What this entry questions is the phrase that dismisses veganism without that debate, not the importance of fighting racism.
