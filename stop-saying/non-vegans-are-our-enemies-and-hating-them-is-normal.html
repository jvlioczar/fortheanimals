Home / Weak arguments / Exaggeration or attack

V5

"Non-vegans are our enemies, and hating them is normal."

Exaggeration or attack

They claim more than the evidence supports, or attack people instead of discussing what they do.

Why it is weak

Almost every vegan was once a non-vegan. People who eat animals today are, in general, where we used to be: repeating a habit learned in childhood, without ever having stopped to examine it. Treating them as enemies is treating who we were as an enemy.

Hatred aims at people, and the problem lies in the practice. You can regard animal exploitation as a grave injustice and still recognize that those who take part in it are usually ordinary people, capable of affection and of change (E13).

It is counterproductive. The people who still eat animals are the only ones who can stop: they are the audience, not the adversary. Someone who feels hated defends themselves instead of listening. And living angry at almost everyone wears advocates down and leads many to leave activism.

Anger at what happens to animals is understandable, and feeling it is not a flaw. The mistake is turning the feeling into a rule, saying that hating is normal and aiming the hatred at people instead of aiming the energy at what needs to change.

How to say it better

Aim your indignation at the practice and at those who organize it and profit from it, and treat each person as someone who can change, as you did. Firm with the argument, respectful with the person.

Feeling anger at an injustice is human. What is at issue here is what you do with it: hating people helps no animal.
