What if the person starts from a political theory?

Liberals, Marxists, anarchists, utilitarians, feminists: each theory has a principle, and none offers, on its own terms, a reason to leave animals out.

Liberalism

"Freedom is a right of rational individuals; animals aren't subjects of liberal rights."

Libertarianism and anarcho-capitalism

"What's mine is mine: animals are property, and the freedom to use property is sacred."

Marxism, socialism and communism

"Exploitation is a relation between human classes. Animals aren't workers, and the struggle is a different one."

Anarchism

"Anarchism fights domination between people; animals are not part of that."

Utilitarianism

"Human pleasures are worth more: better to be a human dissatisfied than a pig satisfied."

Contractualism and Rawlsian justice

"Justice holds among those who have a sense of justice and can make agreements."

Deontology and Kantianism

"Only rational beings are ends in themselves; toward animals we have, at most, indirect duties."

Communitarianism

"Duties arise from the community and its traditions, and animals are not members of it."

Egalitarianism and prioritarianism

"Equality is among human citizens; animals belong to another category."

Ethics of care

"Care applies to close relationships, not to unknown animals."

Virtue ethics

"Virtue is about human character; treating animals well matters only because it makes us better."

Feminism

"Feminism is about women; talking about cows and hens is changing the subject, or even offensive."

Anti-racism and anti-ableism

"Comparing speciesism to racism or ableism is offensive; they are different things."

Antispeciesism and sentientism

Veganism is one consequence of a broader criterion: what matters morally is the capacity to be harmed or benefited.

Overview

The state of the art in animal ethics

Since 1975, animal ethics has been tested on ever harder cases, by authors from schools that disagree with each other, and has reached the same rejection of speciesism.

Ethical question

Wild animal suffering

Hunger, disease and predation were not caused by us. But if sentience is what matters, this suffering cannot be dismissed just because it is natural.

Possibility

Sentience in AI and robots

No current system is recognized as sentient. But the possibility is not absurd, and the criterion does not depend on what a being is made of.

Debate

The definition of veganism, debated

What defines veganism: refusing the exploitation of animals, or refusing everything of animal origin? The question has come back strongly with cultivated meat and precision fermentation.

Possibility

Sentience in extraterrestrials, plants and other forms of life

There is no evidence of life beyond Earth, let alone sentient life, and no robust evidence that plants or fungi feel. Both questions test whether our criterion is really a criterion, and what we would do if the evidence changed.

Foundation

What supports animal ethics, and what weakens it

Critical reason, relevance, impartiality and sentience support veganism and animal ethics. Partiality, relativism, tribalism, ableism, making abstractions sacred and weak will weaken them.

Strategy

Restraint with indirect arguments

Just as vegans should be careful when criticizing other vegans, those who use health and environment arguments should be careful not to strengthen anthropocentrism and environmentalism, which work against animals.

Strategy

The common minimum

Finding the point of convergence: someone who rejects the whole conclusion can almost always accept part of it. Showing that part is not giving up what you argue for, it is not demanding that people change their worldview before taking the first step.

Strategy

When logic isn't enough

Facts and arguments show what is right, but they rarely suffice to make someone change. Apathy, habit and indifference call for other reasons: someone to care about, people to change with and an easy path to follow.

The currents of the vegan movement

All vegans want the end of animal exploitation. The currents disagree about why, with whom and how to get there. A map of the ends, the means and the main debates.
