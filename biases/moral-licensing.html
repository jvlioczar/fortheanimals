Moral licensing

One good act licenses a bad one

What it is

After doing something good, feeling entitled to do something bad, as if the good deed had earned a credit.

An analogy: Ordering the bigger dessert because you went to the gym in the morning.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 19 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • B4 Free-range animals are happy: "Better" consumption licenses carrying on consuming
  • B5 Milk and eggs don't kill anyone: Giving up meat licenses keeping the rest
  • C7 We are animals and part of the ecosystem; no one lives without impact: Acting "consciously" seems to clear the act itself
  • C13 I honor the animals I eat: Giving thanks makes the act feel fair
  • E9 Everything in moderation: Having cut back seems to license the rest
  • E10 Nobody's perfect; I do what I can: Virtuous actions in one area seem to compensate for others
  • E16 I agree with vegans; veganism is an important movement: Declaring support gives the feeling of having already contributed
  • H14 What matters is how food is grown, not whether it comes from animals: Choosing a good farmer seems to waive the question about the animal
  • H20 Kangaroo meat is the most sustainable meat there is: The "green" choice gives permission to ignore the rest
  • I14 I only use cruelty-free products: Having done something good reduces attention to the rest
  • I27 I fish what I eat; it's more honest than buying at the store: Being more honest than others gives permission to carry on
  • J8 I eat little meat; that's enough: The good deed seems to make up for the rest
  • J9 I swapped red meat for chicken, which is healthier: Cutting red meat seems to make up for the rest
  • J10 I'm vegetarian for the animals; I already do my part: Having done something good reduces attention to the rest
  • K4 The problem is the industry, not consumers: Criticizing the system gives the feeling of having done one's part
  • L11 You use a phone, so you're a hypocrite: Pointing out others' flaws makes one's own conduct seem acceptable
  • L13 A vegan who buys from companies using slave labor isn't really vegan: Pointing out another's failing eases one's own
  • L29 Vegans treat vegetarians as enemies: The step already taken seems to excuse the next one
  • L30 Vegans think they're the only ones who really defend animals: The good done for dogs and cats seems to make up for the rest

When animal advocates make the same mistake

The Stop saying this catalogue does not yet record an example of this error among animal advocates. That does not mean it does not happen: the same care applies to both sides.
