Identity-protective cognition

Rejecting ideas that threaten the group or one's identity

What it is

Assessing facts and arguments in a way that protects one's identity and place in one's group. Accepting the idea would have a social cost, so it is rejected before being examined.

An analogy: Refusing a fact about one's own neighborhood because admitting it would be betraying the neighbors.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 15 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • D1 We've always done it: Questioning tradition feels like an attack on the group
  • D5 I don't want to dishonor my parents and my ancestors: Questioning the practice feels like an attack on the family
  • I21 A rodeo animal is a well-treated athlete and only bucks for a few seconds: The festival is part of its defenders' identity
  • I22 The fighting bull lives free in the field and dies fighting: Bullfighting is part of its defenders' identity
  • J14 Vegans are against farmers: Criticism of animal farming is felt as an attack on the countryside and those who live from it
  • J17 Fishers and ranchers would lose their identity: Identity doesn't justify harm to others; it calls for a just transition
  • J22 Veganism is a political ideology or a globalist agenda: Guilt by association and conspiracy theory; it doesn't assess the argument
  • L6 Meat is for real men: A gender stereotype; masculinity is neither a moral nor a nutritional criterion
  • L11 You use a phone, so you're a hypocrite: The criticism is felt as an attack on one's own group
  • L19 There are sexist, racist and transphobic vegans: The cause is rejected because it seems to belong to the opposing political group
  • L20 Vegans defend animals, but they support abortion: The topic is pulled into a dispute where each side already has a team
  • L23 Vegans are racist, because they criticize Afro-Brazilian religions: Criticism of the practice is felt as an attack on the group, which has historical reasons for distrust
  • L24 Vegans are against religion: Questioning a custom is felt as a threat to the faith community
  • L37 I hate vegans: Criticism of the habit is felt as criticism of the person
  • M9 I was vegan and started getting too radical, too rigid: Someone whose identity was tied to the role of "the radical" feels that leaving it means dropping the principle too

When animal advocates make the same mistake

The Stop saying this catalogue does not yet record an example of this error among animal advocates. That does not mean it does not happen: the same care applies to both sides.
