Stereotyping

Judging someone by a simplified image of the group they belong to.

What it is

Attributing to all members of a group traits that apply, at most, to some, and judging each person by that ready-made image.

An analogy: Thinking every fan of a rival team is a troublemaker.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 20 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A18 It's normal for humans to be rational: Transfers group characteristics to the individual
  • G12 Vegans are weak, pale and depressed: A stereotype; the existing correlations have uncertain or reverse causation
  • I31 Guide and assistance dogs are well cared for and do important work: Vegans are imagined as condemning any life shared with animals
  • J2 Poor people have no access: A real barrier for some, which doesn't apply to the person objecting; and many cultures have old vegetarian traditions
  • J14 Vegans are against farmers: The vegan is imagined as a city person who looks down on rural life
  • J19 Veganism has become big business: The vegan is imagined as rich and consumerist
  • K9 Radical veganism pushes people away: May be a fair point about strategy, but it is neither a moral rebuttal nor a personal excuse
  • L1 Vegans are annoying and think they are better: The "annoying vegan" label is applied to all, including those who never said anything
  • L2 Vegans are all hippies: A stereotype and an attack on the person: animals' suffering doesn't depend on the lifestyle of whoever points it out
  • L4 Sentientists are even crazier than vegans: Attack on the person and appeal to ridicule: strangeness is not error, and the fair test is to apply the same scenario to humans and examine the premise
  • L8 Veganism is moral puritanism: The vegan is imagined as a bitter ascetic
  • L19 There are sexist, racist and transphobic vegans: Tu quoque and an attack on the messenger: a vegan's failing in another cause doesn't change what happens to animals, and the same reasoning would bring down any cause
  • L20 Vegans defend animals, but they support abortion: Vegans are seen as a homogeneous political group
  • L23 Vegans are racist, because they criticize Afro-Brazilian religions: Vegans are seen as a white, wealthy and hostile bloc
  • L24 Vegans are against religion: Vegans are imagined as young urban people with no religion
  • L29 Vegans treat vegetarians as enemies: An attack on the messenger: some vegans' hostility is a mistake, but the criticism is of milk and egg production, not of people
  • M6 Vegans are annoying and bossy; I don't want to be part of that group: Confuses the principle with the group: you can leave hostile people without ceasing to spare animals
  • M7 I don't want to be labeled; the label brings pressure: Confuses the label with the practice: the name is optional, and what reaches the animals is what one does

In helping wild animals

  • N16 Sentientists want to get rid of forests: Every sentientist is imagined as the author of the most radical text
  • N18 Sentientists defend parasites: Incomplete count: without treatment many parasites die with the host, death is only a harm to those who feel and would have a good life, and preventing is not killing

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V6 Meat eaters are psychopaths: It's inaccurate and counterproductive. Most of these people have genuine emotional bonds.
