The 4Ns rationalization

Justifying eating animals by saying it is natural, normal, necessary and nice (Piazza and colleagues, 2015).

What it is

Justifying the consumption of animal products with four ideas that researchers grouped as the "4Ns": it is natural, normal, necessary and nice. They tend to come ready-made, before any reflection.

An analogy: Defending a habit by saying "everyone does it, that is how it is and you cannot live without it", without examining any of the three.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 51 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A12 It's natural to put our own first: Partiality lets us prefer our own; it does not let us exploit others
  • A25 Treating animals well is a matter of compassion, not justice: Consigning the issue to conscience avoids the question about common practice
  • A26 Equating animals with disabled humans will degrade humans: The objection becomes a ready reason not to change practice
  • B7 Without farming, these animals wouldn't even exist: The animals' existence becomes a ready-made justification
  • B18 The problem is suffering, not use: Consumption seems "normal" as long as there's no visible cruelty
  • B19 Eating animals isn't cruelty; it's for nourishment: The eater's intention doesn't change what the animal suffers, and nourishment can be had without animals, so that suffering is unnecessary
  • C1 It's natural to eat meat: "Natural" is one of the most used justifications and is linked to less guilt
  • C2 We have canine teeth; we are omnivores: "Natural" and "necessary" appear together
  • C3 We naturally crave meat: "Natural" and "nice"
  • C4 Eating meat made us evolve: "Natural" and "necessary"
  • C5 Lions eat other animals: "Natural" is one of the most used justifications
  • C6 It's the circle of life: "Natural" as a ready-made justification
  • C7 We are animals and part of the ecosystem; no one lives without impact: "Natural" is one of the four most common justifications for eating meat
  • C8 We're at the top of the food chain: "Natural" as a ready-made justification
  • C11 Cows need to be milked: "Natural" and "necessary"
  • D1 We've always done it: "Normal" is one of the most used justifications and is linked to less guilt
  • D2 Everybody eats meat: "Normal" as a justification
  • D3 Barbecues bring the family together: "Normal" and "nice"
  • D5 I don't want to dishonor my parents and my ancestors: "Normal": it's what the family has always done
  • D6 It's cultural; criticizing it is ethnocentric: "Normal" as a justification
  • D7 Indigenous and traditional peoples eat animals: "Necessary" and "natural"
  • D11 Jesus ate fish: "Normal" and "natural" with religious backing
  • D12 My religion approves of eating animals: Permitting is not requiring, and religious traditions themselves forbid unnecessary suffering, which today's production causes on a massive scale
  • E1 It's my personal choice; don't impose your morals: Personal choice works as a ready-made justification, linked to less guilt
  • E4 Eating meat isn't an ideology; veganism is: The dominant norm is invisible to those who follow it (carnism, Joy 2009)
  • E13 He eats meat, but he's a good person: Confuses judging the person with judging the act: good people also take part in injustices that custom accepts
  • E18 Why should I be ethical if it doesn't benefit me?: The lack of benefit becomes one more ready-made reason not to change
  • F1 On a desert island, would you eat an animal?: Extreme necessity doesn't justify the practice in normal conditions
  • G1 Eating animals is necessary to survive: "Natural, normal, necessary and nice" are the four commonest justifications, and this is the third
  • G2 We need animal protein: Protein needs are met by legumes, grains and soy
  • G3 Plant protein is incomplete: A 1970s myth; variety over the day provides all the essential amino acids
  • G4 If you need B12 supplements, it isn't natural: Farm animals also get supplemented B12; and natural is not the criterion (C1)
  • G5 You'll lack iron, zinc and other micronutrients: Plannable with a varied diet; iron deficiency is also common in meat eaters
  • G6 Milk is necessary for your bones: Calcium is found in plants and fortified foods
  • G7 Only fish provides omega-3: Fish get their DHA from algae, a direct source available to us
  • G8 Plants have antinutrients: Doses and preparation make them irrelevant; populations eating plant-based diets live longer
  • G10 Children and pregnant women can't be vegan: Major nutrition bodies consider it appropriate at every life stage, with planning
  • G11 Athletes need meat: There are elite vegan athletes; adequate protein intake is what matters
  • G16 A carnivore, paleo or keto diet is healthiest: Weak, short-term evidence that contradicts the large cohort studies
  • G19 Fruitarianism isn't healthy, so veganism isn't either: Veganism isn't fruitarianism: an all-fruit diet is risky, but a varied, planned vegan diet with B12 is considered adequate
  • I10 Sheep need to be shorn: Only because they were bred for excessive wool; mulesing, and slaughter when production ends
  • J1 Veganism is expensive: Beans, rice, grains and vegetables are among the cheapest foods
  • J6 Meat tastes good: Taste as a justification for killing: no other context accepts this
  • J7 Vegan food is bland: A generalization from unfamiliarity; most spices and many dishes are already plant-based
  • J16 This is my living; it feeds my family: Economic dependence explains why change is hard, but it does not make the activity just; the way out is a planned, supported transition
  • J18 Billions of people depend on fish for protein: Fishing is presented as necessary
  • K10 Vegans will always be a minority: Being a minority doesn't make something wrong or useless: change begins with a few, each person reduces demand, and animal protection laws change without a vegan majority
  • L10 Raising vegan children is imposing an ideology: Every family passes values on to its children, and serving meat is also the parents' choice; what matters is caring for the child's health and explaining the reasons
  • L35 My food beats yours: A taunt, not an argument: the pleasure of taste doesn't weigh against someone's life, and much vegan food is the same food as always
  • L36 Look what you're missing: Showing the plate doesn't answer the question about who became the plate; temptation doesn't make the act just
  • M1 I stopped being vegan because I started feeling unwell: The discomfort is real but rarely investigated: it usually comes from eating too little, lack of B12 or poor planning, and can be solved without animal products

When animal advocates make the same mistake

The Stop saying this catalogue does not yet record an example of this error among animal advocates. That does not mean it does not happen: the same care applies to both sides.
