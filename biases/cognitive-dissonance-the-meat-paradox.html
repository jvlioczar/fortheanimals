Cognitive dissonance (the meat paradox)

Loving animals and eating them creates tension, resolved by denying minds to the animals eaten (Bastian et al., 2012)

What it is

The discomfort of holding two conflicting beliefs or attitudes, such as liking animals and eating them. Instead of changing behavior, many people reduce the discomfort by changing the belief, for example by coming to see the animals they eat as less able to feel. Psychologists call this case the meat paradox.

An analogy: The smoker who knows cigarettes are harmful and starts saying "everyone dies of something".

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A1 Animals aren't conscious and don't feel pain like we do: Contradicts the neuroscientific evidence (Cambridge Declaration, 2012; New York Declaration, 2024)
  • A4 Plants feel too: Diluting the criterion eases the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • A5 There are different degrees of consciousness, so less conscious animals matter less: The scale eases the conflict between liking the animal and eating him or her
  • A7 Vegans anthropomorphize animals: Calling it projection eases the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • B1 There is humane slaughter: Eating meat leads people to attribute less mind and less moral status to the animal, which makes its death less troubling
  • B2 Killing without pain isn't harm: Attributing less mind to the animals we eat makes their death less troubling
  • B3 They had a good life and a quick death: The image of a good life eases the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • B7 Without farming, these animals wouldn't even exist: Presenting consumption as a benefit eases the tension
  • B9 Animal scientists take good care of the animals: The idea that someone competent is looking after the animals eases the conflict between liking them and eating them
  • B14 Those videos are exceptions or staged: Discrediting the image eases the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • B15 You don't live on a farm, so you don't know the truth: Disqualifying the speaker avoids facing what is said
  • B16 It's a fair trade: we care for them and feed them, and they give us meat: The idea of a trade eases the discomfort of eating someone you value
  • B17 Animals don't mind, they even like it and, in a way, they consent: Assuming the animal doesn't mind reduces the discomfort of eating them
  • B18 The problem is suffering, not use: Separating "use" from "suffering" eases the discomfort of eating animals
  • B19 Eating animals isn't cruelty; it's for nourishment: Redefining cruelty eases the discomfort between liking animals and eating them
  • C1 It's natural to eat meat: The tension between liking animals and eating them is eased by ready-made justifications
  • C6 It's the circle of life: The idea of a cycle eases the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • C13 I honor the animals I eat: A gesture of respect eases the discomfort of eating someone you value
  • E1 It's my personal choice; don't impose your morals: Treating the subject as taste avoids the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • E3 Morality is relative: Relativizing eases the tension between values and practice
  • E11 I love animals, but...: The conflict is reduced by denying minds to food animals
  • E12 I don't want to see those videos: Avoiding the image keeps affection and the plate apart
  • E15 I know it's wrong, but I won't change: The discomfort is tolerated or eased without changing the practice
  • E16 I agree with vegans; veganism is an important movement: The discomfort of agreeing and not acting is relieved by treating the cause as someone else's
  • E17 Being vegan is admirable, but it goes beyond duty: Classifying the change as heroic eases the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • E18 Why should I be ethical if it doesn't benefit me?: Reducing the question to "what's in it for me" eases the discomfort of admitting the wrong
  • F8 What if they discover that plants feel?: Diluting the criterion eases the tension between liking animals and eating them
  • F12 Would you eat animals if someone paid you well?: Showing that the other "would give in too" eases one's own discomfort
  • G1 Eating animals is necessary to survive: Calling necessary what is chosen reduces the discomfort
  • H15 If meat is sustainable, there's no problem: Focusing on the environment avoids thinking about the animal
  • J6 Meat tastes good: Eating meat leads to attributing less mind and less moral status to the animal
  • J8 I eat little meat; that's enough: Cutting down eases the tension without resolving it
  • K1 One person doesn't make a difference: Powerlessness eases the tension between loving animals and eating them
  • L11 You use a phone, so you're a hypocrite: The tension between loving animals and eating them is eased by putting down whoever raises it
  • L14 Vegans shouldn't use artificial intelligence: Pointing out the other's failing reduces discomfort with one's own
  • L15 Vegans shouldn't watch violent films or play violent games: Pointing out the other's failing reduces discomfort with one's own
  • L26 Vegans support pseudoscience: Discrediting the messenger avoids dealing with the message
  • L30 Vegans think they're the only ones who really defend animals: Loving animals and eating them creates a discomfort that needs an outlet
  • M10 I used to be vegan; I know what I'm talking about: Disparaging veganism eases the discomfort of having given up

When animal advocates make the same mistake
