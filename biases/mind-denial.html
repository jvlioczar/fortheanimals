Mind denial

Attributing less mind and less capacity to suffer to the animals we eat.

What it is

Attributing less mind, less intelligence and less capacity to suffer to the beings we use or eat. The belief tends to follow the habit: the more an animal is eaten, the less mind is recognized in it.

An analogy: Assuming the waiter does not mind being mistreated because "it is just their job".

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 31 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A1 Animals aren't conscious and don't feel pain like we do: Contradicts the neuroscientific evidence (Cambridge Declaration, 2012; New York Declaration, 2024)
  • A2 Fish and invertebrates don't feel pain: Growing evidence of sentience in fish, cephalopods and decapods
  • A5 There are different degrees of consciousness, so less conscious animals matter less: Attributing less mind to what we eat reduces the discomfort
  • A6 Humans suffer more than animals: Suffering more in some cases doesn't license ignoring those who suffer less; and less cognition often means more suffering and more vulnerability
  • A7 Vegans anthropomorphize animals: Denying similar mental states is also an error (anthropodenial), and the evidence contradicts it
  • A8 They're just animals: Explicit speciesism: species membership as a moral criterion, like race in racism
  • A15 Animals aren't rational: Rationality isn't what makes suffering matter, and the criterion would exclude humans who lack it
  • A16 Animals don't have language: An irrelevant criterion for pain; it excludes babies and non-verbal people
  • A17 Animals don't have souls: An unfalsifiable premise used as a public criterion
  • A19 Human babies have the potential to be rational: Potential doesn't confer the rights of the actual, and it doesn't cover humans without potential
  • A20 Animals aren't persons: A persuasive definition: pick the concept that excludes them, then conclude they are excluded
  • A27 Human life can't be compared with an animal's life: Animals seen as food are attributed less mind, which widens the perceived distance
  • B2 Killing without pain isn't harm: Death takes away good future experiences; the same holds for humans with no sense of time
  • B13 A lobster boiled alive feels nothing and tastes fresher: Lobster, being food, seems to have less mind
  • B17 Animals don't mind, they even like it and, in a way, they consent: We attribute less mind to the animals we eat
  • B19 Eating animals isn't cruelty; it's for nourishment: The eater's intention doesn't change what the animal suffers, and nourishment can be had without animals, so that suffering is unnecessary
  • E5 Veganism is opinion, not science: Doubting the science about animals eases the discomfort
  • E11 I love animals, but...: Being reminded of suffering leads to attributing less mind
  • E12 I don't want to see those videos: Perceiving less animal mind eases the discomfort
  • F7 Oysters and mussels don't feel: Oysters and mussels, being food, seem to have less mind
  • G7 Only fish provides omega-3: Fish seem to have less mind than other animals
  • H17 Sustainable fishing and aquaculture solve it: Fish seem to have less mind than other animals
  • H18 Eating insects is the sustainable protein of the future: Small, strange animals seem to have no mind
  • I11 Silk and down are natural and don't harm animals: Insects and birds seem not to feel
  • I12 Bees make extra honey: Insects seem to have no mind
  • I26 In sport fishing the fish is released: Fish seem not to feel
  • I27 I fish what I eat; it's more honest than buying at the store: Fish seem not to feel
  • J18 Billions of people depend on fish for protein: Fish seem to have less mind than other animals
  • L31 Defending animals is an emotional appeal, not an argument: Attributing less mind to the animals one eats makes the concern seem exaggerated

In helping wild animals

  • N20 Sentientists give the same weight to insects, invertebrates and mammals: Confuses equal consideration with equal weight: similar interests weigh the same, and each being's weight depends on how much it can feel, which is uncertain but not zero

In the long term and with new minds

  • N21 Machines will never feel; robots and AIs need no rights: If an artificial mind ever feels, its substrate matters no more than species does; and uncertainty calls for precaution before creating minds at scale

When animal advocates make the same mistake

The Stop saying this catalogue does not yet record an example of this error among animal advocates. That does not mean it does not happen: the same care applies to both sides.
