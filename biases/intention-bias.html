Intention bias

Reacting more strongly to harm when someone caused it on purpose than to equal harm from causes with no one to blame.

What it is

Reacting more strongly to a harm when someone caused it on purpose than to an equal harm that happened without intent, such as from natural causes.

An analogy: Being outraged at someone who smashed a vase in anger, but indifferent to a vase broken by the wind, though the loss is the same.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 3 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In helping wild animals

  • N2 Suffering and death are part of nature: Harm only seems serious when someone meant to cause it
  • N6 We didn't cause it, so we have no duty to help: Harm only seems serious when someone meant to cause it
  • N14 Helping wild animals isn't veganism; it's a different cause: Harm only seems serious when there's a culprit

When animal advocates make the same mistake

The Stop saying this catalogue does not yet record an example of this error among animal advocates. That does not mean it does not happen: the same care applies to both sides.
