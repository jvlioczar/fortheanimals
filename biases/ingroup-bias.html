Ingroup bias

Valuing "our own" and devaluing outsiders

What it is

Favoring those who belong to our group and devaluing those outside it, whether the group is a family, a team, a country or a species.

An analogy: Finding the same move brilliant when your team does it and dirty when the opponent does.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 43 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • A3 Where do you draw the line? Insects? Bacteria?: Continuum fallacy: a fuzzy boundary doesn't erase clear cases
  • A8 They're just animals: Explicit speciesism: species membership as a moral criterion, like race in racism
  • A9 Humans are superior, so we can use the inferior: Superior abilities give no right to dominate
  • A11 Species is biology, race is a social construct: A biological criterion can be as unjust as a social one: what makes discrimination unjust is giving someone's interests biased weight
  • A12 It's natural to put our own first: Partiality lets us prefer our own; it does not let us exploit others
  • A13 I respect animals, but humans come first: A special status no one explains and that wouldn't pass the veil of ignorance: the weight should depend on the size of the harm, not on species
  • A14 Humans have intrinsic value; animals have instrumental value: Circular: it assumes the conclusion
  • A18 It's normal for humans to be rational: Treats individuals by what others have, not by what they have
  • A19 Human babies have the potential to be rational: Potential doesn't confer the rights of the actual, and it doesn't cover humans without potential
  • A20 Animals aren't persons: A persuasive definition: pick the concept that excludes them, then conclude they are excluded
  • A21 No duties, no rights: Confuses moral agents with moral patients; it would exclude children
  • A22 Rights are a human invention, only for humans: Genetic fallacy: where a norm came from doesn't set how far it reaches
  • A26 Equating animals with disabled humans will degrade humans: Confuses comparing the criterion with comparing people's worth: sentience protects every human who feels and extends that protection to animals, lowering no one
  • A27 Human life can't be compared with an animal's life: Whoever says they can't be compared has already compared by calling human life worth more; comparing isn't equating, and the ban only blocks examining what we do to animals
  • A30 The sentience criterion is anthropocentric: Makes it seem natural for moral consideration to start from one's own species
  • B2 Killing without pain isn't harm: Death takes away good future experiences; the same holds for humans with no sense of time
  • B15 You don't live on a farm, so you don't know the truth: What is criticised is in the industry's own manuals and regulations; and attacking the speaker doesn't show that what is said is wrong
  • D3 Barbecues bring the family together: The bond is the gathering, not the animal
  • D5 I don't want to dishonor my parents and my ancestors: Confuses honoring people with repeating what they did: every generation revises earlier practices, and judging a practice is not condemning those who followed it
  • D6 It's cultural; criticizing it is ethnocentric: Selective cultural relativism: not applied to practices the person condemns
  • I20 Rodeos, bullfights and circuses are culture: Appeal to tradition (D1) applied to suffering imposed for entertainment
  • J13 Agriculture is the backbone of the economy: Economic importance is not a moral justification; it ignores subsidies and external costs
  • J17 Fishers and ranchers would lose their identity: Identity doesn't justify harm to others; it calls for a just transition
  • J21 People are dying in a war and you're worried about animals: The animals are victims of the same war; the cost of helping them is far smaller than the sentence assumes, and a cost existing decides nothing, since that holds for every use of resources
  • J22 Veganism is a political ideology or a globalist agenda: Guilt by association and conspiracy theory; it doesn't assess the argument
  • L2 Vegans are all hippies: A stereotype and an attack on the person: animals' suffering doesn't depend on the lifestyle of whoever points it out
  • L6 Meat is for real men: A gender stereotype; masculinity is neither a moral nor a nutritional criterion
  • L7 Veganism is a cult, a religion: A label used to avoid evaluating; veganism rests on public arguments
  • L16 Vegans are speciesists too: Tu quoque: having a bias doesn't license harming anyone, it calls for correction; and speciesism is the unjustified disregard of interests, not any preference
  • L17 You don't feel empathy for every animal in the world either: The question isn't empathy: it is limited and biased, and where it fails we use reason and principles, as we do with distant humans
  • L20 Vegans defend animals, but they support abortion: Tu quoque and generalization: veganism has no position on abortion, the two cases have different structures, and someone's inconsistency wouldn't justify exploiting animals
  • L26 Vegans support pseudoscience: Some vegans spread pseudoscience, but the case for animals rests on sentience and the nutritional consensus; judging an idea by its worst advocates would bring down any cause
  • L37 I hate vegans: Vegans are seen as an outgroup that departs from the majority’s norm
  • M9 I was vegan and started getting too radical, too rigid: The rigidity comes from how veganism is lived, not from the principle; the way can change without giving up the reason concerning animals

In helping wild animals

  • N3 A balanced nature is good for animals: Balance measures numbers, not wellbeing; and environmental programmes kill thousands of animals for goals we would never accept for humans
  • N4 Nature is fair, every animal has weapons to escape: Most victims are defenceless young, predators pick the weakest, and a species surviving doesn't show that each individual can escape
  • N5 Helping the weak ruins natural selection: Selection favours whoever leaves more offspring, not whoever lives well; species don't feel, and for humans this idea is called eugenics
  • N6 We didn't cause it, so we have no duty to help: Omission bias: no one would accept this reasoning about a drowning child or flood victims
  • N7 Domestic animals are our responsibility, wild ones are not: A special relationship can give priority, but it doesn't erase the duty to others; we help distant humans with no relationship to us
  • N11 Saving a wild animal only prolongs its agony: What makes animals suffer usually kills them, so treating the cause ends the suffering; and some help only prevents births
  • N13 We'd have to drop everything to help wild animals: Professionals in the field do the research and helping; the public is asked for taxes, donations and support, as with public health
  • N15 Sentientists are against preserving species and biodiversity: Species and biodiversity don't feel: the sentientist supports preservation when it is good for individuals and questions it only when it harms them

In the long term and with new minds

  • N21 Machines will never feel; robots and AIs need no rights: If an artificial mind ever feels, its substrate matters no more than species does; and uncertainty calls for precaution before creating minds at scale

When animal advocates make the same mistake

To be honest: animal advocates fall into it too. These items from Stop saying this show how, and what to say instead.

  • V7 Non-vegans are our enemies, and hating them is normal: Almost every vegan was once a non-vegan. People who eat animals today are, in general, where we used to be: repeating a habit learned in childhood, without ever having stopped to examine it.
