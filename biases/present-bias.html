Present bias

Giving too much weight to now and too little to future consequences.

What it is

Giving too much weight to what happens now and too little to future consequences, even when they are larger.

An analogy: Putting off the dentist to avoid today's discomfort and ending up with a bigger problem later.

How it shows up against the case for animals

In the catalogue, this bias supports 7 arguments. Each links to the full answer.

In the exploitation and use of animals

  • E15 I know it's wrong, but I won't change: Immediate pleasure weighs more than a future commitment
  • J4 It's hard; I don't have time: Immediate effort weighs more than future benefit
  • J6 Meat tastes good: Taste as a justification for killing: no other context accepts this
  • K11 I'll wait for cultivated meat: The cost of changing today weighs more than the future benefit

In the long term and with new minds

  • N22 Those not yet born don't matter: Whoever is born will feel what we leave them: that's why burying toxic waste is wrong, even if the victims don't exist yet
  • N24 There's no way to plan for the long term, since we can't predict a distant future: It works against plans that depend on getting one scenario right, not against actions that pay off in almost all of them; and uncertainty cuts both ways
  • N26 That's science fiction; we have real problems now: An uncertain but immense risk can deserve attention, and much of what reduces future suffering, like widening the moral circle, also helps now

When animal advocates make the same mistake

The Stop saying this catalogue does not yet record an example of this error among animal advocates. That does not mean it does not happen: the same care applies to both sides.
