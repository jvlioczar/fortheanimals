The state of the art in animal ethics

Since 1975, animal ethics has been tested on ever harder cases, by authors from schools that disagree with each other, and has reached the same rejection of speciesism.

Different currents, same conclusion

When theories built on incompatible premises converge on the same practical conclusion, that convergence works as a corroboration no single theory offers. Many vegans still don't know these authors.

In Brazil, Luciano Carlos Cunha is the most prolific author in the field, with fourteen books, counting standalone titles and a twelve-volume collection.

What science has declared

  • Cambridge Declaration (2012): humans are not unique in possessing the neurological substrates that generate consciousness.
  • New York Declaration (2024): strong evidence for mammals and birds, and a realistic possibility of conscious experience in all vertebrates and many invertebrates, including cephalopods, decapods and insects. It also states that certainty should not be a precondition for considering welfare risks.
  • Montreal Declaration (2022): philosophers from different currents declared animal exploitation morally unacceptable.

The price of following the argument

When someone takes an argument as far as it logically goes, the most common reaction tends to be hostility, not counter-argument. Difficult proposals deserve to be refuted, not dismissed as eccentric. Agreeing with the authors above on every point is another discussion.
